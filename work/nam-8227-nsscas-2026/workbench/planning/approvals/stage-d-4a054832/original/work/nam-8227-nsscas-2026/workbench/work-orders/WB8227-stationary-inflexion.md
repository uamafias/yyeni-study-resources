## Canonical mathematics and valid alternatives

Two-extrema: $f'=3a[(x-h)^2-r^2]$, so show $3a(x-h-r)(x-h+r)=0$ (or a complete quadratic-formula substitution) before concluding roots $h\pm r$. Substituting in f gives $(h-r,c+2ar^3)$ and $(h+r,c-2ar^3)$. Either first-derivative signs or $f''=6a(x-h)$ classify both; for a>0 left maximum/right minimum, reversed for a<0.

Flat: $f'=na(x-h)^{n-1}$. Starting from the supplied expanded polynomial, show its repeated-factor equation equals0 (binomial recognition or the factor-theorem route above) and solve x=h; substitute in original f to get y=c. A **first-derivative sign test alone earns full classification credit**: for n3, same sign on both sides with f'(h)=0 gives stationary inflexion; for n4, negative→positive gives minimum and positive→negative maximum. Values at stated points either side, or a valid even-/odd-power sign argument, are sufficient. No extra f'' concavity test is required.

A second-derivative-neighbourhood route is also valid: for n3, f'' changes sign with f'(h)=0; for n4, local positive/negative second derivative away from h with stationarity establishes min/max although f''(h)=0. A correct local-increment proof or higher-derivative proof can fill the same slots. No mark for the unsupported rule f''(h)=0 therefore inflexion. A naked classification may coincide with the truth, but supplies no required evidence.

## Discrimination, samples and limitations

`calculus-design-check.py` enumerates both revised domains and tests the named errors. Solving f=0 instead of f'=0 never gives the entire correct two-stationary-point pair, although some instances share one zero; those are flagged non-diagnostic for that partial coincidence and cannot earn the absent calculus/solve method. In the flat branch c=0 is removed so using f' for y never gives the correct full point. h=0 two-extrema cases are an explicitly easy tier/non-diagnostic for dropping a horizontal shift, not hidden extra difficulty. Taking a sign test at arbitrary wrong coordinates is not an accepted proof.

The unsupported shortcut f''(h)=0⇒inflexion returns the right **name** on the128 cubic cases but earns0/3 classification without a valid test; these cases are explicitly non-diagnostic for that answer-only shortcut. The128 quartics supply contrary extrema. Likewise an 'always minimum' guess happens to match64 cases but earns0 without work. This distinction between final-answer collision and method-evidence discrimination is recorded; the pilot is not advertised as diagnosing every error from the final word alone.

Example flat normal $f=(x-1)^3-2$ (displayed expanded): showing f'=3(x-1)², derivative=0, point(1,-2), then positive f' on both sides and a justified inflexion earns **6/6 using first derivatives alone**. A complete correct location method followed only by f''=0⇒inflexion earns3/6; a bare point and that unsupported claim earn0/6. For changed quartic $(x+1)^4+2$, f' negative left/positive right with point(-1,2) gives full minimum credit. For boundary $-2(x-2)^4-4$, positive→negative gives maximum, not inflexion despite f''(2)=0. Normal/changed/boundary complete solutions and rejected cases are in the exemplar document.
