## two-extrema: normal (WB8227-stationary-inflexion:two-extrema:normal)

Question. For $f(x)=x^{3} - 3 x + 2$, find the stationary-point coordinates. Classify both points and justify each classification. Proposed tariff 6.

1. **Action:** differentiate and set $f\prime=0$. **Reason:** stationary tangents have zero gradient. **Result:** $f\prime(x)=3 x^{2} - 3$.
2. **Action:** solve this quadratic and substitute in f. **Reason:** derivative roots give x only; coordinates require the original function. **Result:** $x=-1$ or $1$, giving $(-1,4)$ and $(1,0)$.
3. **Action:** use $f\prime\prime(x)=6 x$. **Reason:** a negative second derivative gives a local maximum and a positive one a local minimum at these stationary points. **Result:** values $-6$ and $6$, so the points are respectively maximum and minimum. A correct first-derivative sign chart is an equivalent route.

## stationary-inflexion: normal (WB8227-stationary-inflexion:stationary-inflexion:normal)

Question. For $f(x)=x^{3} - 3 x^{2} + 3 x - 3$, find the stationary-point coordinates. Explain whether the value of the second derivative at the stationary point settles its type, then classify it with valid local evidence. Proposed tariff 6.

1. **Action:** differentiate and set $f\prime=0$. **Reason:** stationary tangents have zero gradient. **Result:** $f\prime(x)=3 x^{2} - 6 x + 3$.
2. **Action:** solve $f\prime=0$ and evaluate f. **Reason:** this gives the stationary point rather than the derivative point. **Result:** $x=1$, $f(1)=-2$, hence $(1,-2)$.
3. **Action:** assess $f\prime\prime(x)=6 \left(x - 1\right)$. **Reason:** $f\prime\prime(1)=0$ alone does not classify the point. **Result:** its sign is negative to the left of 1 and positive to the right, while $f\prime$ keeps the same sign on both sides. Concavity changes and the horizontal tangent does not turn: stationary inflexion.

## Boundaries and exclusions

The extrema boundary uses a=-2,h=-2,r=3,c=4, giving roots at the widest admitted separation; the inflexion boundary uses a=-2,h=2,c=-4. Changing a's sign reverses the classifications/concavity, not the assessed method. Reject a=0; reject r=0 in two-extrema and route it only to the independently reviewed inflexion branch; reject a quartic x^4 as a cubic-branch instance. In particular f''=0 alone cannot certify inflexion (x^4 supplies a familiar counterexample), but this recipe does not claim to assess every possible degenerate stationary curve.
