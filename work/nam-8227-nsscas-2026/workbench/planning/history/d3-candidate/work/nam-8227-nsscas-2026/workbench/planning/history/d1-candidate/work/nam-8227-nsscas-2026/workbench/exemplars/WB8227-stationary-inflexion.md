## Boundaries, diagnostic limits and valid alternatives

The flat question never cues a second-derivative test or announces that its value is zero. The admitted branch mixes cubic inflexions with positive and negative quartic extrema. All three exemplars have f''(h)=0, yet their outcomes differ; that value alone earns no classification credit. The original polynomial is displayed expanded, so its stationary factorisation is learner work.

Reject a=0, r=0 in two-extrema, h=0 or c=0 in the chosen flat domain, and any extra stationary root or function family outside the work order. h=0 remains an admitted easy tier in two-extrema. c=0 is excluded only in the flat branch so the named wrong substitution f'(h) for f(h) never accidentally gives the correct single stationary y-coordinate. Both exact coordinates are still required for the final two-extrema coordinate award.

The128 cubic instances are explicitly non-diagnostic for the unsupported final-name shortcut 'f''=0 so inflexion': its word can be right, but without the valid test it earns0 classification marks. The quartic cases refute that shortcut; they do not silently change a claimed always-inflexion branch. A correct two-sided first-derivative sign argument, an appropriate second-derivative neighbourhood proof, local-increment proof or higher-derivative argument fills the same criterion slots. No double credit or compulsory concavity add-on.
