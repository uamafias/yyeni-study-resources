| OD-6 | **How should a learner be marked after copying a number wrongly from the question (for example, reading V = 12 as 21) and then working correctly?** <br>• **D2 proposal** (`D2-MISREAD-ALLOCATION`, subject-rules D1-MR03): every value-bearing A or B mark tied to the correct givens fails, and M, DM and unaffected structural B marks survive. That gives vector 3/7 and motion 2/5 in the D2 samples. <br>• **NSSCAS 2025 report** (PDF 345, printed 343, P1 general item 4, `CONVENTION:108`): "Learners who misread numbers do not score maximum marks even if their method is correct." It does not say how many marks are lost. <br>• **Held Cambridge 9709/12 March 2016 scheme** (PDF 3, "Penalties"): "A penalty of MR−1 is deducted from A or B marks when the data of a question or part question are genuinely misread and the object and difficulty of the question remain unaltered. In this case all A and B marks then become 'follow through' marks. MR is not applied when the candidate misreads his own figures – this is regarded as an error in accuracy." This gives vector 6/7 and motion 4/5. <br>Both readings satisfy the NSSCAS statement. | **Cambridge convention: lose one mark, with follow-through.** <br>• A genuine misread of the question's data, where the object and difficulty of the question are unchanged, costs one mark, deducted from the A or B marks. All A and B marks then follow through on the misread value. M and DM marks are judged as usual. <br>• Misreading the learner's own figures is an accuracy error, not a misread. <br>• A misread that changes the object or difficulty of the question gets no misread treatment. For example, one that makes it easier or leaves no solution is marked as normal against the canonical question. <br>• Practice feedback names the misread. <br>• This replaces the D2 proposal everywhere. The rule is an owner decision under OD-6, citing both pages above. |
