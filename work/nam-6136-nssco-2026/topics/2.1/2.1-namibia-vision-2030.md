## Five connected areas

### Five connected areas

Vision 2030 provides the long-term development context for this lesson. Study the five areas named here without assuming that a stated ambition has already been achieved. Education, science and technology can build knowledge and usable skills. Health and development connect people's well-being with their ability to learn, work and participate. Sustainable agriculture maintains farming while protecting soil, water and future production. Peace and social justice concern cooperation, fair treatment and access to opportunities. Gender equality means equal rights, opportunities and treatment regardless of gender; formal equality does not by itself establish equal lived outcomes.

### Make the connection explicit

In an invented community, a training centre has equipment but some learners cannot attend because sessions clash with caring duties. Offering workable session times and fair access could connect education and gender equality: more people could gain usable skills. This proposal addresses the identified barrier rather than assuming that equal admission rules have removed it. In a separate fictional comparison, Centre A has 40% course completion and 35% participation among eligible women; Centre B has 75% completion and 70% participation. B combines higher completion with wider participation. That relationship helps identify an access issue to investigate; the figures do not prove which policy caused it.

### Use the areas together

These areas reinforce one another when practical conditions allow it. Healthy learners can benefit from accessible education; usable skills and sustainable farming can support livelihoods. Topic 1.2 explains sustainability, Topic 7.2 health, Topic 7.3 education and Topic 6.2 gender. Here, describe each area's contribution and show a connection using the facts of a case. A programme's title is not evidence that its intended outcome occurred; look for participation, service and living-condition changes.

### Meaning and time horizon of Vision 2030

Namibia Vision 2030 is a national development framework setting a direction from the country’s position in 2004 towards improved living conditions by 2030. It connects long-term social and economic goals with development strategies. It describes an intended future; the title and end date are not evidence that the goals have been achieved.
