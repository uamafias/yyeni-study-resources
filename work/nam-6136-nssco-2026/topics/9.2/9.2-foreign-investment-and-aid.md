## Investment and aid

### Investment and aid

Foreign investment places resources in an economy from abroad with an investment interest or return; aid provides assistance for development or humanitarian needs. Aid can include grants, expertise, goods or concessional finance. Purpose and terms differ; foreign money is not automatically a gift.

### Foreign investment

Foreign investment is investment by an investor based outside the recipient economy. Direct investment involves lasting interest and influence in an enterprise; other holdings may be financial investments without the same productive control.

### Aid

Aid is assistance intended to support development or humanitarian needs, through resources such as grants, expertise, goods or suitable finance. A grant need not be repaid, while a loan carries repayment terms. Assess delivery, purpose and conditions separately.

### Assess the arrangement

A fictional school has trained staff but needs technical design and funding for a safe sanitation system. Suitable assistance should match both gaps and include a credible operating and maintenance arrangement. A grant avoids repayment, but its size alone does not establish useful delivery. Recipient priorities and the terms of support affect whether the result can last.

### Benefits and limitations of outside resources

Aid can finance essential services when local public resources are limited, supply technical knowledge and provide relief after a shock. A grant avoids repayment, but donor conditions or poorly chosen projects can reduce local control or usefulness. Foreign investment can provide equipment, jobs, training and market connections. Profits may leave the economy, local suppliers may be bypassed, and weak safeguards can leave pollution or poor work conditions. Loans can spread payment for useful capacity over time but commit later resources to debt service. Judge feasibility, local influence, maintained benefits and opportunity cost for each arrangement; a larger inflow is not proof of greater welfare.

## Forms and conditions

### Forms and conditions

Investment, grants, technical assistance, loans and credits have different ownership, repayment and delivery arrangements. A loan is a borrowing arrangement; debt is the amount outstanding. Emergency or development purpose differs from bilateral delivery between countries or multilateral delivery through institutions.

### Direct investment and companies

Foreign direct investment can establish productive facilities and connect technology or markets through firms, including multinationals. Local skills, suppliers and retained benefits matter; finance and jobs can coexist with profits leaving or environmental costs.

### Loans and credits

Foreign loans provide resources with repayment obligations, often with interest and agreed timing. They may finance useful capacity but create debt-service needs. Borrowing for an unusable asset adds obligations without the expected earning or service benefit.

### Assess the arrangement

An invented council compares a small grant for a limited service with an affordable loan that reaches more residents but commits later revenue. The grant protects future funds; the loan widens current reach. Neither financing label settles the judgement. Compare need, maintained operation, repayment capacity and the services postponed under each feasible option.

## Institutions have distinct roles

### Institutions have distinct roles

The syllabus groups several international development bodies, but their mandates and institutional forms differ. The World Bank and IMF are international financial institutions, UNDP a development programme and UNICEF a children’s fund; FAO and UNESCO are specialised agencies. Match the actual need and role rather than calling them interchangeable donors.

### World Bank

The World Bank supports development through finance, knowledge and technical assistance, including long-term projects and institutional capacity. A proposed infrastructure or service project needs feasible design and implementation; institutional support does not prove the project’s outcomes.

### IMF

The IMF supports monetary cooperation and economic stability through monitoring, advice, capacity development and lending for balance-of-payments difficulties. Its stabilisation role differs from simply building schools or giving unconditional grants; lending conditions and effects require case evidence.

### UNDP

UNDP works on development including poverty reduction, governance, resilience and environmental sustainability through partnerships and technical support. It can help strengthen capabilities and coordination; a broad mandate does not mean it performs every service itself.

### UNICEF

UNICEF focuses on children’s rights and well-being, including health, nutrition, education, water and protection. Child-focused assistance can link several needs, while implementation and reach still require evidence.

### FAO

FAO works on food security, agriculture and related knowledge and cooperation to address hunger. Agricultural advice and evidence can support suitable production, but need local access, capabilities and conditions to become useful.

### UNESCO

UNESCO promotes international cooperation in education, science, culture and information. Its education and heritage work differs from general lending or food-distribution functions; select the role relevant to the development question.

### Assess the arrangement

A fictional programme seeking child-focused nutrition support needs a different institutional role from one seeking finance for an external-payment shortfall. Match the agency’s mandate and type of assistance to the actual problem before evaluating terms and delivery. A broad international role does not mean an organisation performs every service or supplies unconditional funds.
