SECTION A
1 Study the extract, about sanitation in Omuthiya in the Oshikito region.
In Omuthiya in the Oshikoto region, the directorate of rural water supply commenced
a countrywide survey on rural water and sanitation in an effort to ascertain the
accessibility and availability of sanitation and water services.
The survey does not include urban areas. The survey covered 37 411 households
in Oshikoto. The researcher used interviews and information from the internet about
sanitation and clean water. The survey would find out the current number and type of
toilets as well as access to and quality of water supplies in the region. Experts, such
as statisticians from the Namibia StatisticsAgency (NSA) were involved in capturing,
storing and analysing the data.
The aim of the survey is to determine how far the government has gone with its
mandate of providing water and sanitation in the rural areas.
(New Era, 14 June 2017)
(a)  (i) Where was the survey carried out according to the extract? [1]
(ii) What was the aim of the survey? [1]
(iii) Explain why clean water and improved sanitation help to prevent the
spread of diarrhoea. [2]
(b)  (i) Using your own words; what is meant by statistic? [1]
(ii) According to the extract, state why statisticians were important in the survey. [1]
(c)  (i) Differentiate between primary and secondary sources. [2]
(ii) The researcher used interviews to collect information on sanitation and
water services.
Describe four difficulties which researchers might have experienced in
using interviews. [4]
(iii) Suggest four things which researchers should have done while preparing
and carrying out their interviews. [4]
(iv) Apart from the internet, suggest four other ways in which secondary data
about sanitation and water services could be collected. [4]
(d) What should a researcher do with the information collected about sanitation
and water services? [5]
