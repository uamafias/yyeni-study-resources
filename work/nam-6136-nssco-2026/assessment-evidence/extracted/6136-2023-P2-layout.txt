(c) Study Fig. 2, an extract about aquaculture, fish resources and rural                                                            Use
    livelihoods: a village Computable General Equilibrium (CGE) analysis from
    Namibia’s Zambezi Region.

     Aquaculture is breeding, rearing and harvesting of fish and shellfish and is widely
     recognised as a way to reduce malnutrition and poverty. So far research has mainly
     focused on Asia and only a few studies are available from sub-Saharan Africa. A
     rural case study was carried out in Namibia where malnutrition, poverty and fish
     resource overexploitation are currently problems. Aquaculture is one of the activities
     initiated by the government to enhance food security, alleviate poverty and improve
     the livelihood of the rural people.
     About 50 percent of the population makes a living from inland fish resources.  The
     consumption of freshwater fish in the Zambezi region is higher than that of other
     types of food like beef, game and poultry. Tilapia is the most abundantly available
     type of fish.  Furthermore, aquaculture can prevent malnutrition through increased
     fish consumption.
