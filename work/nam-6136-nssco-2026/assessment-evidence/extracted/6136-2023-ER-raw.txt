POSSITIVE SUGGESTIONS TO TEACHERS
• Teachers should encourage learners to write neatly and clearly (using the spaces provided they can do this by
limiting their answers to fit in the space allocated)
• Teachers should emphasise that learners must consider the number of marks given to each question as most
learners only write less than the marks allocated.
• Teachers should encourage their learners to prepare for the examination well in advance for them to be able to
recall common core facts asked in the examination.
• Learners should be trained to read questions and study sources with understanding before they attempt to answer
the questions. This is because it is observed that most learners read questions half way.
• Learners should be taught that questions that are asking to differentiate should be in table format.
• Terminologies and command words listed in the syllabus such as Evaluate, Describe Show your work, etc., should
be well explained to learners.
• Teachers are encouraged to study the examiner’s report together with their learners in order to prevent repetition
of common errors year by year.

General comments
The overall performance was moderate, however the part time candidates performed better than the full time
candidates. Some centres ignored examiner’s reports as common mistakes were picked up and they are more or less
the same as in the previous year. Regional Offices should make sure that examiners reports are studied thoroughly
and draft recommendations for implementation purposes from the regions to the schools. Regional Offices should give
Continuous, Professional Development (CPD) programmes to teachers who teach Development Studies. If possible
networking with DNEA, NIED and other regions with Senior Education Officers as well as relevant stakeholders to
ensure teachers are capacitated and assisted on a regular basis. It is unacceptable to see centres repeating the same
mistakes year in year out.
The question paper covered the whole syllabus thoroughly with clear instructions and in simple English.
Individual questions were well structured with clear and concise instructions.
Learners showed capabilities of getting answers from the source provided.
Overall the allocation of marks was fair.
The application of knowledge is lacking in most candidates, e.g. they know the methods of sampling but cannot explain
how these methods are used and why.
There are still more centres where candidates scored less or no marks in section B and it is evident that some teachers
did not guide the learners properly in carrying out the research. Teachers must refrain from giving one topic to the whole
centre.
Candidate scripts were generally neat, English language grammar hampers them in expressing themselves fully.

Systematic sampling
- Handing questionnaires to every tenth person until 50 people are selected out of 500 people.
The reason why it was used:
- more representatives;
- it covers large area
- less time consuming
Random sampling
- Giving residents numbers, collect those numbers and put them
in a bowl. Draw 50 numbers out of 500 numbers. The 50 selected people will be given questionnaire to
complete or to fill in.
Reasons why it was suitable:
- it reduces bias
- everyone has equal chance to be selected
- more representatives
- it is fast, easy and simple to use.
Stratified sampling
- The researcher will divide the residents according to their age groups
and select from each sub group using systematic or random selection until 50 people are selected to be
given questionnaires to complete.
Why it is suitable:
- represents subgroup of entire population
- fair coverage with random selection
- more representatives
- different sampling method can be applied. [Any method] [4]

Positive suggestions to teachers
• Teachers should study the examiner’s report together with their learners to help them understand what is required
from them.
• Teachers must put more emphasis on command words to ensure that learners are familiar with them as listed at
the back of the syllabus.
• Teachers must emphasise that learners consider the number of marks given for each question.
• Teach the learners to read instructions and questions carefully before they attempt them.
• Candidates should be motivated to carry out their own research and avoid all learners doing one topic.
• Brainstorm topics with the learners to ensure broad range of developmental topics.
• Refrain from using Geography related topics.
• Make a clear distinction between a research question and a hypothesis.
• Guide the learners on how to formulate more than one research question and more than one hypothesis.
• Guide the learners on how to link the research questions or hypothesis to the aims. Focus on teaching them on how
to formulate aims from the research question or hypothesis.
• Train the candidates on how to derive aims from the source (extract given) and put them in their own words without
altering the meaning.
• Teach all data presentation methods except line graph and explain which graph weigh more marks and why.
• Train the learners on how to extract information from the source given.
• Give a clear guide on sampling methods e.g. define, the application (how) and the why (advantages and
disadvantages) strictly random, systematic and stratified avoid other sampling methods at all cost.
• Emphasise the way a conclusion is structured, considering the general trend and use words such as majority, most,
mainly, least.
• Guide the learners to differentiate between conclusions and findings.
• Guide them on how to design a questionnaire following the designing guidelines.
• Teach them how to apply when they are asked to do so.
• Carry out a practical research in their communities, the role of the teacher during research investigation is to
facilitate and to mentor them. Stop giving one topic to the whole centre, it is an offence
