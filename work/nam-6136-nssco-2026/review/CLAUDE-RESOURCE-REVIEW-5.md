## Findings

**R5-01 — Low: generic guides on four-mark evaluation cards (confidence 0.9).**
- **Affects:** 31 four-mark EVAL cards across 11 topics (counted by search). Examples: 4.1-010/012/013/019, 4.2-016/018/020, 5.1-024 to 030, 7.1-018/021/030, 7.2-008/011, 7.3-018/020/022, 9.3-001/003/004/005, 10.1-032/055.
- **Problem:** every one uses the same template: "compare genuine benefits and costs… develop each option… give a conditional conclusion." None names the evidence that earns marks or how the four marks divide.
- **Consequence:** at the highest flashcard tariff, a learner marking their own answer can't tell a weighed answer from a one-sided one.
- **Repair:** for each card, name the decisive trade-off from the prompt (for 4.1-010: 12 stable local jobs versus 40 jobs that depend on one buyer). Allocate about one mark per developed option, one for weighing them, and one for the conditional conclusion.

**R5-02 — Low: generic two-mark guides and copied package guides (confidence 0.85).**
- **Two-mark FEATURE cards:** 51 of them have only the guide "credit two distinct accurate descriptions matching the prompt". Examples: 5.1-003 to 022 and 052 to 054, 9.1-011 to 018 and 023/024, 6.2-006 to 009 and 024/025, 10.1-005 to 008 and 018, 3.1-061 and 063 to 065.
- **Research packages 10.1-P01 to P04:** every Section A part's guide repeats the model answer word for word, adds a generic "credit the essential method" line, and gives no point or band allocation. That matters most for Design [5] and Draw [4] parts, because the corpus shows graphs and charts are marked by levels.
- **Repair:**
  - FEATURE cards: one mark per feature, with the acceptable features named.
  - Design parts: one mark per usable element.
  - Draw parts: band descriptors (title, axes, scale, bars, labels).

**R5-03 — Low: pyramid shape with broad unequal age bands (confidence 0.75).**
- **Affects:** BLK-6136-7.1.06-05 and BLK-6136-7.1.06-01.
- **Problem:** the bands span 15, 50 and an open-ended 65-plus years. Drawn as specified, the 15–64 bar is the widest, so the figure looks like a diamond, not a broad-based pyramid. Block 06-01 teaches learners to read a "broad base", and the notes don't explain the mismatch.
- **Repair:** add one sentence: with broad unequal bands the middle bar is widest because it covers about 50 years, so compare each band across the two populations rather than reading the overall shape.

**R5-04 — Low: two valid names for one method (confidence 0.7).**
- **Affects:** 10.1-P02 part A1(1), compared with card 10.1-022.
- **Problem:** the prompt describes the same neutral questions asked aloud with spoken answers recorded. That fits a structured interview, but card 022 calls the same procedure a questionnaire. The guide accepts only "Interview".
- **Repair:** also accept "structured interview or interviewer-administered questionnaire".

**R5-05 — Low: cards reuse the notes' worked figures (confidence 0.6).**
- **Affects:** 3.1-046 and 3.1-047, against BLK-6136-3.1.10-05.
- **Problem:** card 046's 800/2,000 = 40% and card 047's 40/25/35 versus 20/30/50 are the notes' own worked figures. Cutting cards from the notes is the intended model, but it is the same transfer weakness F2 removed.
- **Repair:** change the card numbers.

## Learner tests (notes alone)

- **High-tariff sampling explanation — passes at the top band.** From the 10.1 notes a learner can explain:
  - population versus sample;
  - random selection: a complete numbered list, an equal chance, a fair draw, and why picking the nearest fails;
  - stratified selection: proportional allocation (30/45 × 9 = 6), random choice within groups, and the non-response limit;
  - systematic selection: interval = population ÷ sample size, a fair start, and the risk of a repeating pattern in the list;
  - list coverage and recording refusals.

  Because the worked numbers now differ from the cards', the learner has to transfer the method rather than recall answers.
- **Three-mark explanation of why climate action matters for sustainable development — passes, 3/3, but thinly.** From BLK-6136-1.2.01-R01:
  1. drought and flooding damage crops, water and infrastructure, undermining livelihoods;
  2. adaptation reduces present vulnerability;
  3. cutting emissions limits further warming and protects future generations' needs.

  This rests on one paragraph of teaching, and no card practises it.
- **Describe three HPP pillars — passes, 3/3.** From BLK-6136-2.2.02-R01: governance means accountable decisions and services; economic advancement means productive opportunities and jobs; social progression covers food security, housing, sanitation and skills.
