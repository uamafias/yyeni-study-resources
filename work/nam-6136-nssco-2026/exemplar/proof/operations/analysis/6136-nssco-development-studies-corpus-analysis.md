## 4. What a creditable answer looks like

- **RELATIONSHIP-3** — 6136-2020-P1-1(a)(iv), report p.123: State the relationship. Support it with two appropriate numerical references. The report assigns 1 mark to the relationship and 2 to statistics. Guard: A pair of definitions does not answer a relationship question.
- **BUNDLE-IDENTIFY-1** — 6136-2020-P1-2(a)(ii), report p.124: Supply the requested set of three factors. The whole three-factor identification is one paper mark; do not infer one mark per named item. Guard: Tariff must be read, not counted from the number of requested items.
- **COMPARE-3** — 6136-2020-P1-2(e)(ii), report p.125: Pair corresponding features across both technologies. The report explicitly indicates horizontal comparison marking at a three-mark tariff. Guard: Separate unpaired lists are weaker than matching feature-by-feature contrasts.
- **NAME-EXPLAIN-6** — 6136-2020-P2-2(d)(ii), report p.132: Name three relevant participation strategies. Explain a mechanism for each. Three strategy-plus-development units form the reported six-mark response. Guard: A strategy label without its mechanism is incomplete.
- **EXPLAIN-2** — 6136-2020-P2-1(c)(iii), report p.130: Identify the relevant governance idea. Explain its developmental role. Observed two-mark explanation; do not generalise this one response to all Explain/2 tasks. Guard: The answer must address the actual feature named in the question.
- **CHART-5-BANDS** — 6136-2020-P3-2(d), report p.136: Construct the requested pie chart. Include an appropriate title/key and accurate sector plotting. Level 1: 1; level 2: 2–3; level 3: 4–5, as reported. Guard: This is level-marked, not five independent checklist ticks.
- **FINDINGS-6-BANDS** — 6136-2021-P3-3(a)(iv), report p.127: Present findings relevant to the study aims. Support them with the learner’s evidence. Develop the implications. Reported bands 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6; stronger evidence/aim links and implications distinguish the upper band. Guard: Do not invent results for a study the learner did not undertake.
- **FINDINGS-4-BANDS** — 6136-2022-P3-3(c), report p.155: Present evidence-linked findings related to the investigation. Develop implications or practical improvements. The report prints overlapping lower bands (1–2 and 2) and an upper 3–4 band. Preserve the ambiguity; do not fabricate a corrected official rubric. Guard: A bare list of observations is not a developed report.
- **GRAPH-4-BANDS** — 6136-2023-P3-1(c)(ii), report p.165: Choose a suitable graph. Label and scale it appropriately, give a title, and plot accurately. Reported bands: 1–2 for an unsuitable/weak attempt, 3 for one weakness, 4 for an accurate complete graph. Guard: The report caps use of a line graph in this categorical-data task; that is not a blanket syllabus prohibition of all line graphs.
- **CONCLUSION-3-BANDS** — 6136-2023-P3-3(c), report p.168: Draw a conclusion related to the research question. Support it using the investigation’s evidence. Develop a valid overall inference. Reported levels give 1, 2 and 3 marks, distinguished by evidence, relevance and development. Guard: Repeating findings without an inference does not establish a conclusion.
- **RECOMMEND-3** — 6136-2023-P3-3(d), report p.168: Give feasible recommendations tied to the investigated problem. Identify appropriate people or institutions to implement them. Three marks in the paper; the report explicitly identifies missing implementers as a weakness. It does not print a universal point-per-recommendation formula. Guard: Avoid generic unassigned calls for action.
- **PILOT-APPLICATION-2** — 6136-2024-P3-3(a)(iv), report p.166: Describe testing the actual questionnaire/interview instrument. Explain how the trial identified issues and informed adjustments. The report gives zero for a definition alone at this application tariff of two marks. Guard: A definition of pilot study cannot substitute for how it was carried out.
- **FULL-REPORT-6-BANDS** — 6136-2024-P3-3(a)(v), report p.166: Report valid findings with supporting evidence. Relate them to the aims and develop implications. Include appropriate recommendations and solutions. Reported bands 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6; integrated evidence and development are required for the upper band. Guard: No fixed six-bullet rule is inferred.
- **PROBLEM-SOLUTION-2** — 6136-2024-P3-3(b), report p.166: Identify a practical obstacle encountered by the researcher. Match it with a workable response. The report says solution-only responses receive zero, but also prints a two-mark cap for problems without solutions on a two-mark question. This cap is ambiguous; teach paired reasoning without claiming an invented official split. Guard: Keep researcher difficulties distinct from the community problem under study.
- **METHOD-APPLICATION-4** — 6136-2024-P3-1(b)(i), report p.163: Name the two primary methods. Describe how each was used to obtain and record information in this survey. The report permits identification marks and additional application marks at a four-mark tariff; preserve the two-method application structure. Guard: Method names or copied survey text alone do not explain the collection procedure.

These are fifteen cited exemplars, not fifteen universal rules. The 2020 P1 relationship example explicitly separates one relationship mark from two statistics marks; an Identify-three-factors item is only one mark. Conversely, the report on 2024 P3 pilot-study application gives no credit for definition alone. Exact credit-point formulas for other parts remain unverified without standalone schemes or a specific report statement. `curriculum/answer-shapes.json` preserves those distinctions.

## 6. How candidates lose marks

- **Naming or defining instead of applying.** Evidence: 2020 P3 report p.136; 2023 P3 p.164; 2024 P3 pp.163,166. Resource response: Use a fresh survey scenario and require the instrument, respondents, collection/recording procedure and any pilot adjustment.
- **Listing instead of developing an explanation.** Evidence: 2020 P1 report pp.124,127; 2024 P1 p.155; 2024 P2 p.158. Resource response: Show a point, its mechanism and consequence at the actual tariff; do not award repeated labels as development.
- **Ignoring or merely copying a source.** Evidence: 2020 P1 report p.123; 2023 P3 p.164; 2024 P1 p.151. Resource response: Require a source-supported statement and relevant figures where requested; use own words for interpretation.
- **Unpaired comparisons.** Evidence: 2020 P2 report p.129; 2021 P2 p.120; 2024 P1 p.154. Resource response: Compare the same feature across both cases in each statement; a table is helpful but not a universal requirement.
- **Weak numerical/graph work.** Evidence: 2020 P1 report p.123; 2023 P1 pp.153,155–156; 2024 P1 p.154; 2024 P3 p.164. Resource response: Practise units, subtraction versus percentage change, scale/key/title and pie-sector angles; match chart type to data.
- **Findings, conclusions, aims and recommendations not linked.** Evidence: 2021 P3 report p.127; 2022 P3 p.155; 2023 P3 pp.167–168; 2024 P3 pp.165–166. Resource response: Build an own-study chain: question → aims → collected evidence → findings → conclusion → feasible recommendation and implementer.
- **A problem without its matched response, or the wrong problem.** Evidence: 2020 P3 report p.137; 2022 P3 p.153; 2024 P3 pp.165–166. Resource response: Distinguish researcher obstacles from community issues; pair practical problems with feasible responses and keep ambiguous printed caps visible.

## 7. Misconceptions

Thirty conceptual pairs are in `curriculum/misconceptions.json`, each with a report page, paper reference, source-bullet linkage and a discrimination test. General habits such as omitting units or giving an unpaired comparison were not padded into conceptual entries. Each entry cites one occurrence; this is not an exhaustive recurrence count.

- money capital ↔ capital goods: P1 2(b)(i), 2020 report p.124. Test: Does the item finance production, or is it a produced tool used in production?
- land ownership ↔ organisation of production: P1 2(c), 2020 report p.125. Test: Is the question about rights over land, or the business arrangement that organises production?
- division of labour ↔ mass production: P1 2(d)(i), 2020 report p.125. Test: Does the description specify how tasks are divided between workers, or only the quantity produced?
- renewable resource conservation ↔ non-renewable resource conservation: P1 1(d)(ii), 2020 report p.124. Test: Can the resource replenish on a human timescale, or does the strategy reduce use of a finite stock?
- exports ↔ export-oriented industrialisation: P1 3(a)(i), 2020 report p.126. Test: Is this merely a sale abroad, or a strategy for developing production for overseas markets?
- market economy ↔ mixed economy: P1 1(c)(ii), 2021 report p.115. Test: Are productive enterprises chiefly privately owned, or do private and state enterprises coexist?
- communal ownership ↔ public ownership: P1 2(b)(i), 2021 report p.116. Test: Does control rest with a community sharing land, or with the state?
- labour/capital intensity ↔ simple/intermediate/complex technology: P1 2(d)(i), 2021 report p.116. Test: Does the comparison concern the relative use of workers and capital, or the sophistication of the technology?
- globalisation ↔ global warming: P1 4(c)(i), 2021 report p.118. Test: Is the process about international connections and exchange, or rising global temperatures?
- GNI per person ↔ total GNP: P2 1(b)(iii), 2021 report p.120. Test: Does the measure divide income by population, or report a national aggregate?
- highest level ↔ largest increase: P2 1(c)(ii), 2021 report p.121. Test: Are we selecting the largest value, or the steepest positive change between observations?
- nomadic pastoralists ↔ settled agriculturalists: P2 2(b)(i), 2021 report p.122. Test: Does the community move with livestock, or remain to cultivate fields?
- infrastructure ↔ factors of production: P2 2(a)(iv), 2021 report p.122. Test: Are we naming supporting networks and services, or the land/labour/capital inputs?
- sample ↔ sampling method: P3 1(c)(ii), 2020 report p.135. Test: Is this the selected group, or the procedure used to choose that group?
- data collection method ↔ data presentation method: P3 3(a)(iii), 2021 report p.127. Test: Does the method obtain observations, or display information already collected?
- research question ↔ hypothesis: P3 3(a), 2020 report p.136. Test: Is this a question to investigate, or a testable statement predicting a relationship?
- population density ↔ population distribution: P1 3(a)(i), 2023 report p.155. Test: Does the statement give people per unit area, or describe where people live?
- urban population ↔ urbanisation: P1 3(b)(i), 2023 report p.156. Test: Is this the number living in urban areas, or an increase in their share of the national population?
- traditional/modern education ↔ formal/non-formal education: P2 2(b)(i), 2024 report p.161. Test: Does the distinction concern a traditional versus modern system, or organisation within or outside formal schooling?
- findings ↔ conclusions: P3 3(c), 2023 report p.168. Test: Does the statement report observations, or infer an answer to the research question from those observations?
- biotechnology ↔ technology in general: P1 2(b)(iv), 2023 report p.155. Test: Does the example involve biological processes or organisms, or a different kind of technology?
- sustainable agriculture ↔ sustainable development: P2 1(b)(iii), 2022 report p.148. Test: Is the statement specific to farming practices, or a broader development principle?
- informal sector ↔ informal settlement: P1 3(a)(iv), 2022 report p.144. Test: Is the description about economic work and registration, or a residential area?
- production difficulty ↔ marketing difficulty: P1 2(b)(ii), 2024 report p.153. Test: Does the obstacle stop growing the crop, or stop reaching and selling to buyers?
- trade-balance improvement ↔ foreign-investment inflow: P1 4(b)(ii), 2024 report p.156. Test: Does the transaction directly change export/import values, or bring in investment finance?
- loan ↔ debt: P1 4(d)(i), 2024 report p.156. Test: Is this a borrowing arrangement, or the outstanding amount owed?
- project organisation ↔ project funding: P3 2(b), 2024 report p.165. Test: Does the action arrange people and tasks, or obtain money/resources to pay for the project?
- researcher’s practical difficulty ↔ community’s development problem: P3 3(b), 2024 report p.166. Test: Does the problem obstruct data collection, or is it the issue being investigated?
- aid purpose ↔ aid delivery arrangement: P2 2(c)(iii), 2023 report p.162. Test: Does the category explain what help is needed, or whether it is delivered bilaterally or multilaterally?
- absolute difference ↔ percentage change: P1 3(b)(ii), 2024 report p.154. Test: Is the difference divided by the original value and multiplied by 100, or left as an absolute difference?
