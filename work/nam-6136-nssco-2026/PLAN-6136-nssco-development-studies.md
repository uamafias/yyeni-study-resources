## 3. Answer shapes

Use curriculum/answer-shapes.json for fifteen report-backed examples, with their specific limits. Some graphs, reports and conclusions are level-marked. Guidance requirements are minimal and tariff-scaled: short interpretations need evidence and a relationship; calculations need working and units; limits or extra counterarguments are not compulsory boilerplate. An Identify-three-factors part can be worth one mark; there is no universal one-point-per-bullet rule. New practice rubrics must fit the original task and be labelled indicative internally. Exact unseen official allocations are not invented.

- RELATIONSHIP-3: Describe 3; The report assigns 1 mark to the relationship and 2 to statistics. A pair of definitions does not answer a relationship question.
- BUNDLE-IDENTIFY-1: Identify 1; The whole three-factor identification is one paper mark; do not infer one mark per named item. Tariff must be read, not counted from the number of requested items.
- COMPARE-3: Differentiate 3; The report explicitly indicates horizontal comparison marking at a three-mark tariff. Separate unpaired lists are weaker than matching feature-by-feature contrasts.
- NAME-EXPLAIN-6: Name and explain 6; Three strategy-plus-development units form the reported six-mark response. A strategy label without its mechanism is incomplete.
- EXPLAIN-2: Explain 2; Observed two-mark explanation; do not generalise this one response to all Explain/2 tasks. The answer must address the actual feature named in the question.
- CHART-5-BANDS: Draw 5; Level 1: 1; level 2: 2–3; level 3: 4–5, as reported. This is level-marked, not five independent checklist ticks.
- FINDINGS-6-BANDS: Describe 6; Reported bands 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6; stronger evidence/aim links and implications distinguish the upper band. Do not invent results for a study the learner did not undertake.
- FINDINGS-4-BANDS: Outline 4; The report prints overlapping lower bands (1–2 and 2) and an upper 3–4 band. Preserve the ambiguity; do not fabricate a corrected official rubric. A bare list of observations is not a developed report.
- GRAPH-4-BANDS: Draw 4; Reported bands: 1–2 for an unsuitable/weak attempt, 3 for one weakness, 4 for an accurate complete graph. The report caps use of a line graph in this categorical-data task; that is not a blanket syllabus prohibition of all line graphs.
- CONCLUSION-3-BANDS: Outline 3; Reported levels give 1, 2 and 3 marks, distinguished by evidence, relevance and development. Repeating findings without an inference does not establish a conclusion.
- RECOMMEND-3: Suggest 3; Three marks in the paper; the report explicitly identifies missing implementers as a weakness. It does not print a universal point-per-recommendation formula. Avoid generic unassigned calls for action.
- PILOT-APPLICATION-2: Explain 2; The report gives zero for a definition alone at this application tariff of two marks. A definition of pilot study cannot substitute for how it was carried out.
- FULL-REPORT-6-BANDS: Write 6; Reported bands 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6; integrated evidence and development are required for the upper band. No fixed six-bullet rule is inferred.
- PROBLEM-SOLUTION-2: Explain 2; The report says solution-only responses receive zero, but also prints a two-mark cap for problems without solutions on a two-mark question. This cap is ambiguous; teach paired reasoning without claiming an invented official split. Keep researcher difficulties distinct from the community problem under study.
- METHOD-APPLICATION-4: Name and describe 4; The report permits identification marks and additional application marks at a four-mark tariff; preserve the two-method application structure. Method names or copied survey text alone do not explain the collection procedure.

## 8. Rules particular to this subject

1. All ten themes receive teaching and practice, regardless of corpus frequency.
2. No copied exam stems, report phrases or marking prose reach learners. Use original scenarios and figures.
3. Explanations show a mechanism and consequence; comparisons pair the same feature; data answers use relevant figures.
4. Numerical work distinguishes absolute differences, percentage changes and percentage points; show method, working, units and interpretation.
5. Research notes connect topic → question/hypothesis → aims → instrument and pilot → sample → data → presentation → findings → conclusion → feasible recommendation and implementer → evaluation.
6. Teach suitable bar/pie charts, divided rectangles, tables, sketch maps and flow charts from the Paper 3 guidance. Ask learners to construct representations from supplied original data. Answers specify axes, scale, labels, calculated values and shape. Do not claim an unseen visual has been checked or use an invented hand-rendered plot.
7. Generate no line-graph construction exercises under the report-based practice policy. Teach random, systematic and stratified sampling, not adjacent sampling curricula. These teaching boundaries and per-theme depth ceilings are explicit in contracts; no content theme is removed.
8. Claims remain unverified/non-publishable in the structured authoring model until the prescribed review process; a deterministic PUBLISH result is reported with all warnings and not_run checks.
9. Structural examples are in exemplar/proof/work/nam-6136-nssco-2026/. Their reduced registry and budgets never replace the full plan. Each item now has a topic-specific rubric; C-16 checks keywords, not the truth or relevance of marking. Both examples have zero deterministic failures; C-35/C-41/C-43 have no opinion and are explicitly listed.
10. planning/standard-compatibility.json records unresolved standard-level discrepancies, including stale profile schema and graph support. Nothing under standard/ was modified. Required pilot incompatibilities must be resolved by the standard owner before pilot completion is recorded.
