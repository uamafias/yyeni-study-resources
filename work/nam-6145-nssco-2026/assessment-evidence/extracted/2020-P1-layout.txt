                               GOVERNMENT SUBSIDIES (N$)

      TransNamib                                       313	million         220             213
      NSFAF	(Namibian	Student	                         1,2	billion         962             950

      UNAM	(University	of	Namibia)                     999	million         926             919
      NUST	(National	University	of	                    482	million         533             522

      Namibia	Training	Authority                       509	million         392             400

     Namibian	State	Owned	Enterprises	(SOEs)	and	institutions	felt	the	effect	of	the
     tightening	state	budget	since	2016/17.		Despite	the	lower	amount	of	subsidies,
     SOEs	 and	 government	 institutions	 were	 still	 allocated	 about	 N$4,6	 billion	 from
     the	state	budget	in	2017/18.		At	N$4,6	billion,	government	subsidies	to	SOEs	in
     2017/18	was	N$1,8	billion	less	than	in	2016/17.
     Air	  Namibia,	  as	  a	  loss-making	  national	  airline,	  has	  received	  financial	  lifelines
     totalling	N$4,2	billion	from	the	government	over	the	past	seven	financial	years,
     from	2010/11	to	2016/17.
     Two	 thirds	 of	 the	 total	 amount	 of	 subsidies	 were	 allocated	 to	 higher	 education
     institutions	 such	 as	 NUST	 and	 UNAM.	 	 Higher	 education	 received	 65%	 of	 the
     subsidies	   that	   government	   would	   have	   given	   to	   other	   SOEs	   and	   other	   state-
     supported	institutions.
     Critics	of	SOEs	often	argue	for	a	privatisation	of	loss-making	enterprises.		The
     government	though,	believes	that	privatisation	may	cause	more	problems	than	it
     solves,	resulting	in	more	corruption,	a	greater	rich-poor	divide,	increased	social
     tensions,	and	enriching	only	a	select	few.		SOEs	are	assets	of	enormous	value
     to	the	state	and	thus	the	citizens	of	the	country.		A	business	such	as	NamPower,
     apart	from	the	potentially	large	future	earnings,	has	a	significant	amount	of	land,
     buildings,	equipment,	and	other	physical	assets.		To	privatise	such	a	firm	it	must
     be	ensured	that	the	business	is	sold	at	a	fair	price	and	it	also	needs	to	find	a	way,
     through	service	contracts,	or	through	regulation,	to	ensure	the	business	continues
     to	provide	affordable	services	to	all	citizens.
                                                                              (Adapted: Namibian March 2017)
