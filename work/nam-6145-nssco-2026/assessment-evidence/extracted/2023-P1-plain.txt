25 Road	accidents	in	Namibia	are	a	major	concern,	because	millions	of	Namibian	
dollars	are	spent	each	year	due	to	this.		Urban	areas	are	rapidly	expanding	in	
terms	of	population	and	geographical	size,	causing	significant	increase	in	traffic	
and	accidents.
The	frequency	of	injuries	and	fatalities	as	a	result	of	road	crashes	is	a	threat	to	
economic	and	social	development,	because	the	effect	of	accidents	goes	beyond	
those	directly	involved.
The	most	economically	productive	age	groups	(18	-	45),	who	represent	about	
35,8%	of	the	total	population	of	Namibia,	are	the	worst	affected	in	terms	of	
numbers	of	road	traffic	injuries	and	deaths.
Any	person	injured	in	a	road	crash	or	a	dependant	of	anyone	killed	in	a	road	crash	
in	Namibia	can	claim	assistance	from	the	state-owned	Motor	Vehicle	Accident	
Fund	(MVA	Fund).		The	MVA	Fund	is	funded	by	the	government	through	a	levy	
(tax)	added	to	every	litre	of	fuel	sold.

26 In	July	2020,	a	local	private	limited	Namibian	company,	harvested	Namibia’s	
first-ever	commercial	blueberries	which	are	cultivated	next	to	the	Okavango	
river.		 The	Government	Institutions	Pension	Fund	(GIPF)	has	invested	N$90	
million	in	this	project.		The	chairman	of	the	fund	said	the	pensioners’	money	
was	safe,	because	of	the	good	returns	expected	from	this	project.		The	quality	
of	the	fruit,	harvested	and	marketed	under	the	Namib	Blue	brand	name,	has	
been	exceptional	and	Namibia	exported	tons	of	blueberries	to,	for	example,	the	
Netherlands,	Germany,	Spain,	Russia	and	even	Hong	Kong.	The	product	is	also	
stocked	on	local	retail	shelves.
The	company	was	planning	to	double	its	production	area	by	the	end	of	2021	in	
order	to	expand	its	operations.		Blueberries	are	hand-harvested	to	ensure	that	
the	fruit	is	not	damaged	or	bruised.		The	industry	offers	employment	opportunities	
to	seasonal	workers,	because	during	peak	harvest	times,	a	larger	number	of	
workers	is	required.		The	management	of	the	firm	is	convinced	that,	with	the	right	
planning,	proper	infrastructure	and	efficient	management	in	place,	the	increasing	
output	might	continue	to	contribute	to	a	fall	in	average	cost.
