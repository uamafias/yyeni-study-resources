# 2.3 Financial institutions

## Notes

### B-6145-2.3-001 Exchange connects specialised activities
Specialisation means a producer does not make every good or service wanted. A grower supplies vegetables but may need clothing, tools and transport. A repairer supplies a service but still needs food. Exchange connects these activities, allowing each producer to obtain other outputs in return for what the producer supplies. The benefit depends on finding acceptable terms and having something the other side is willing to receive.

Barter is direct exchange of goods or services for other goods or services. It can be difficult when the vegetable grower wants a repair but the repairer does not want vegetables at that time. The participants need a double coincidence of wants: each must want what the other offers. Different quantities, timing and indivisible products can make agreement harder.

Generally accepted money can separate selling from buying. The grower sells vegetables for money and later uses the money to pay the repairer, who can spend it elsewhere. The repairer need not personally want the grower’s vegetables. Money does not eliminate the need for resources, trust or willing participants; it provides a widely acceptable means of settling transactions. The economic mechanism is improved coordination of exchange, not the creation of unlimited goods merely because payments are easier.

### B-6145-2.3-002 Distinguish function, quality and form
Money is an asset generally accepted as payment for goods, services and debts. Its functions explain the economic jobs it performs. Its qualities explain why it can perform those jobs conveniently and reliably. Its types describe the forms through which it is held or transferred. These are connected descriptions, but they are not interchangeable answers to the same question.

For example, being a medium of exchange is a function: money is accepted in settlement of a purchase. Portability is a quality: an asset that is easy to carry can make such transactions convenient. A banknote is one form of cash through which the payment may occur. Saying that a banknote is portable does not name all the functions of money, while naming a function does not identify a particular type.

Use the distinction to organise an explanation. State what money enables someone to do, identify the feature supporting that activity, and name the form used in the supplied situation. A payment can take place without handing over coins when a transferable balance is moved between accounts. Equally, possessing a card or a written payment instruction does not by itself establish the amount of money available. The underlying asset and the way someone accesses it need to be distinguished.

### B-6145-2.3-003 Functions of money
As a medium of exchange, money is accepted in payment, reducing the need for the double coincidence of wants required by barter. As a unit of account, it provides a common measure in which prices and records can be expressed. A household can compare the money prices of transport and food without writing a separate exchange ratio for every possible pair of products. The comparison still requires attention to quantities and quality.

As a store of value, money allows purchasing power to be carried from one time to another. Selling vegetables today can therefore help finance a repair later. Holding a fixed money amount does not guarantee unchanged real purchasing power: a rise in the prices of the goods later purchased reduces what that amount can buy. Money’s convenience as a store must be distinguished from a promise that its value is perfectly stable.

As a standard for deferred payments, money provides a unit in which an obligation payable later can be expressed. A stated money debt is easier to record than a promise to deliver a changing collection of products. The future value of that payment still depends on prices and the terms agreed. These functions can be related in one transaction, but a developed answer identifies the particular function and explains the problem it helps solve rather than simply listing four labels.

### B-6145-2.3-004 Qualities that support those functions
General acceptability matters because a seller must be willing to receive the asset in settlement. Durability helps preserve it between transactions, while portability makes it convenient to move or access for payment. Divisibility allows different transaction values to be settled without destroying the asset’s usefulness. Recognisability helps participants identify what they are receiving and reduces uncertainty about authenticity or value.

Compare a durable, recognisable note with an easily spoiled product used in barter. The note can be kept and passed on without the storage problem of the perishable product. Smaller denominations or divisible balances make it possible to settle a low-value purchase and give change. These qualities support exchange; they do not prove that every object with them is already money. A small durable stone is not money merely because it can be carried easily: general acceptance remains crucial.

Relative stability of value also matters. If purchasing power changes sharply, comparing prices or saving for a later purchase becomes more uncertain. This is a useful quality, not a claim that money’s purchasing power is fixed. The qualities should be linked to an actual function in the answer. Explain how portability eases transactions, how durability supports holding an asset, or how divisibility helps make different-sized payments. That connection is more informative than describing money as simply good or convenient.

### B-6145-2.3-005 Cash, transferable balances and payment devices
Cash includes notes and coins. These forms can be handed over directly in a transaction, subject to the relevant currency arrangements. Transferable bank-account balances can also support payments: a transfer moves a recorded monetary balance rather than requiring the payer to deliver physical cash. The medium used to send the instruction and the monetary asset transferred are different things.

A debit card provides access to funds according to the account arrangements; the plastic card is not itself the account balance. A cheque is an instruction to make a payment, rather than a separate quantity of money that should automatically be added to the account funds. A credit facility similarly concerns the ability to borrow or pay on agreed terms and is not evidence that a person already owns unlimited money. Whether a payment succeeds depends on the funds, agreement and service being used.

Historically, a commodity could function as money when it was generally accepted and had other uses. Modern monetary arrangements also include currency whose acceptance does not depend on the physical material being worth the amount printed on it. The basic teaching distinction is between the form of money and the mechanisms used to transfer it. Avoid treating every digital message, card or financial asset as identical to money. Identify the particular asset and how it is accepted or transferred in the supplied example.

### B-6145-2.3-006 State the currency quotation before converting
An exchange rate is the price of one currency expressed in another currency. The quotation convention matters. A rate expressed as Namibia dollars per US dollar answers a different numerical question from a rate expressed as US dollars per Namibia dollar. Read the units before choosing whether to multiply or divide.

Use invented teaching rates, not a current market quotation: US$1 = N$18. A US$50 invoice costs 50 × 18 = N$900, ignoring fees. The foreign units cancel against the per-foreign-unit rate, leaving Namibia dollars. In the reverse direction, N$900 ÷ 18 buys US$50 at that rate. Writing N$50 × 18 would mix up which currency amount is being converted. State the starting currency, the rate’s units and the resulting currency.

Now compare an invented later quotation of US$1 = N$20. Each US dollar costs more Namibia dollars, so the Namibia dollar is weaker against the US dollar in this example. The maintained Namibia-dollar/rand peg remains one-to-one: a change against the US dollar does not imply that the Namibia dollar has floated away from the rand. The two linked currencies can change together against a third currency. All these figures are original teaching data, with no assumption about today’s actual exchange rate or transaction fees.

### B-6145-2.3-007 Different agents experience different exchange-rate effects
For Namibia, the one-to-one rand peg remains maintained in these third-currency examples. A depreciation against a foreign currency makes a fixed foreign-currency-priced import cost more domestic currency. In the invented US-dollar quotation changing from N$18 to N$20 per US dollar, an importer needs more Namibia dollars for the same dollar-priced order. If the business passes the increase on, domestic buyers can face higher prices. If it absorbs the increase, its profit or other spending may be affected. The response depends on its costs, customers and available alternatives.

Exporters may gain price competitiveness if domestic-currency prices translate into lower prices for overseas buyers. That benefit is conditional. Overseas customers must respond, producers need capacity to supply additional output, and imported inputs can become more expensive. Exchange-rate movement alone does not establish a rise in export volumes or an improved trade result. Appreciation can reverse some effects: imports with unchanged foreign prices become cheaper in domestic currency, while exporters may become less price competitive.

A judgement therefore identifies the agent and the price conditions. An importer relying on essential foreign inputs may be harmed by a depreciation even while an exporter benefits. A household buying imported goods may gain from appreciation while a producer selling abroad faces pressure. Topic 7.4 develops the wider causes and trade-pattern implications. Here the central task is to interpret the quotation and trace direct effects, with demand response, capacity and input-cost conditions travelling with any claim of benefit.

### B-6145-2.3-008 Financial institutions perform different jobs
Central banks, commercial banks and stock exchanges participate in financial activity, but their main functions differ. A central bank performs system-level monetary functions. A commercial bank supplies services such as deposits, payments and credit to customers under the relevant terms. A stock exchange organises the buying and selling of securities such as shares. None should be described merely as a place that provides money without explaining the service or transaction.

The distinction matters when identifying a suitable institution in an example. A household needing to receive wages and make regular payments is considering deposit and payment services, not a public share issue. A company seeking ownership finance is considering investors and share arrangements, not simply a household transaction account. System-level monetary policy affects the environment in which these activities occur but does not replace the individual decisions of banks, investors and borrowers.

Institutional functions can complement one another. Payment services support trading, while orderly financial markets can help investors exchange securities. Complementarity does not make the organisations interchangeable. A useful comparison names the participant, the financial need and the mechanism through which the institution can help. Evaluation then checks the limitations of that mechanism, including risk, access, costs and whether the service fits the need actually stated.

### B-6145-2.3-009 Central bank: stability and system-level responsibility
A central bank performs central monetary functions such as monetary policy and oversight of the banking system. It can have responsibilities relating to currency, the government’s banking arrangements, banks’ settlement needs and financial stability. These are system-level roles, rather than a description of an ordinary branch accepting a household’s loan application. The exact allocation of statutory responsibilities belongs to the applicable institutional framework.

The Bank of Namibia’s monetary-policy framework supports price stability and the environment sustaining the one-to-one Namibia-dollar/rand link. This constraint matters when using actual Namibia examples. Do not assume unrestricted independent policy or a floating N$/rand rate. Equally, maintaining a currency link does not mean every domestic commercial transaction is decided directly by the central bank or that individual credit applications must be approved.

System stability can support confidence in payments and financial activity, benefiting households and firms. The limitation is that system-level action cannot guarantee the soundness of every private decision or meet every borrower’s preferred terms. A change intended to restrain inflation or support the monetary framework can also affect borrowing conditions. The appropriate judgement recognises the value of monetary stability while distinguishing it from direct customer services and from a guarantee of cheap or unlimited credit. Topic 6.2 develops policy transmission; this section owns the institution’s role and its boundaries.

### B-6145-2.3-010 Stock exchange: securities trading and finance
A stock exchange is an organised market for buying and selling financial securities such as shares. Shares represent ownership interests under the relevant company arrangements. An organised market can help buyers and sellers find one another and obtain information about trading prices. The possibility of resale can make participation more attractive to investors who do not wish to hold a security indefinitely.

Distinguish new finance from resale. When a company issues new shares and investors subscribe, funds can reach the company. When one investor sells an existing share to another investor, the buyer normally pays the seller; that transaction does not automatically create fresh finance for the company. A developed explanation can still link a useful resale market to investor willingness to buy new issues, but it should not confuse the two transactions.

The potential advantage for a company is access to ownership finance where its proposal and arrangements attract investors. Against this, preparing and maintaining an appropriate issue or market presence has responsibilities, ownership is shared and prices can fluctuate. Investor losses are possible, so an exchange does not guarantee a return or establish that every small enterprise should seek public equity. Prefer this route when the finance need, investor interest and governance capacity fit; ordinary short-term payment needs call for a different service.

### B-6145-2.3-011 Commercial bank: transactions, deposits and credit
A commercial bank accepts deposits, provides payment services and supplies credit. A customer can use an account to receive money, hold balances and arrange payments. A business can seek credit to finance an activity, subject to the bank’s assessment and terms. These services help coordinate transactions across time and between people who need not exchange cash in person.

Credit is not automatic. Banks assess repayment prospects and risks while managing funding and liquidity. A business expecting additional sales may still have a weak cash-flow plan or insufficient capacity to meet repayments. The bank’s willingness to lend depends on its assessment and circumstances, not merely on the business’s enthusiasm. Similarly, a deposit or payment service must be evaluated for access, charges and reliability rather than assumed suitable from its label.

Commercial-bank services can support households’ everyday payments and firms’ productive activities. The limitations include costs, repayment obligations and the possibility that a proposed loan is unsuitable for the need. A company seeking long-term ownership finance may prefer to investigate equity rather than finance every requirement with debt. A household wanting a transaction account needs payment access rather than a share issue. Judge the contribution by matching the service to the financial need and checking the stated risks and terms, while keeping central-bank policy and stock-exchange functions distinct.

### B-6145-2.3-012 A spendable balance is not the whole financial position
Use invented teaching data for an account after completed transactions, with charges ignored. A customer starts with N$300, pays N$70 to a supplier and has N$230 left. A bank loan then adds N$200 to the account, making N$430 available. The customer also owes the N$200 principal under the loan agreement, with any agreed borrowing costs additional. The larger spendable balance is therefore not evidence that the customer has earned an extra N$200 or become wealthier by that amount. Funds available for payment and obligations to repay are different parts of the position.

This distinction helps evaluate a bank’s credit service. Borrowing can make a useful purchase possible earlier, but the activity must support repayment on the agreed schedule. A business may hold usable equipment while still struggling to obtain cash at the times repayments fall due. A promising expected profit does not remove that timing problem. Banks therefore examine risk and liquidity as well as the purpose of an application. Lending also depends on the bank’s own funding, liquidity and the relevant framework; the ability to provide credit does not mean it can satisfy every request without limit.

### B-6145-2.3-013 Check a conversion in the reverse direction
A quick check is to reverse the conversion with the same quotation and assumptions. In the invented example US$50 × N$18 per US$ gives N$900. Dividing that N$900 by N$18 per US$ recovers US$50. If the starting currency and the answer have been mixed up, this check often reveals the error. With a new quotation, use the new rate consistently rather than mixing the first rate into one part of the calculation.

The quoted buying and selling terms of an actual provider may differ and charges can apply. These teaching calculations explicitly ignore those features, so their results should not be described as guaranteed amounts obtainable in a real transaction. The objective is to understand currency units and the relationship between amounts. Interpretation then explains whether a stated change makes a foreign unit more or less expensive, rather than merely repeating the multiplication.

### B-6145-2.3-014 Connect institutions with wider economic mechanisms
Topic 6.2 develops fiscal and monetary policy, while topic 7.4 owns wider trade patterns and the causes of exchange-rate changes. This topic owns exchange quotations, conversions, direct agent effects and institutional functions. Use those links to extend a mechanism without confusing the owner of each teaching task. Money’s role in exchange also connects back to specialisation in topic 1.3: specialised production becomes more useful when people can obtain other outputs through workable payment and trading arrangements.
