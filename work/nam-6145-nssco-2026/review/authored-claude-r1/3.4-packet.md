# 3.4 Advertising

## Notes

### B-6145-3.4-001 Paid communication with an intended purpose
Advertising is paid communication intended to inform or influence potential buyers about a product or organisation. It connects a message with an intended audience through a medium such as a poster, radio announcement, print publication or online advertisement. A business can pay to make people aware of a service, explain an offer or encourage preference for its product. The communication is not the same event as a customer completing a purchase.

An invented Namibian repair business, Blue Pebble Repairs, pays for a notice announcing a new bicycle-service desk. Its aim may be to tell nearby households where and when the service is available. The notice can be advertising even before anyone responds. Conversely, a customer’s unpaid conversation recommending the business is not the same paid communication, though it may also influence buyers. State the paid message and its intended purpose when identifying advertising.

### B-6145-3.4-002 Information, persuasion and reminders
Informative advertising gives potential buyers relevant facts: product features, availability, opening hours or a stated price. It can reduce the difficulty of finding a suitable supplier. Persuasive advertising seeks to influence preferences, for example by emphasising a style, convenience or image associated with the product. A reminder keeps an existing product or organisation in customers’ attention. These purposes can overlap within one message rather than requiring completely separate advertisements.

Blue Pebble’s statement that its bicycle desk opens on Saturday morning provides information. A separate phrase encouraging customers to choose its convenient local service is persuasive. The business might repeat the opening notice before the weekend as a reminder. Explain the role of the wording in the given message rather than classify an entire medium as always informative or always persuasive. Radio and posters can carry either kind of content.

### B-6145-3.4-003 Connect the method with the audience
The appropriate method depends on who needs to receive the message, where they can be reached, what needs to be explained and the resources available. A permitted local noticeboard can reach people who regularly pass it. Radio can communicate an audible message to listeners across a wider area. An online advertisement can combine text and pictures for people who use the relevant service. Access and attention differ, so a large potential audience is not evidence that the intended customers actually receive the message.

Use invented teaching facts: Blue Pebble has N$200 for a small local notice, and its intended customers regularly pass a community noticeboard where advertising is permitted. A clear poster with opening times can connect the information to those households within the stated resources. An expensive campaign reaching distant people would not automatically serve that local information purpose better. This is a connection between purpose, audience and method, rather than a guarantee of extra sales.

### B-6145-3.4-004 Awareness is not a guaranteed sale
After seeing an advertisement, a household still considers need, price, income and alternatives. Sales can also change because those other conditions change. A rise in sales after a campaign is therefore evidence to investigate, not proof that the advertisement alone caused it. Topic 3.2 explains demand determinants; topic 5.3 separates any additional sales receipts from the costs of the campaign. Here the task is to identify the communication’s purpose and explain how its method can reach the stated audience.
