# Worked teaching explanation — 3.2

Read after attempting the separate learner items. This worked version deliberately explains the results.

Use these invented weekly schedules for the fictional Khomas Bean Stall in Windhoek. Price in N$ per bag: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50. Original quantity demanded in bags: 100, 80, 60, 40, 20. Quantity supplied: 20, 40, 60, 80, 100. New quantity demanded: 140, 120, 100, 80, 60. Plot price vertically and quantity horizontally. Plot the original demand and supply schedules and label D and S. Their intersection is N$30 and 60 bags. New demand D1 lies to the right: at every listed price buyers now want 40 more bags. Supply is unchanged. The new intersection is N$40 and 80 bags. This is a demand shift and a movement along supply; a price rise alone would cause a movement along demand rather than the stated shift. The result depends on the supplied supply conditions and is not a guarantee for every possible market.
