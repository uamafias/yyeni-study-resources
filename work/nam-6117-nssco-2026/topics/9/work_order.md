- `OBJ-6117-9.3-01` tier 1 — state that most metals occur combined with other elements in compounds, as part of mixtures known as ores
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-02` tier 1 — identify ores of copper, zinc, uranium and tin as important ores that occur in Namibia and name the uses of the metals extracted from these ores (cross reference to 9.4)
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-03` tier 1 — name metals that occur ‘native’ e.g. gold, platinum
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-04` tier 2 — explain how the method of extraction of a metal from its ore depends on the position of the metal in the reactivity series
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-05-01` tier 1 — name the main ores of aluminium (Bauxite) and iron (Haematite) — focus: bauxite and aluminium
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-05-02` tier 1 — name the main ores of aluminium (Bauxite) and iron (Haematite) — focus: haematite and iron
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-06-01` tier 1 — name the main Namibian ores of copper (Chalcopyrite), lead (Galena) and uranium (uranium oxide) — focus: chalcopyrite and copper
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-06-02` tier 1 — name the main Namibian ores of copper (Chalcopyrite), lead (Galena) and uranium (uranium oxide) — focus: galena and lead
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-06-03` tier 1 — name the main Namibian ores of copper (Chalcopyrite), lead (Galena) and uranium (uranium oxide) — focus: uranium oxide and uranium
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-07` tier 2 — describe the essential reactions in the extraction of iron from Haematite in the Blast Furnace
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-08` tier 2 — describe the conversion of iron into steel by basic oxygen steelmaking (using molten iron from a Blast Furnace) and by using an electric arc furnace (using scrap iron and steel)
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-09` tier 2 — explain that the properties and uses of steel depend on the amount of carbon or other elements in it
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-10` tier 2 — describe the extraction of zinc from zinc blende (zinc sulfide)
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-11-01` tier 2 — describe the methods of extraction of aluminium (electrolysis) and copper (reduction/roasting) — focus: aluminium by electrolysis
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-11-02` tier 2 — describe the methods of extraction of aluminium (electrolysis) and copper (reduction/roasting) — focus: copper by reduction or roasting
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-12` tier 2 — describe metal ores as a finite resource and hence the need to recycle metals
- `OBJ-6117-9.3-13` tier 2 — discuss the advantages and disadvantages of recycling metals (iron/steel and aluminium)

**CU-6117-9.4** — sub-topic 9.4, 9 objectives, 2070–2970 words
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-01` tier 2 — explain that alloys are mixtures of metals and that the properties of metals can be changed, often to make them more useful
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-02` tier 2 — describe the idea of changing the properties of iron by the controlled use of additives to form steel alloys
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-03` tier 1 — name the uses of mild steel (car bodies and machinery) and stainless steel (chemical plant and cutlery)
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-04` tier 1 — state the uses of zinc in galvanising and making brass
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-05` tier 1 — state the uses of tin in tin-plating and making bronze
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-06` tier 1 — name the uses, related to their properties, of copper (electrical wiring and in cooking utensils) and of aluminium (aircraft parts and food containers)
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-07` tier 1 — name the uses of uranium (in nuclear power stations and nuclear weapons)
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-08` tier 2 — describe methods of rust prevention: paint and other coatings to exclude oxygen, galvanising (with zinc), plating (with tin) and sacrificial protection (with reference to the reactivity series)
- `OBJ-6117-9.4-09` tier 2 — account for the apparent unreactivity of aluminium and zinc in terms of the oxide layer adhering to the metal
