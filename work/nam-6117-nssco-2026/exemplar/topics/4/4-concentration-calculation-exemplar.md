## Amount and solution concentration

### Choose compatible units

Concentration tells us the amount of dissolved substance in a given volume of solution. When concentration c is measured in mol/dm³ and volume V in dm³, calculate the amount n in mol using n = c × V. Rearranging the same equation gives c = n / V. Write the required quantity before choosing a rearrangement: concentration and amount are different quantities, so their units differ.

### Convert volume before substitution

A solution has concentration 0.125 mol/dm³. For a 32.0 cm³ portion, first calculate V = 32.0 / 1000 = 0.0320 dm³. Substitute into n = c × V: n = 0.125 × 0.0320 = 0.00400 mol. The concentration describes the solution; the calculated amount describes this particular portion. Using 32.0 without conversion would give an answer one thousand times too large.

### Reverse the calculation

Suppose 0.00900 mol of solute is present in 30.0 cm³ of solution. The volume is 0.0300 dm³, so c = 0.00900 / 0.0300 = 0.300 mol/dm³. Check that the denominator is the solution volume, expressed in units matching the requested concentration. Dividing the amount by 30.0 would instead give a concentration per cm³; that number cannot be labelled mol/dm³.

### Show a checkable method

For 18.0 cm³ of a 0.250 mol/dm³ solution, the conversion gives 0.0180 dm³ and multiplication gives 0.00450 mol. Show the equation, substituted values and final unit. Keep extra digits during working and round the final answer to match the supplied data. As a size check, a portion smaller than one dm³ contains less solute than one dm³ of the same solution. This check detects many volume-conversion errors.
