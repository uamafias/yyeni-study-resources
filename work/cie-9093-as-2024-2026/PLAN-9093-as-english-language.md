Teach them; never present them as a closed list.

## 1. What the corpus says

The full analysis is `operations/analysis/9093-as-english-language-corpus-analysis.md`. What the plan takes from it:

- **What a grade takes** (corpus, median of 14 series): Paper 1 c 26/50, a 34; Paper 2 c 25/50, a 33; AS c 50/100, a 67. The November series (the October 2026 sitting belongs to it) ran close to the median: an a needed 65–67 and a c 48–50 from 2021 to 2025. **A grade c is Level 3 on every table and an a is the bottom of Level 4** (our arithmetic). Level 5 is not needed for an a.
- **Analysis is forty of the hundred marks.** Across Paper 1 Questions 1(b) and 2 and Paper 2 Question 1(b), the same abilities are rewarded: choosing features of form, structure and language, quoting them briefly, and saying what each does for this audience. The toolkit (1.2) and evidence (1.3) topics exist for this.
- **The ways of losing marks that recur across tasks** (report variants raising each, of 42):
  - the word range ignored (150-200, no more than 400, 600-900) — 40
  - accuracy: sentence boundaries, comma splices, tense and capitals — 37
  - control lost in over-ambitious long sentences — 36
  - naming a device or feature without saying what it does (feature-spotting) — 35
  - generalised comments on effect ("engages the reader", "positive connotations") — 35
  - summarising or paraphrasing content where analysis is asked for — 34
  - the form, role or purpose of the task missed — 34
  - evidence missing, over-long, or cited by line range — 31
- **The misconception register** holds 61 entries in `curriculum/misconceptions.json`, each with its evidence: the number of report variants whose sentences raise it, and the pattern that found them.
- **Section B’s question order changes** from paper to paper (six orders in 34 papers), so a practice task names its category, never a question number.

## 3. How each task is set and marked

The full specification of every task, including each level table paraphrased band by band, is `curriculum/answer-shapes.json`. Marks, lengths, texts and AOs are **syllabus**; the AO split inside each question and the ladders are **corpus**, and every ladder appears with the same bands in all 34 schemes for its paper. Every task is marked by levels; there are no point-marked items at AS.

- **Question 1(a), directed response** (P1, 10 marks). reading (AO1) /5, 5 levels; top band 5: a subtle, assured grasp of the source’s meaning, context and audience; its typical features drawn on perceptively • writing (AO2) /5, 5 levels; top band 5: assured, highly accurate writing; everything suits the audience and purpose, and ideas are developed with subtlety from start to finish. Set as: a new form for a named audience and purpose, re-using the source’s content (corpus). Forms set in the 34 papers: email 6, speech 5, letter 4, advertisement 4, diary 4, report 3, blog 2, podcast 2, leaflet 2, brochure 1, review 1. A role is given in 24 of the 34..
- **Question 1(b), comparison** (P1, 15 marks). reading (AO1) /5, 5 levels; top band 5: a subtle comparative grasp of both texts’ meaning, context and audience; typical features drawn on perceptively • analysis (AO3) /10, 5 levels; top band 9-10: a subtle, assured comparison of form, structure and language across both texts, explaining with precision how each writer’s choices suit its audience and create meaning. Set as: the instruction opens with Compare and names form, structure and language in all 34 papers (corpus).
- **Question 2, text analysis** (P1, 25 marks). reading (AO1) /5, 5 levels; top band 5: a subtle, assured grasp of the text’s meaning, context and audience; its typical features drawn on perceptively • analysis (AO3) /20, 5 levels; top band 17-20: an assured, coherent analysis that is very well organised; features chosen with insight; a subtle sense of how the writer’s choices suit the audience and make meaning; exact, apt language joining evidence to comment. Set as: Analyse, focusing on form, structure and language, in all 34 papers (corpus). Sources: newspaper or magazine article 11, fiction or narrative 5, blog or website 4, memoir, autobiography or diary 3, speech, talk or podcast transcript 3, review 2, travel writing 2, other 2, advertisement, leaflet or brochure 1, letter or email 1.
- **Question 1(a), shorter writing** (P2, 15 marks). writing (AO2) /15, 5 levels; top band 13-15: assured expression drawing on a broad repertoire of structures and vocabulary, some complex or unusual; very few errors; organised with clear logic and strong effect, ideas developed with subtlety throughout; the task achieved in full, everything relevant; the audience held throughout. Set as: a form for a named audience and purpose; in 29 of the 34 papers the prompt adds a focus sentence (corpus). Forms: speech 7, email 6, story 5, blog 5, report 3, leaflet 3, letter 2, diary 2.
- **Question 1(b), reflective commentary** (P2, 10 marks). analysis (AO3) /10, 5 levels; top band 9-10: assured analysis of the text’s form, structure and language, and of how the choices suit its audience and shape its meaning.
- **Section B, extended writing** (P2, 25 marks). writing (AO2) /25, 5 levels; top band 21-25: assured expression drawing on a broad repertoire of structures and vocabulary, some complex or unusual; very few errors; organised with clear logic and strong effect, ideas developed with subtlety throughout; the task achieved in full, everything relevant; the audience held throughout. Set as: one question per category in all 34 papers, in six different orders, so a question number never identifies a category; imaginative/descriptive: narrative 19, descriptive 15, a focus added in 33, a given sentence in 9; discursive/argumentative: article 14, essay 12, speech 8, letter 4; review/critical: a review in all 34 (corpus).

Two marking rules matter for model answers (corpus, every scheme): the **word range is judged, not deducted** (an answer well outside it is a poorer fit to the task), and on Paper 1 **form, structure and language need not be taken in separate sections**.

Every slot is decided in `topics/<T>/work_order.json` (and readable as `work_order.md`): its objective, subtype, AOs, **Bloom level**, and a one-line statement of what it teaches; for a performance task also its practice text, command word, tariff, the AO split of the table it models, and for a part (b) the part (a) it is written about.

- **No flashcard models a paper item.** Every AS question is levels-marked and long, so cards carry no command word and no tariff. They teach the terms and moves the tasks are built from.
- **MISCON cards** are discriminations, one per misconception-register entry, placed on the objective the error belongs to: the error, the correct move, and the test that tells them apart.
- **APP, INTERP, MECH and EVAL cards supply their own sentence or short extract**, original and written for the card. A card never depends on a practice text or on another card.
- **Performance tasks rehearse the paper twelve times over**: 12 directed responses and 12 comparisons on 12 Section A-style texts (1.4); 12 text analyses on 12 Section B-style texts (1.5); 12 shorter pieces and 12 reflective commentaries (2.1); 12 extended pieces, four per category (2.3, 2.4, 2.5). Around them sit short analysis tasks on extracts written into the prompt (1.1, 1.2, 1.3), style-matching (1.4), planning and paragraph building (2.2), and proofreading and rewriting (2.6).
- **Paired tasks.** A part (b) is written about its part (a): the comparison compares the model directed response with the source; the commentary analyses the model shorter piece. The work order links them (`written_about`); the item carries the link in `prerequisite_item_ids`.
- **AO balance by marks.** The rehearsed tasks carry 1,200 marks of practice, AO1 15%, AO2 45%, AO3 40%: the syllabus weights exactly (largest gap 0 points; `curriculum/ao_coverage.md`). Cards carry their objective’s AOs and no marks.

## 7. Answers and marking guidance

- **Every paper-style task is levels-marked.** Write a model answer to the top band and inside the stated length. Then guidance that names, table by table, the band descriptors in `answer-shapes.json` the answer meets, in your own words, and one or two sentences on what a Level 3 answer to the same task would do instead, so a learner can place their own. A grade c is Level 3; say so where it helps.
- **Directed responses and shorter writing**: the model answer is in the set form, for the set audience, with its conventions; the guidance names them.
- **Comparisons and commentaries** quote their part (a) model answer and the source, in short embedded quotations.
- **Short analysis tasks** (1.1, 1.2, 1.3): the model answer, then the features a complete answer identifies, each with its evidence and effect.
- Every item’s guidance names the parts its subtype or task type declares (`subject-profile.yaml -> answer_structures`, checked by C-16).

## 8. Scope

- Not in this route, and scanned for by C-11: `Paper 3`, `Paper 4`, `language change`, `child language acquisition`, `English in the world`, `language and the self`, `n-gram`, `phonemic transcription`.
- Never use the command word Discuss in a prompt (C-14): the AS papers never use it.
- Never state a fixed deduction for word counts.
- Each contract lists its adjacent topics: name them in passing if needed, teach them in their own topic.
- 3 conditioned mechanisms are registered (C-37): a short sentence creates emphasis *by contrast with the sentences around it*; a formal register is right *for an audience and a purpose*, not in general; a rhetorical question works *by the answer it invites*.
- Two category terms have variants that must be named wherever they appear (C-12): imaginative/descriptive (narrative, descriptive) and discursive/argumentative (discursive, argumentative).

## 9. What the checks catch, and what they do not

The suite checks structure, traceability, coverage of every objective, command-word discipline, declared answer parts, misconception cards, universals, scope terms, conditioned mechanisms and practice-text length. A worked slice of topic 1.5 in `work/cie-9093-as-2024-2026/exemplar/` passes all of it (`python3 standard/v0.2.0-draft/mine/exemplar_9093.py --prove`), including a 25-mark model analysis of an original feature article.

It cannot judge whether a model answer is really top band, whether a practice text reads well, or whether a text echoes a published one. Those need review, which under RS-42 runs on published material. After the run, `standard/v0.2.0-draft/mine/verify_9093.py` compares every item’s Bloom level, AOs, command word and tariff with its slot, and checks authored text against the corpus.
