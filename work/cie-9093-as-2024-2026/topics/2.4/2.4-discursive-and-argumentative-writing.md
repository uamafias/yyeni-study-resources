# 2.4 Discursive and argumentative writing

*cie-9093-as-2024-2026. Offline study notes, rendered from the approved content units on 2026-09-29. Everything here is also what the flashcards are built from.*

**What this topic asks of you**

- Structure a discursive piece dialectically: a thesis, its antithesis, and a synthesis; set counterarguments beside your points and answer them.
- Use rhetorical devices with control to persuade a named audience; keep the tone persuasive rather than ranting.

---

## Building the case

### Two variants, one task

Discursive writing explores an issue from more than one side before reaching a view; argumentative writing argues for one position and answers the other side. The variant the task’s wording signals: "explore", "consider" and "both sides" point to the first; "argue", "make the case" and a named position point to the second — and the wording is decoded before a word is written. The task is one of Section B’s category selection, set as 600–900 words for twenty-five marks.

### Dialectical structure

Dialectical structure is thesis, antithesis, synthesis: the case, its strongest opposition, and the reasoned view that survives the collision. Planning the piece means giving the opposition its strongest form — not its weakest — because a synthesis earned against the best counterargument is worth having, and one earned against a straw man convinces no one who held the objection. The plan marks three rooms: the thesis with its reasons, the antithesis voiced fairly, and the synthesis that gives ground where ground is owed and holds the line where the case stands.

### The four forms

The forms the question takes are the article, the essay, the speech and the letter to an editor. The article carries a headline and subheadings and addresses a magazine’s readership; the essay is continuous prose for a class or a publication that expects prose; the speech finds its hall, signposts its route and closes on its ask; the letter addresses the editor, quotes the claim it answers and signs off. Each convention set is part of the brief — writing an article with subheadings when an essay was set breaks the brief as visibly as writing 900 words of poem.

### Worked example: a dialectical plan

A task sets an article exploring whether homework should be abolished. The plan: thesis — homework done well works (the reason: practice consolidates; the example: the two-week spellings experiment in the plan’s writer’s own class; the consequence: learners who keep it keep the gain). Antithesis — the strongest case against (the reason: homework measures the home, not the learner; the example: the learner with the quiet room against the learner with the shift-working parent; the consequence: the practice that consolidates for one humbles the other). Synthesis — abolish the homework that measures homes, keep the homework that consolidates: set it shorter, set it smarter, and let the school day carry the practice. Three rooms, one door at the end.

### Where the pieces are marked

The twenty-five marks of this category are the same five strands as the whole of Section B: expression and range, accuracy, organisation and development, task achievement, and the audience held — and the dialectical structure serves the last three of them directly. The organisation strand rewards the thesis-antithesis-synthesis shape; the development strand rewards the reasons, examples and consequences inside each room; and the audience strand rewards the fairness of a piece that met its opposition instead of hiding it. The plan is not extra work beside the writing: it is the writing’s marks, arranged in advance.

### Three failures of the brief

The first failure is the one-sided list: reasons for, reasons for, reasons for, and a one-line conclusion — the argument that never happened, because the reader who held the objection leaves holding it still. The second is narrowing gone wrong: the wording names a whole civilisation and the piece takes it all on, thinning every section, when the writing should take one part and let the whole be visible behind it. The third is the wrong form: an article’s subheadings in an essay, a speech that never addresses its hall. Each failure is checked the same way — reread the brief, and test the plan against variant, scope and form.

## The language of argument

### Counterargument, rebuttal, concession

A counterargument is the position that opposes yours; a rebuttal is your answer to it; a concession is the part of it you admit is right before you answer the rest. The three work as a sequence: the counterargument is voiced fairly, the concession pays it its due, and the rebuttal answers what remains — which is why an argument that concedes nothing convinces nobody, and an argument that concedes everything has already stopped.

### Why concessions convince

A concession followed by a rebuttal makes an argument more convincing because the reader is shown fairness before they are shown the answer: the admission earns the trust the reply spends. "It is true that the new road will take the old trees, and the old trees matter — and the flooding that the road prevents will take more of them, root and bank" — the reader who loved the trees has been met, and then given a reason they can weigh rather than a verdict they must resist. Placing the counterargument beside the point, and answering it there, lets the reader watch the case survive its strongest test.

### Repetition in argument’s clothing

Repeating the same point in new words is not developing it: development adds reason, example or consequence, and an argument that repeats merely occupies the words its development needed. "Homework is stressful, and stress is what homework causes" has said one thing twice; the paragraph’s real work — who is stressed, how much, compared with what — has not begun. The cure is the addition test: what did the second sentence add? If the answer is only phrasing, the paragraph is repeating, and its development is still owed.

### Rhetorical force, from devices

Tricolon and anaphora add force to an argument because rhythm carries conviction: a list of three completes a thought with a beat the reader can agree to, and a repeated opening makes the points feel like a single pressure rather than a pile. Inclusive pronouns — we, us, our — build common ground with an audience: the reader is made a member of the case rather than its target, and the member argues with you while the target argues back. The devices are used sparingly and aimed: one tricolon per section, placed where the case turns.

### Force against rant

Persuasive force and a ranting or accusing tone are opposites: force argues the case, and a rant relieves the writer. The reader of a rant defends themselves — checking the case for faults instead of weighing it — and the audience the piece was written to hold becomes the audience the piece has attacked. The line between them is the second person: "you have ignored" accuses, and "we have not yet asked" recruits; the same evidence, the same numbers, lands on opposite sides of the reader’s defences.

### Worked example: the accused sentence repaired

A draft sentence: "You have all sat by while the library fell apart, and you should be ashamed." What it accuses the reader of — sitting by — is the one thing the reader will now argue about, and the piece’s evidence is suddenly on trial. The repair: "The library fell apart while the town was busy, which is not a charge — it is what happens to shared things when nobody is asked to share. We are the ones who can ask." The evidence stays, the shame goes, and the reader who was the target becomes the membership the argument needs.

### Judging an argument’s strength

Judging how convincing an argument paragraph is means asking what it overlooks: the strongest case is the one whose omissions you had to search for, not the one that never looked. The moves are three: name the paragraph’s claim and its evidence; ask which of the reader’s doubts it has not met; weigh whether the unmet doubt defeats the claim or merely qualifies it. A paragraph can be well written and unconvincing — the sentences polished, the objection unmet — and the judgement says which.
