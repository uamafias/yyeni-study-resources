STEAM RETURNS TO THE OMURAMBA VALLEY

For sixty years the Omuramba line carried copper, cattle and the post up the valley and down again. It closed in 1974. In 2023, after a seven-year restoration by the Omuramba Heritage Railway Trust, the line carries passengers: eleven kilometres of working steam through the riverine country your grandparents knew, behind a locomotive older than the town hall.

THE TRAIN
The Omuramba Express is hauled by locomotive No. 7, "Naledi", built in 1936 and restored to working order by volunteers and two paid engineers who between them have given eleven years to the project. The train carries 84 passengers in five restored carriages — three closed, one open-sided for the photographers, and the restaurant car, "The Firebox", which serves a three-course valley lunch cooked on the journey.

THE JOURNEY
Departure is 09:30 from Junction Station, where parking is free and the platform tea room opens at 08:30. The train follows the old valley route through four working farms and one private reserve, crossing the Omuramba twice on the original steel bridges, restored at a cost the Trust is proud to state: N$4.2 million, raised by 2,100 individual donors, whose names are cut into the platform rail at Junction. The round trip returns by 14:30, with a ninety-minute stop at Valley End, where the engine takes water exactly as it did in 1974, and passengers may watch, photograph, and ask the crew anything, which they usually do.

WHAT YOU WILL SEE
The valley route passes through riparian forest of camelthorn and ana trees, and the reserve stretch is a game corridor: oryx, kudu and the giraffe herd that the warden swears can tell 09:30 from any other time of day. The river itself is home to heron, kingfisher and one hippo of local renown, named Dora by the crew and best photographed from the open carriage, at a safe and respectful distance which the crew will explain without being asked twice.

WHO IT IS FOR
Everyone. The closed carriages are wheelchair accessible and the open carriage is not, which is stated plainly in the interest of plain speaking; the platform at Junction has step-free access to all closed carriages, and the crew are trained to assist. Children under twelve travel at half fare, and under three free on a lap. Well-behaved dogs travel free in the open carriage, because the Trust’s constitution says the line is for the valley, and the valley includes dogs.

PRICES AND TIMES
Adults N$380, children (3–12) N$190, seniors N$300. The train runs Wednesday to Sunday, departing 09:30, returning 14:30. Private charters — weddings, school groups, and the famous "Footplate Experience", in which you drive — are booked through the Trust office.

WHY WE DID IT
The Trust is a non-profit organisation, and every fare goes to the line: to coal, to maintenance, and to the apprenticeship programme that has now trained six young valley mechanics in steam, of whom four are employed on the railway and two on the mines’ heritage equipment. The line exists because 2,100 people believed a valley deserves its own train, and it continues because every Saturday, someone like you steps onto the platform and hears Naledi’s whistle for the first time.

BOOKING
Book at Junction Station, at the fuel station kiosk in town, or on the Trust’s site. Groups of fifteen or more should book ahead. The tea room opens at 08:30, the whistle is at 09:25, and steam, as the old drivers said, waits for no one — but it does come back, which is the whole point of the Trust, and the reason the copper line lives again.
