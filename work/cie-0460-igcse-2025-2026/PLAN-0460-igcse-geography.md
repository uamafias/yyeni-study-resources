## 1. What the corpus says

18 series, March 2020 to November 2025: 126 papers (42 each of Papers 1, 2 and 4) with their mark schemes and 17 examiner reports; 4,182 question parts, 11,333 marks. The content has not changed across the window.

- **Every Paper 1 question has the same shape:** parts of 1, 2, 3, 4, 3 and 5 marks built on a resource, then a 7-mark case-study part marked by levels. 216 of the 251 case-study parts ask for a named example or an area studied.
- **Paper 2 is a third mapwork** (1-mark parts: the key, grid references, directions and bearings, route distances, heights, cross-sections, relief and drainage) **and two thirds data** (graphs, tables, pyramids and photographs set in theme contexts).
- **Paper 4 follows fieldwork enquiries:** hypotheses, methods, results, a graph to complete, and conclusions. A conclusion keeps one mark for the decision and one for paired data.
- **Point-marked, with development at 4-5 marks.** Describe and Suggest at 3 marks are ideas from a longer list; at 5 marks a developed idea earns a further mark.
- **The failures that recur in every series:** ideas listed but not developed; vague terms (infrastructure, pollution, facilities); case studies with no named place or place detail, or answering a different question; no comparison where asked; on Paper 4, conclusions without data, vague methods and "repeat it" as the improvement, graph-completion errors.
- **82 examiner-evidenced misconceptions**, curated by hand from 4,559 error statements, each with a test that separates the two ideas.

**Never open a prompt with:** Add, Choose, Circle, Devise, Draw, Label, Locate, Match, Measure, Plan, Plot, Predict, Put, Shade, Show, Sketch, Tick. Devise, Predict and Sketch open no part in 2020-2025; Locate and Plan open three between them. Tick, Circle, Put, Choose, Match, Shade, Label and Add are response formats for a printed resource, and Plot, Draw, Measure and Show ask for marks on one: they appear only as steps inside a data_response or practical_task item. Questions that open "What is meant by …?" are asked on a card as "State what is meant by …".

- **Definitions** open with "State what is meant by": the papers ask "What is meant by …?" in 56 parts, and "Define" opens only 19.
- **Describe and Explain are different demands.** Describe says what something is like (from the resource, or a landform’s features); Explain says why or how. The reports record candidates swapping them in every series.
- **Suggest** (476 parts) asks for reasons that fit an unfamiliar resource; the answer applies knowledge to what the resource shows.
- **Justify** is a Paper 1 word at 4-5 marks: justify a choice between options.

## 8. Rules particular to this subject

- **Case studies are real places, written out.** Every case-study objective gets a named place at the scale the syllabus asks for (a named area of coastline, a named city district, a named farm or scheme), with place-specific detail. Prefer Namibian and southern African examples where they fit. This is the one place the resource states real-world facts: use only facts you are confident of, round figures and give their year, and describe the pattern without a figure where unsure.
- **Resources without a renderer.** A map extract is a markdown table of grid squares (eastings as columns, northings as rows) with their features, heights and a stated scale; a graph is its data table, for the learner to plot on their own paper; a photograph or field sketch is a described scene. No code fences (C-41).
- **Describe and Explain kept apart.** A Describe answer says what it is like; an Explain answer says why or how. A card never mixes them unless the command asks for both.
- **Develop at 4 marks and above.** Each idea is followed by its consequence or its reason ("… so rain reaches the river faster").
- **Words and figures together.** A data answer states the pattern in words and supports it with figures from the resource, with units.
- **Paper 4 enquiries end to end.** Hypotheses, method (equipment, siting, sampling, timing), a results table, a graph to complete, a conclusion on each hypothesis with paired data from two sites, and an evaluation that names what changes and why it is more reliable.
- **No brand names,** except the transnational corporation the 3.1 case study is about.
