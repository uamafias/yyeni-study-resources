## Content units

**CU-6118-5.6** — sub-topic 5.6, 10 objectives, 1064–1742 words
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-01` tier 1 — state the properties of magnets
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-02` tier 2 — explain magnetism and induced magnetism in terms of alignment of magnetic dipoles (molecular magnets)
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-03` tier 1 — state that the Earth has bar-magnet like properties and that a magnet freely suspended will align itself with the Earth’s magnetic field (convention: the north-seeking pole will be termed the “north pole” of the magnet)
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-04` tier 1 — distinguish between ferrous and non-ferrous materials
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-05` tier 1 — state that iron, nickel, cobalt and their alloys are ferromagnetic materials
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-06` tier 2 — describe an experiment to show the magnetic field around a bar magnet
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-07` tier 4 — draw and explain the pattern of field lines around a bar magnet and a horseshoe magnet
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-07-M01` tier 2 — Draw a bar-magnet field
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-07-M02` tier 2 — Draw a horseshoe-magnet field
- `OBJ-6118-5.6-08` tier 2 — describe and explain the difference between the design and use of permanent magnets and electromagnets (as used in e.g. a bell and a d.c. motor)

**CU-6118-5.7.1** — sub-topic 5.7.1, 18 objectives, 1915–3137 words
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-01` tier 1 — describe an experiment to demonstrate that a current carrying conductor (a straight conductor and a solenoid) has an induced magnetic field around it
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-02` tier 4 — sketch the magnetic field around a current carrying solenoid and around a current- carrying wire (both shape and direction of magnetic field lines are required, the direction can be found from the right-hand grip rule)
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-02-M01` tier 2 — Draw the field of a straight current-carrying wire
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-02-M02` tier 2 — Draw the field of a solenoid
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-03` tier 2 — describe how to build an electromagnet and outline its uses
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-04` tier 2 — describe an experiment that shows the difference between the electromagnetic properties of iron and steel
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-05` tier 2 — describe and use the relative directions of the force, current and magnetic field for a wire carrying a current in the field using Fleming’s left-hand rule
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.1-06` tier 2 — describe an experiment to show the corresponding force on beams of charged particles
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-01` tier 4 — explain how a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences a turning effect and that the turning effect is increased by increasing (1) the number of turns on the coil, (2) the current and (3) the strength of the magnetic field.
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-01-M01` tier 2 — Explain the turning effect on a current-carrying coil
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-01-M02` tier 2 — Explain how turns affect coil torque
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-01-M03` tier 2 — Explain how current affects coil torque
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-01-M04` tier 2 — Explain how field strength affects coil torque
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-02` tier 2 — discuss how this turning effect is used to make a simple electric motor
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-03` tier 2 — suggest how to convert an electric motor into a generator
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-04` tier 4 — describe the action of a split-ring commutator in a two-pole, single-coil motor and the effect of winding the coil onto a soft-iron cylinder
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-04-M01` tier 2 — Explain split-ring commutation
- `OBJ-6118-5.7.1.2-04-M02` tier 2 — Explain the soft-iron coil core
