GENERAL COMMENTS
Candidates demonstrated a good understanding of the content across the syllabus. Many were able to recall formulae
or equations of the subject, but failed to transform to the quantity asked in a specific question.

Many candidates struggled with questions that were asking for explanations, and also many failed to round off at least
to two significant figures or at least a correct rounding off that is not one significant figure if not specified in the question.
Candidates showed that the concept of general Physics is well understood. Many candidates demonstrated the
understanding of pressure but the challenge was applying it to specific questions. Many candidates demonstrated
that they could not derive equations, and it seems most candidates did not master the specific objective of deriving
equations.

A significant number of candidates could not follow the instructions of questions that required them to answer the
question on the Fig provided. This proved that some candidates do not read the instructions of the question before they
answer. It is worth noting that this is time consuming for other questions as candidates who do this end up redrawing
the Fig again and in most cases they do not draw it correctly.
Generally, candidates lack knowledge on how to answer questions demanding a clear comparison and distinguishing
between concepts. This is evident when they compared the pressure of water coming from the two stoppers in question
2, arrangement and motion of ice and liquid water in question 4, and also distinguishing between ferrous and non-
ferrous materials in question 7.

Correct spelling of scientific terminologies and definitions given in the syllabus is still a big challenge to most candidates
and these lead to loss of marks.
Moreover, most of the candidates find it difficult to transform equations or make a quantity in a question a subject of the
formula. This was the reason why some candidates could not score marks on the following questions:
•   Question 2 (a) (ii) when they had to make area the subject of the formulae from the equation of pressure
•   Question 3 (b) (ii) when they had to make F the subject of the formulae from the law of moments.
•   Question 4 (b) (i) when they had to make mass the subject of the formulae from the equation of density and
    when they had to calculate the number of turns and current in question 8.

POSITIVE SUGGESTIONS TO TEACHERS
•   Teachers should ensure that Mathematical requirements are well emphasized to ensure learners are introduced to
    the mathematical concepts in Physics. More emphasis should be given to the subject of the formulae. Be informed
    that a mark is given to the transformed formula.

•   Learners should be encouraged to spell the scientific terminologies correct. More tasks focused on the spelling of
    the scientific terminologies should be part of teaching and learning process. These can be done using games or
    written quizzes.
•   The specific objectives in the syllabus should be taught with understanding to ensure learners get overview of what
    the content is all about.

•   Emphasis should also be made on the conversion of units of measurement such as unit of time; milliseconds to
    seconds and others.
•   Teachers are encouraged to engage more on practical with the learners or show them some videos on how some
    Physics concepts work, e.g. electromagnetic induction process in a transformer etc.

•   In General Physics, teachers should pay more attention to graphs especially reading the value from the scaled
    graph.
•   Teachers should emphasize on the correct symbol denotations of physical quantities especially on the use of upper
    and lower cases of letters.

•   Teachers should emphasise that when a question asks distinguish between two concepts, both sides should be
    mentioned.

GENERAL COMMENTS
The aim of the examination is to enable learners to display their knowledge and understanding of practical physics
techniques, which include the following:
•   Graph plotting
•   Manipulating data to obtain results
•   Drawing conclusions
•   Tabulating readings with correct quantity and units
•   Control of variables
•   Dealing with possible sources of error
•   Understanding the concept of results being equal within the limits of experimental accuracy
•   Choosing appropriate apparatus

Most learners entering this paper were well prepared and the range of practical skills being tested proved to be
accessible to them. Most learners showed that they were able to draw their own personal practical experience to
answer the questions.
All parts of all questions were attempted and successfully completed within the allotted time. Most learners followed the
instructions and performed the calculations to the required accuracy.

Each practical examination will include a question where learners will be asked to plan an investigation. These answers
should be based on careful reading of the question and a logical application of good experimental practice.

POSITIVE SUGGESTION TO TEACHERS
To perform well in this examination, learners need to have a thorough grounding in practical work during the course.
Learners should have as much personal experience of carrying out experiments themselves as possible. The practical
work should include reflection upon, and the discussion of the significance of results, precautions taken to improve
accuracy and reliability and control variables.

Learners should be advised to read questions through very carefully to ensure that they are answering the question as
written and not simply recalling the answer to a different question.
The ability to record readings to an appropriate precision, usually reflecting the measuring instrument being used or to
quote a derived result to an appropriate number of significant figure was challenging for many learners.

Some learners found it difficult to choose an appropriate scale to plot their graphs and to draw a best-fit line to display
their data.
Candidates sometimes found it challenging to draw conclusions from given experimental data and justify them.
