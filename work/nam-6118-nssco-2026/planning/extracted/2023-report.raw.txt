GENERAL COMMENTS
Learners need to be taught definitions as they are clearly stated in the syllabus and not definitions from other sources.
Muchemphasisshouldalsobepaidtomathematicalrequirements. Learnersneedtobetaughtonhowtousecalculators
as most of them failed to use the second function of sine ratio to get the angle of refraction. Teachers should also allow
learners to practice on drawing ray diagrams using appropriate instruments, the use of protractors and ruler, not free
hand drawings.
Question 1
(a) This question was moderately answered. Although the definition for distance is clearly stated in the
syllabus, there were still a number of candidates who could not state it properly.
(b) Well answered. Most candidates could read the maximum distance on the graph as 58 km.
(c) Poorly answered. Fewer candidates could give the correct answer of 0.8 hour. Most candidates
proved that the interpretation of the distance – time graph is not well understood because most
candidates could not figure out that the horizontal line in the graph indicates that the cyclist is at rest.
Teachers are encourage to emphasise more on the interpretation of the distance – time graph.
(d) Poorly answered. Most candidates could not relate the steepness or gradient of the line to show
that the speed of the cyclist near the end of the journey is greater.
(e) Fairly answered. A moderate number of candidates could use the correct formula to calculate the
average speed, however fewer candidates struggled to get the correct time taken for the journey as 4 hours.
Question 2
(a) Very poorly answered. Most candidates gave 120 N as their answer. This is an indication that most
candidates did not understand the concept of constant velocity of an object implying that the resultant
force of an object is zero.
(b) Very poorly answered. Most candidates had no idea of what a labelled force diagram is, most
candidates drew a vector diagram and others did not attempt the question at all.
Most candidates did not acknowledge the friction caused by the movement on the rough surface, instead
they wrote air resistance and some candidates indicated the upthrust instead of reaction/normal force.
Some candidates lost marks because they drew lines of forces without indicating or showing the direction of
the forces and some candidates added additional incorrect forces.

GENERAL COMMENTS
Although there were some candidates who performed exceptionally well, there also those who performed poorly,
marks ranged from 0 to 36. Most candidates struggled to respond well to questions which required mathematical
applications and manipulations. This includes rounding answers correctly, giving answers to appropriate
number of significant figures, using a ruler to measure length (see question 3(b).

using a sensible scale, drawing line of best fit and calculation of gradient.
Question 4 in this paper proved that most candidates are not sufficiently exposed to practical activities and they
appeared clueless on how they approached this question.

understanding of experimental work.
