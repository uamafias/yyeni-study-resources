General comments

In the first place, acknowledgements to all teachers who tried with all efforts, regardless of the difficult time of COVID- 19
to ensure candidates achieve the content of Physics with understanding for them to score good marks.

with understanding, know scientific terminologies, good basic mathematics and all other related sciences. Teachers are

how to answer different questions with different command word, e.g. explain, define etc. and avoid a lot of options as

1 GENERAL COMMENTS

The paper proved to be challenging to the average learner, which is probably not surprising, given that the standalone
NSSCO Physics is a new syllabus (new subject), and it is expectedly much more demanding compared to the old
curriculum (legacy) NSSCO Physical Science.

The nature of the subject calls for a higher cognitive demand in mathematical manipulations and applications compared
to the old NSSCO Physical Science. Nonetheless, a large number of learners struggled to respond to questions which
required Mathematical applications and manipulations. This includes, but not limited to, rounding answers correctly,
giving answers to the appropriate numbers of significant figures, conversion of units such as cm to m, approximate
evaluations of numerical expressions, using usual mathematical instruments (ruler) to measurement length, as well
as the application of given formulas. In addition, a large number of learners also struggled to demonstrate scientific
skills such as recording of data in tables in a logical manner including constancy and consistency in decimal places
and significant figures, plotting graphs including the use of sensible scales, drawing lines of best fit (straight lines and
smooth curves), extrapolating the lines of best fit to determine/ deduce expected data from axes of graphs, calculation of
gradient, reading of meter scales (e.g. spring balance, thermometer), using a given scale to determine the actual length,
drawing conclusions from given experimental quantitative and qualitative data, handling anomalous (inconsistent)
results, discussing trends in results and suggesting sources of errors.

The questions which required learners to demonstrate their knowledge on exposure to practical activities such as
planning an investigation were also generally poorly answered by most learners. These are for example to plan an
experiment to investigate how the height of the image depends on the distance between the steel pin and the lens
(question 5), which proved to be the most challenging question to most learners in the entire question paper.

Overall, given the fact that Paper 3 is an Alternative to Practical Paper, there is acommon impression that learners are not
sufficiently exposed to assessment objective C (practical — experimental and investigative — skills and abilities) aspects
of the syllabus. These include the suggested practical activities at the end of each topic; Topic 1 (scientific processes)
as well as Annexe A (Practical assessment and Paper 3, Alternative to practical paper). It should be highlighted that the
Annexes in the syllabus are there to provide additional information to teachers and learners regarding aspects of the
subject which cannot be explicitly stated in the specific objectives in the syllabus. It is therefore important that learners
are taken through and informed about these Annexes plus all other additional information in the syllabus preamble and
assessment sections.

POSITIVE SUGGESTIONS TO TEACHERS

First and foremost, it is important to highlight that in terms of assessment objectives, there is a significant difference
in the NSSCO Physics Paper 3 compared to the old curriculum NSSCO Physical Science Paper 3. Page 39 of
the NSSCO Physics syllabus specifies that Paper 3 is only comprised of Assessment Objective C questions, the
entire 40 marks, whilst the old curriculum NSSCO Physical Science Paper 3 used to be comprised of a combination
of Assessment Objective A, B and C questions. This means that teachers need to put a great emphasis on
assessment objective C and also make practical work an integral part of teaching and learning.

Once again, the manner in which learners responded to the questions in this paper, to a large extent, demonstrated
that learners lack conceptual understanding and exposure to practical experiences. Teachers are encouraged to
use easily accessible and locally available / familiar materials to conduct practical activities in their classrooms.
Annexe A on Page 43 of the syllabus outlines the experimental contexts that guide the assessment objective C
skills. At the end of each topic in the syllabus, there are also suggested practical activities. These should serve as
a guide regarding the expectations of Paper 3.

Lastly, although NSSCO Physics used to be an integral part of NSSCO Physical Science as a Physics section
(together with Chemistry), it is important to highlight that when Physics became a standalone subject, it became
much more demanding compared to when it was just one of the two sections in the old NSSCO Physical Science
curriculum. Thus, the teaching of the NSSCO Physics needs to be done in the depth that it deserves and not just
exactly the same way we have been teaching the old NSSCO Physical Science curriculum.
