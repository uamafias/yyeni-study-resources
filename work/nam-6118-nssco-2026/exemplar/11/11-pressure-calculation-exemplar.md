## Pressure: meaning, calculation and area

### Force spread over an area

A force can be distributed over a small or large surface. Pressure compares the perpendicular force with the contact area over which it acts. It describes force per area, rather than force alone. The pressure can therefore change even when the force stays fixed. The force component used is perpendicular to the surface: a sideways force must not simply be inserted as though it were a normal load.

### The pressure relation

Use P = F/A for a load spread uniformly across area A. Here F is the perpendicular force in newtons, A is the area in square metres and P is the pressure in pascals. A pressure of one pascal corresponds to one newton acting on each square metre. Write the relation before inserting the values. If the requested quantity is force, rearrange it to F = PA; if it is area, use A = F/P. The units help check the rearrangement: force divided by pressure has units of area.

### Worked example: a model press

A classroom model exerts 48 N uniformly over a contact area of 0.0060 m². Use P = F/A. Substitution gives 48 ÷ 0.0060 = 8000 Pa. This is 8.0 kPa to two significant figures. Keep the unrounded value during working, then choose a final precision consistent with the supplied measurements. The force and area are invented practice values, not measurements from a published experiment.

### Changing area

Imagine replacing the model press foot with a wider foot while leaving its perpendicular force unchanged. That force is now shared over more square metres, so the force acting on each square metre decreases. The pressure is lower. If area doubles while force is fixed, pressure becomes half as large. That conclusion depends on holding the force fixed; changing force at the same time requires a fresh calculation.

### Converting an area

An area of 12 cm² converts to 0.0012 m². Both dimensions change when an area is converted: a centimetre is one hundredth of a metre, so a square centimetre is one ten-thousandth of a square metre. Convert the area first, then substitute. Dividing an area by one hundred instead of ten thousand introduces a factor-of-one-hundred error. A sketch of a square with both sides labelled can make the squared conversion easier to see.
