2. [T21][S2] ITEM-6118-21-001-SYNTH. Prompt "Explain how rubbing and an electroscope can demonstrate electrostatic charging." canonical_answer: "Link rubbing to electron transfer and opposite net charges. The charged object can redistribute charge in an electroscope and cause deflection." The answer is an instruction to the marker ("Link ... to ..."), not a statement a learner can learn; marking_guidance "Combine the linked ideas and explain the reason: Link rubbing to electron transfer and opposite net charges. (1 mark)." is template text that just prefixes the answer. Correct version: "Rubbing transfers electrons from one material to the other, so one becomes negatively and the other positively charged (equal and opposite). Bringing the charged object near the electroscope cap pushes like charge onto the leaf and stem, so the leaf rises (deflects) because like charges repel." Same defect: ITEM-6118-21-007-SYNTH ("Link charged material to an electric force in a field.") and ITEM-6118-21-014-SYNTH ("Link a point charge to radial field lines."). Rewrite all three as statements.

3. [T21][S3] ITEM-6118-21-004, -005, -006, -008, -009, -010 marking_guidance. Every guidance line is "Property or unit: <the answer repeated> (1 mark)." (e.g. 21-004 "Property or unit: Positive and negative. (1 mark)."; 21-006 interaction is not a "property or unit"). Other cards use "Method step:", "Cause and effect:", "Contrast:" prefixes on the same restated answer. No accepted alternatives, no "do not accept" lines. Give each card real credit points (e.g. 21-008: "toner is attracted/held by charged areas of the drum; accept 'sticks to charged image'").

4. [T21][S3] ITEM-6118-21-006 "State the interaction between two negative charges." The syllabus objective is "unlike charges attract and like charges repel"; no flashcard covers "unlike charges attract" (only MC 3 and P02(b) cover the repel half). Add a card for unlike charges attracting.

5. [T21][S3] ITEM-6118-21-013 "Explain a conductor-insulator difference using the simple electron model." The syllabus says "give typical examples"; the card gives none (notes: copper; dry plastic, glass). Answer wording "does not have comparable freely mobile electrons throughout its bulk" is stilted. Better: "In a metal some electrons are free to move through the material, so charge can flow; in an insulator the electrons are held in their atoms and cannot move through the material. Examples: copper (conductor); dry plastic, glass (insulators)."

6. [T21][S3] ITEM-6118-21-002 "Describe a simple demonstration of frictional charging." answer "Rub a dry insulating rod with suitable cloth and show that it can attract small paper pieces." "suitable cloth" and "insulating rod" are vague (name polythene rod and woollen/cotton cloth, or acetate and silk). The notes (section "Show frictional charging") state that attraction of paper "does not prove the paper initially has opposite net charge"; the card gives no way to show the rod is charged other than attraction.
