1 Michael started a small take away business in a food caravan. He sells different
types of toasted sandwiches, brötchens with fillings and wraps. He is situated in
the town centre where his customers can reach him easily on their way to work
or during lunch. Michael appoints an employee to help him in the take away.
(a) (i) Identify two elements of effective communication.
(ii) Identify two qualities of a good manager.
(b) Identify two ways the business responds to the needs of the community.
(c) Identify and explain two purposes of business activity in the take away
caravan.
--- PDF PAGE 3 ---
(d) Evaluate the appropriateness of on the job training for the worker in the
take away caravan.

2 Silas and Ruben started Safety First, a small manufacturing business. They
produce burglar bars and security gates for customers and businesses. They
employ four workers to help with the cutting of steel, welding and painting of
these burglar bars and security gates. All the employees belong to a trade union.
(a) (i) Define the term trade union.
(ii) Identify two reasons why workers join a trade union.
(b) Identify two reasons why funds are needed in a business.
(c) Explain two factors that may increase Safety First’s productivity.
--- PDF PAGE 5 ---
(d) Suggest the best method of production for Safety First burglar bars and
security gates.

3 Jonas Johannes start Johannes Leather a few years ago. At the start he
only produced leather shoes for men and women. Due to a high demand for
his leather products he expanded his range of products to leather belts and
handbags. He sells all his products in his own shops located throughout the
country. 40 workers are employed in the factory. Jonas is seeking finance to
buy an industrial sewing machine.
(a) (i) Define product life cycle.
(ii) Define promotion.
(b) Identify two levels of specialisation.

4 Producing agricultural products locally has its benefits. Farmers near the
Okavango river have the option to enter into a leasehold contract with the chief
of the tribe whose land it is.
Attie Beukes, a farmer, entered into such a contract with one of the tribes chiefs
and decided to start producing maize. His idea is to produce maize that can be
locally consumed and the surplus exported to neighbouring SADC countries.
Attie delegates different responsibilities to his farm workers.
(a) (i) Define the term responsibility.
(ii) Define delegation.
(b) Identify two objectives of consumers.
--- PDF PAGE 10 ---
(c) Discuss two reasons why the Namibian government intervenes in the
exporting activities of farmers such as Attie Beukes.
--- PDF PAGE 11 ---
(d) Evaluate the implications to Namibia of being a member of SADC.

5 A well-established accounting firm in Namibia, Check your Shillings, has
experienced organisational conflict during the last year. The management
assigned more clients to each worker than in previous years. Now each
worker has 20 clients to serve per month.
Workers used to receive a monthly salary, but now management have decide
to use a time rate based on a percentage of the hourly rate charged to clients.
However, it will only be paid if the work is completed on time. Workers
will also be asked to become involved in decision-making in the business.
Management hope these changes will resolve the organisational conflict.
(a) (i) Define organisational conflict.
(ii) Give two advantages of worker participation.
(b) State two non-financial rewards that could be offered to workers at Check
your Shillings.
--- PDF PAGE 13 ---
(c) Management decided that workers are only paid when a task is completed
in the time period allocated for it.
Analyse how time rate can motivate the workforce of Check your Shillings.
--- PDF PAGE 14 ---
(d) Suggest how Check your Shillings can use democratic leadership to
improve worker participation.
