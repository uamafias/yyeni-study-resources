## Attracting, choosing and developing staff

### Attracting, choosing and developing staff

Recruitment attracts suitable applicants for a vacancy; selection chooses the most suitable applicant using relevant criteria. Induction introduces the new employee to the business, colleagues, procedures and role. Further training develops the skills and knowledge needed for work. Start by identifying the vacancy and preparing a job description and person or job specification. Advertise through suitable channels, assess applications consistently, shortlist, interview or use relevant work tests, then appoint and introduce the worker. Develop capability and review progress after appointment. Training can be on-the-job in the work setting or off-the-job away from normal work tasks. These methods differ in resources and suitability; attracting an applicant is not the same as developing a current employee.

### Putting the idea to work

Omi Foods appoints a new order clerk and then teaches that person to use its order system. Advertising and gathering applicants belong to recruitment, comparing applicants belongs to selection, introduction to the workplace is induction and developing system capability is training. A course does not itself attract applicants to a vacancy.

### Recruitment

Recruitment identifies a vacancy and attracts a pool of suitable applicants. Define the duties and relevant person criteria before selecting a channel such as an internal notice or external advertisement. Internal recruitment can use known staff and offer progression but restrict the pool; external recruitment can bring new skills but requires assessment and introduction. The aim at this stage is suitable applications, not yet a final appointment.

### Putting the idea to work

Hela Print needs an additional customer clerk. It should specify the role and advertise through channels likely to reach suitable people. Announcing a vague vacancy without duties makes it harder for applicants to judge their fit.

### Selection

Selection evaluates applicants and chooses the person best suited to the stated job criteria. Use the application evidence to shortlist consistently, interview relevantly and use an appropriate task or skills test where useful. Check stated qualifications or references as appropriate before making an offer. A friendly interview alone may not show accuracy or competence. The process should use job-related evidence rather than unrelated impressions.

### Putting the idea to work

Eya Goods needs accurate order entry. Shortlisted applicants complete a brief practice entry task and explain how they check it. The task provides evidence of accuracy relevant to the vacancy. Compare the same essential criteria rather than choosing solely on confidence in conversation.

### Induction

Induction training is an introduction given to a new employee, explaining the firm’s organisational structure, activities and procedures. It can include colleagues, reporting, safety arrangements, role expectations and where to ask for help. It can reduce uncertainty and avoidable early mistakes. It is not a substitute for learning every specialist task. On-the-job and off-the-job methods can support later skill development according to need.

### Putting the idea to work

Kena Repairs introduces its new assistant to the supervisor, workplace layout and procedure for reporting customer problems. This is induction because it helps the new worker understand the organisation and settle into the role. Further system practice may still be required.

### Training

Training develops the knowledge and skills required for work. Identify the capability gap, set a learning objective, choose a suitable method, provide practice and feedback, then check whether the worker can perform the task. On-the-job training uses workplace guidance; off-the-job training provides learning away from normal duties. The goal is capability, not attendance alone. Production or service improvements depend on applying the learning with adequate equipment and support.

### Putting the idea to work

Ota Stores’ clerk repeatedly enters incorrect collection dates. Teach and practise the date-checking procedure, then assess correct use on sample bookings. A certificate of attendance alone would not show the error has been resolved.

### Keeping the ideas apart

Explain the difference: Omu Goods advertises a vacancy, then later teaches the appointed worker to use its stock system.

Advertising attracts applicants and belongs to recruitment. Teaching the appointed worker develops capability and is training. Selection chooses between applicants; it is another stage. A business may need both recruitment and development, but one is not a definition of the other.

## Choosing a training method

### Choosing a training method

On-the-job training develops skills while working with guidance from an experienced colleague or supervisor. It can use the actual equipment and avoid travel, but takes the trainer’s time and may pass on poor habits or expose real work to mistakes. Off-the-job training takes place away from normal duties, for example through a course or simulated practice. It can provide specialist teaching and space to learn without disrupting real orders, but costs time and fees and may not match local equipment perfectly. Choose according to the task, safety, existing expertise, cost, time and learning needs. Induction introduces the new worker but does not replace all specialist skill development.

### Putting the idea to work

Tano Stores has a patient experienced clerk and a safe practice account on its familiar booking system. A new assistant needs routine booking skills. On-the-job guidance using the practice account suits the local routine and available expertise. An external course could offer broader knowledge but adds cost and may teach a different system. Prefer supervised workplace learning here, checking competence before live work; a specialist or hazardous task without a competent trainer would change the choice.

## Designing a consistent recruitment pack

### Designing a consistent recruitment pack

Recruitment documents should describe the same vacancy consistently. A job description states the role’s duties and responsibilities; a job specification states the relevant abilities, qualifications or experience required from the person. An application form gathers comparable evidence against those criteria. An advertisement attracts suitable applicants by stating the role, location, key requirements and how and when to apply. Avoid vague requests for a wonderful person or irrelevant conditions that do not help perform the job. Distinguish essential criteria from desirable experience so useful applicants are not excluded by an unnecessarily high barrier. The documents should support a fair evidence-based comparison, not guarantee that the selected person will perform perfectly.

### Putting the idea to work

Nela Repairs in Tsumeb needs a full-time bookings assistant to record customer requests, maintain the repair diary, confirm collection times and communicate changes. The assistant reports to the office supervisor. Hours are Monday to Friday, 08:00–17:00, and pay is N$6,500 per month. Work requires accurate records, clear English communication and basic computer skills; previous booking experience is desirable, not essential. Initial system instruction will be provided. Model pack: Job description—Bookings assistant, Nela Repairs, Tsumeb; reports to office supervisor; record requests, update diary, confirm collections and communicate changes; full-time, Monday to Friday, 08:00–17:00; N$6,500 per month. Specification—essential accurate record keeping, clear English communication and basic computer use; desirable booking experience. Application fields—name, contact, relevant skills and examples, relevant work history, availability and referee details with permission. Advertisement—Nela Repairs seeks a full-time bookings assistant in Tsumeb; duties and essential skills as above; previous booking experience desirable; system instruction provided; obtain the form at the Nela Repairs workshop office, 18 Oryx Lane, Tsumeb, and return it by 31 October 2026. The pack connects criteria to the actual tasks.

### Job descriptions

A job description is a document which outlines the responsibilities and duties expected to be carried out by someone employed to do a specific job. Include the title, reporting relationship, purpose and relevant main duties. It describes the job rather than listing the person’s qualifications. A concise description supports both recruitment and later understanding of responsibilities.

### Putting the idea to work

Nela Repairs in Tsumeb needs a full-time bookings assistant to record customer requests, maintain the repair diary, confirm collection times and communicate changes. The assistant reports to the office supervisor. Hours are Monday to Friday, 08:00–17:00, and pay is N$6,500 per month. Work requires accurate records, clear English communication and basic computer skills; previous booking experience is desirable, not essential. Initial system instruction will be provided. Model job description: Title—Bookings assistant. Employer/location—Nela Repairs, Tsumeb. Reports to—Office supervisor. Purpose—Maintain accurate repair bookings and customer arrangements. Duties—Record requests; update the diary; confirm collection times; notify the supervisor and customer of changes; protect the accuracy of records. Working arrangement—Full-time, Monday to Friday, 08:00–17:00 with the agreed break. Each duty follows the stated vacancy rather than inventing technical repair responsibilities.

### Job specifications

A job specification is a document which outlines the requirements, qualifications and expertise required from a person to do a specific job. Connect each criterion to the work and distinguish essential from desirable criteria. It describes the person needed, not the tasks alone. Unnecessary requirements can exclude capable applicants and shrink the pool without improving performance.

### Putting the idea to work

Nela Repairs seeks a Tsumeb bookings assistant for accurate diary entries and clear customer confirmations. Basic computer use is essential. Previous booking experience is useful but can be learned after appointment. Choose job-related essential and desirable person criteria. Model specification: Essential—accurate record keeping demonstrated by an example or practical task; clear English communication for customer confirmations; basic computer skills for the diary; ability to organise overlapping requests. Desirable—previous booking or customer-record experience. System-specific experience is not essential because initial instruction is provided. Do not demand a technical repair qualification for this administrative role.

### Application forms

An application form gathers comparable information from applicants so the employer can assess relevant criteria consistently. Use clearly labelled spaces for contact information, relevant skills, experience and availability. Ask for evidence, not just a yes/no claim of being suitable. Keep the form focused on the job and explain submission. It does not replace an interview or practical assessment where those are needed.

These fields gather evidence aligned to the specification.

### Job advertisements

A job advertisement communicates a vacancy to attract suitable applicants. State the employer, title, location, main duties, key essential criteria, pay and any benefits, working hours, application method and a clear closing date. It should be accurate and consistent with the description and specification. Too little detail creates unsuitable applications; unnecessary demands can discourage suitable people. Choose a channel applicants can access.
