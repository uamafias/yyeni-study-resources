## Content units

**CU-9702-11.1** — sub-topic 11.1, 12 objectives, 3593–5146 words
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-01` tier 3 — infer from the results of the α-particle scattering experiment the existence and small size of the nucleus
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-02` tier 2 — describe a simple model for the nuclear atom to include protons, neutrons and orbital electrons
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-03` tier 2 — distinguish between nucleon number and proton number
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-04` tier 2 — understand that isotopes are forms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons in their nuclei
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-05` tier 2 — understand and use the notation ᴬZX (nucleon number A written above proton number Z, before the symbol X) for the representation of nuclides
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-06` tier 2 — understand that nucleon number and charge are conserved in nuclear processes
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-07` tier 2 — describe the composition, mass and charge of α-, β- and γ-radiations (both β – (electrons) and β+ (positrons) are included)
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-08` tier 2 — understand that an antiparticle has the same mass but opposite charge to the corresponding particle, and that a positron is the antiparticle of an electron
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-09` tier 2 — state that (electron) antineutrinos are produced during β – decay and (electron) neutrinos are produced during β+ decay
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-10` tier 2 — understand that α-particles have discrete energies but that β-particles have a continuous range of energies because (anti)neutrinos are emitted in β-decay
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-11` tier 3 — represent α- and β-decay by a radioactive decay equation of the form ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂α
- `OBJ-9702-11.1-12` tier 2 — use the unified atomic mass unit (u) as a unit of mass

**CU-9702-11.2** — sub-topic 11.2, 6 objectives, 1796–2573 words
- `OBJ-9702-11.2-01` tier 2 — understand that a quark is a fundamental particle and that there are six flavours (types) of quark: up, down, strange, charm, top and bottom
- `OBJ-9702-11.2-02` tier 2 — recall and use the charge of each flavour of quark and understand that its respective antiquark has the opposite charge (no knowledge of any other properties of quarks is required)
- `OBJ-9702-11.2-03` tier 2 — recall that protons and neutrons are not fundamental particles and describe protons and neutrons in terms of their quark composition
- `OBJ-9702-11.2-04` tier 2 — understand that a hadron may be either a baryon (consisting of three quarks) or a meson (consisting of one quark and one antiquark)
- `OBJ-9702-11.2-05` tier 2 — describe the changes to quark composition that take place during β – and β+ decay
- `OBJ-9702-11.2-06` tier 2 — recall that electrons and neutrinos are fundamental particles called leptons

## Performance tasks

- **multiple_choice_set** — A set of 12 four-option multiple-choice questions on this topic in the style of Paper 1, one mark each: about half recall and half application or calculation, every distractor built from a known error (the misconception register first), the key and the reason each distractor is wrong on the back.
- **short_answer** — A Paper 2 style structured question: four or five linked parts around one situation (define or state, calculate, explain), about 8 marks, each part with its own mark allocation.
- **calculation** — A multi-step calculation set in a practical or everyday situation (about 5 marks): the equation in symbols, the substitution, each intermediate value to three significant figures, the answer with its unit and a justified number of significant figures.
