## Content units

**CU-9702-3.1** — sub-topic 3.1, 6 objectives, 1804–2580 words
- `OBJ-9702-3.1-01` tier 1 — understand that mass is the property of an object that resists change in motion
- `OBJ-9702-3.1-02` tier 2 — recall F = ma and solve problems using it, understanding that acceleration and resultant force are always in the same direction
- `OBJ-9702-3.1-03` tier 1 — define and use linear momentum as the product of mass and velocity
- `OBJ-9702-3.1-04` tier 1 — define and use force as rate of change of momentum
- `OBJ-9702-3.1-05` tier 2 — state and apply each of Newton’s laws of motion
- `OBJ-9702-3.1-06` tier 2 — describe and use the concept of weight as the effect of a gravitational field on a mass and recall that the weight of an object is equal to the product of its mass and the acceleration of free fall

**CU-9702-3.2** — sub-topic 3.2, 3 objectives, 902–1290 words
- `OBJ-9702-3.2-01` tier 2 — show a qualitative understanding of frictional forces and viscous/drag forces including air resistance
- `OBJ-9702-3.2-02` tier 2 — describe and explain qualitatively the motion of objects in a uniform gravitational field with air resistance
- `OBJ-9702-3.2-03` tier 2 — understand that objects moving against a resistive force may reach a terminal (constant) velocity

**CU-9702-3.3** — sub-topic 3.3, 4 objectives, 1203–1720 words
- `OBJ-9702-3.3-01` tier 2 — state the principle of conservation of momentum
- `OBJ-9702-3.3-02` tier 2 — apply the principle of conservation of momentum to solve simple problems, including elastic and inelastic interactions between objects in both one and two dimensions
- `OBJ-9702-3.3-03` tier 2 — recall that, for an elastic collision, total kinetic energy is conserved and the relative speed of approach is equal to the relative speed of separation
- `OBJ-9702-3.3-04` tier 2 — understand that, while momentum of a system is always conserved in interactions between objects, some change in kinetic energy may take place

## Performance tasks

- **multiple_choice_set** — A set of 12 four-option multiple-choice questions on this topic in the style of Paper 1, one mark each: about half recall and half application or calculation, every distractor built from a known error (the misconception register first), the key and the reason each distractor is wrong on the back.
- **short_answer** — A Paper 2 style structured question: four or five linked parts around one situation (define or state, calculate, explain), about 8 marks, each part with its own mark allocation.
- **calculation** — A multi-step calculation set in a practical or everyday situation (about 5 marks): the equation in symbols, the substitution, each intermediate value to three significant figures, the answer with its unit and a justified number of significant figures.
