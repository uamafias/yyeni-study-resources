0500 is **task-led, not command-word-led**. The four words open the short items. The extended tasks are instructions: summarise on a focus, write a letter in role, analyse the language of two paragraphs, write a description. So a prompt uses a command word only where its slot names one, and otherwise the task form of the paper item it models. Two words outside the table do work in the papers (corpus): "Write" opens 120 Paper 2 composition titles and the directed-writing task and "evaluate" opens the first bullet of all 32 readable directed-writing tasks; each is used only inside its own task.

## 1. What the corpus says

The full analysis is `operations/analysis/0500-igcse-first-language-english-corpus-analysis.md`. What the plan takes from it:

- **What a grade takes** (corpus, median of 17 series): Paper 1 C 35/80, A 49; Paper 2 C 42/80, A 55; overall C 78/160, A 103, A* 118. November runs higher than June in every year 2021–2025; in 2023–2025 a November C needed 80–83 and an A 107–110. **Paper 1 is the harder paper.**
- **Reading success** is restating accurately in one’s own words; finding every point on a summary’s focus and grouping them; explaining what language makes a reader see or feel, not only what it means; developing ideas from a text in role without inventing.
- **Writing success** is evaluating two texts rather than summarising them, organised by argument; writing a form that reads as that form; knowing that a description is built from detail and a story from a shaped plot; accuracy, which counts in Paper 2.
- **The errors the reports return to, series after series,** are the misconception register: 39 entries in `curriculum/misconceptions.json`, each with the number of series whose reports raise its theme.

## 3. How each task is set and marked

The full specification of every task, including each level table paraphrased band by band, is `curriculum/answer-shapes.json`. The marks, lengths, texts and skills are **syllabus**; the item layouts, the 16/24 composition split and the ladders are **corpus**, and every ladder appears unchanged in 39–42 of the 42 schemes for its paper. The ladders are paraphrased; paraphrase them again when teaching them.

- **Question 1, comprehension** (P1, 15 marks): point-marked; items of 1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3 marks. Set as: short questions, each naming the paragraph to use; two-mark items ask the candidate to explain in their own words (corpus).
- **Question 1, summary** (P1, 15 marks). reading /10, 5 levels; top band 9-10: understands the task thoroughly; covers a wide range of relevant ideas with consistent focus; points selected skilfully enough to give an overview • writing /5, 3 levels; top band 4-5: clear, fluent and mostly concise; well organised; in the candidate’s own words, with well-chosen vocabulary. Set as: a selective summary on a stated focus (corpus).
- **Question 2, short-answer questions** (P1, 10 marks): point-marked; 2(a) 4 x 1 mark: the text’s word or phrase for a given idea; 2(b) 3 x 1 mark: the meaning of a single word as used, in the candidate’s own words; 2(c) 1 x 3 marks: one example from a short extract, and how it suggests something.
- **Question 2, language task** (P1, 15 marks). reading /15, 5 levels; top band 13-15: wide-ranging discussion of well-chosen language across both paragraphs; comments that add meaning and associations and show why the writer chose the words; imagery handled with precision and imagination; a clear grasp of how language works.
- **Question 3, extended response to reading** (P1, 25 marks). reading /15, 5 levels; top band 13-15: the text analysed and judged in depth; ideas developed, sustained and anchored in it; a wide range of ideas; supporting detail woven in throughout; all three bullets fully covered; a voice that stays consistent and convincing • writing /10, 5 levels; top band 9-10: a register that suits audience and purpose; language that convinces and stays apt; ideas put with force in varied, well-chosen language; structure and sequence sound throughout. Set as: written in role, in a given form, with exactly three bullets to address in every one of the 42 tasks (corpus); forms where readable (33 of 42): letter 9, speech 7, interview 7, journal or diary 5, article 3, report 2.
- **Section A, directed writing** (P2, 40 marks). writing /25, 6 levels; top band 22-25: a highly effective style conveying subtle meaning (W1); structured carefully for the reader (W2); a wide, sophisticated vocabulary used precisely (W3); a highly effective register (W4); accuracy almost always secure (W5) • reading /15, 6 levels; top band 13-15: successfully evaluates explicit and implicit ideas and opinions; absorbs ideas from the texts into a developed, sophisticated response. Set as: two bullets; the first opens with "evaluate" in all 32 readable tasks, and all 32 ask for both texts and the candidate’s own words (corpus); forms where readable (24 of 42): letter 13, speech 6, article 5.
- **Section B, composition** (P2, 40 marks). content and structure /16, 6 levels; top band 14-16: content complex, engaging and effective; structure secure, balanced and managed for deliberate effect. Descriptive: many well-defined, developed ideas and images build a convincing overall picture with varied focus. Narrative: a well-defined, strongly developed plot using features of fiction — description, characterisation, an effective climax, convincing detail • style and accuracy /24, 6 levels; top band 21-24: precise, well-chosen vocabulary and sentence structures varied for effect; a consistent, well-judged register; accuracy almost always secure.

## 4. What each topic gets

Every slot is decided in `topics/<T>/work_order.json` (and readable as `work_order.md`): its objective, subtype, sub-objectives, AO, **Bloom level**, command word and tariff where it models a paper item, and a one-line statement of what it teaches.

| subtype | cards | what it is for in 0500 |
|---|---:|---|
| DEF | 44 | Precise meaning of one term |
| FEATURE | 16 | The features or conventions of a form or a kind of writing |
| PROC | 21 | The ordered steps of a technique |
| WHY | 5 | The reason a technique works |
| DIST | 34 | The boundary between two confusable ideas, and the test that separates them |
| MISCON | 39 | Discriminating a recorded confusion from the idea it is confused with |
| MECH | 11 | How a language choice produces its effect |
| APP | 31 | A technique carried out on a supplied original sentence or extract |
| INTERP | 3 | Reading an implied meaning out of a supplied detail |
| EVAL | 3 | A judgement on how convincing a supplied view is, with the case against |

- **MISCON cards** are discriminations, one per misconception-register entry, placed on the objective the error belongs to: the error, the correct move, and the test that tells them apart.
- **APP, INTERP, MECH and EVAL cards supply their own sentence or short extract**, original and written for the card. A card never depends on a practice text or on another card.
- **Performance tasks rehearse the paper**: 21 comprehension items on 3 Text A-style passages; 4 summaries; 14 word-and-phrase items on 2 Text C-style passages; 4 pairs of "one example" and language-task items; 5 extended responses in 5 different text types; 3 evaluation plans; 3 directed-writing tasks (letter, speech, article); 3 descriptive and 3 narrative compositions; 5 editing and rewriting tasks.
- **AO balance by marks.** The exam is 80 reading and 80 writing marks. The rehearsed tasks here carry 336 reading and 385 writing marks, close to the exam’s even split, with a composition counted as the single 40-mark task it is; 112 flashcards practise reading skills and 95 writing skills. No per-topic item-count target is set, because each topic is one task (C-18 reports not_run by design).

## 5. Bloom’s levels

Every item carries its Bloom level and its AO; they are different axes. The level comes from the subtype for flashcards and from the task for performance items (`assessment-framework.json -> blooms_taxonomy`).

| level | items | marks of practice carried by performance tasks |
|---|---:|---:|
| Remember | 81 | 0 |
| Understand | 124 | 59 |
| Apply | 36 | 0 |
| Analyse | 15 | 132 |
| Evaluate | 6 | 45 |
| Create | 14 | 485 |

By count the bank leans to Remember and Understand, because every technique needs its terms and its discriminations. By marks it leans the other way: **Create carries 485 of the 721 marks of rehearsed practice**, because Question 3, directed writing and composition are compositions. That is the shape of 0500, and it is why the notes must teach craft, not only terms.

## 7. Answers and marking guidance

- **Point-marked items** (comprehension, word-and-phrase, "one example"): the canonical answer, one mark per point, the acceptable alternatives, and what is not credited and why.
- **Level-marked tasks** (summary, language task, extended response, directed writing, composition): a model answer written to the top band and inside the length, then guidance that names, table by table, the band descriptors (paraphrased) the answer meets and how. Add one or two sentences on what a middle-band answer to the same task would do instead, so a learner can place their own.
- **Summaries** also list the content points on the focus, in our words.
- Every item’s guidance names the parts its subtype or task type declares (`subject-profile.yaml -> answer_structures`, checked by C-16).

## 8. Scope

- Not in this route, and scanned for by C-11: `Coursework Portfolio`, `Component 3`, `Component 4`, `Speaking and Listening`.

- Each contract lists its adjacent topics: name them in passing if needed, teach them in their own topic.
- Two conditioned mechanisms are registered (C-37): a short sentence creates emphasis *by contrast with longer ones*; a formal register is right *for an audience and a purpose*, not in general.

## 9. What the checks catch, and what they do not

The suite checks structure, traceability, coverage of every objective, command-word discipline, declared answer parts, misconception cards, universals, scope terms, conditioned mechanisms and practice-text length. A worked slice of topic 1.3 in `work/cie-0500-igcse-2024-2026/exemplar/` passes all of it (`python3 standard/v0.2.0-draft/mine/exemplar_0500.py --prove`).

It cannot judge whether a model composition is really top band, whether a practice text reads well, or whether a text echoes a published one. Those need review, which under RS-42 runs on published material. After the run, a planner audit compares every item’s Bloom level, sub-objectives, command word and tariff with its slot.

## 10. Limits
