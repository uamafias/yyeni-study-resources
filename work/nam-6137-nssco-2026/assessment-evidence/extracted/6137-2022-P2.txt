6 Study	Fig.	4,	which	describes	some	of	the	features	of	agriculture	in	Kenya,	East	Africa.
Agriculture in Kenya
Agriculture	is	vital	to	the	economy	of	Kenya.		Farming	is	mostly	carried	out	on	small	
farms;	no	more	than	2	hectares	is	cultivated.		These	small	farms	account	for	75%	of	
total	production	and	much	of	this	is	subsistence	production.		The	main	cash	crops	are	
tea,	vegetables	and	coffee.		Production	of	tea	and	vegetables	is	increasing	and	these	
crops	are	important	exports.		Exports	of	high-value	vegetables,	flowers	and	house	
plants	often	take	place	by	air	to	European	markets.		Coffee	has	declined	in	importance.	 	
Crop	outputs	vary	unpredictably,	partly	due	to	weather	conditions.
As	some	arable	land	is	used	for	export	crops,	Kenya	needs	to	import	food	to	
feed	the	population.		 For	example,	large	quantities	of	wheat	and	maize	are	often	
imported.		Environmental	issues	facing	the	country	include	deforestation,	soil	erosion,	
desertification	and	water	pollution	from	increased	use	of	fertilisers	and	pesticides.
