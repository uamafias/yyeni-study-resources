## What was verified

Each accepted file opens as a single large-format PDF, has a 1:50,000 scale and a matching map-place and examination-year/Paper 2 label. All four full maps and their legends were visually inspected after rendering. Checks are acquisition/resource matching, not full answer validation for every mapwork question. Source PDFs were not altered.

The 2021 sheet was recovered from the public archive’s **legacy 4332** folder. Its filename is preserved. The sheet itself is labelled **NSSC 2021 - Geography Paper 2** and shows Mossel Bay. The current 6137/2/21 paper’s Linkside school, spot height 166 and beacon 129.2 are present; map bounds cover the coordinates in that paper. This is why it is accepted as a matching resource rather than rejected solely because of its archive folder.

2022: Parys, with the Vaal River and the town in the southeast, labelled for 2022. 2023: Tulbagh, including Wolseley and the southern map area used in the paper, labelled for 2023. 2024: Lüderitz, with Penguin Island, Agate Park, Kolmanskop and the airstrip, labelled NSSCO 2024.

A later **NSSCAS 2023 Mossel Bay** scan was also downloaded while searching. It is retained in candidates/AS-2023-Mossel-Bay/ as supplementary provenance and **is not counted** as an original 2021 resource.

## 2020 gap

The exact 2020 Amersfoort examination extract was not found in the public archive’s new/legacy 2020 folders, local files or targeted public searches. The local 2020 question paper contains no embedded attachment. A general [NGI Amersfoort base map](https://store.avenza.com/products/2729bb-amersfoort-chief-directorate-national-geo-spatial-information-map) is listed for the Avenza app, but that is not authenticated as the exam’s exact extract and has not been substituted. A school-held or DNEA-supplied copy would resolve this gap.
