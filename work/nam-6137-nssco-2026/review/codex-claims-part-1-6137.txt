Most earthquake deaths are caused by building collapse; earthquakes of similar strength usually kill more people in low-income countries, where buildings are weaker, building codes are often not enforced and warning, rescue and medical services are more limited, although focus depth, distance, population density, time and ground conditions also affect losses.

CL-6137-1.3.06-01-R1
When a river loses volume (dry season, end of a flood, evaporation, seepage into a permeable bed, abstraction) it has less energy and deposits load; floods in the ephemeral Kuiseb River in the Namib lose water downstream and deposit all their transported material in the lower river, with no discharge to the Atlantic Ocean.

Seif (linear) dunes occur near Swakopmund and dominate inland in the Namib Sand Sea; they form where winds blow from two directions (strong southerly winds for much of the year and another direction at other times), so the ridge lengthens along the resultant direction instead of migrating; the sand comes mainly from the Orange River, moved north by longshore currents and blown inland by southerly winds.

Star dunes accumulate where winds blow from many directions; they have slip faces on three or more arms radiating from a high centre and grow upward rather than migrating; in the Namib Sand Sea they occur on the eastern side, around Sossusvlei, where some exceed 300 m in height.

CL-6137-1.5.04-R1
Constructive waves are low-energy, low waves with a long wavelength that break infrequently and spill onto the beach; strong swash and weak backwash build the beach up. Destructive waves are high-energy, high and steep waves with a short wavelength that break frequently and plunge; weak swash and strong backwash drag material down the beach and offshore.

Waves are driven by the wind, so they usually approach the shore at the angle of the prevailing wind; longshore drift therefore moves material along the coast in the general direction of the prevailing wind, and a labelled diagram shows prevailing wind, oblique swash, backwash straight down the beach and drift direction.

Temperature generally falls with height because the lower atmosphere is heated mainly from below by the ground, and higher air is thinner and holds less heat; the average fall is about 6.5°C per 1,000 m.

CL-6137-1.6.08-03-R1
The cold Benguela Current flows northward along Namibia’s coast with coastal upwelling; air cooled from below over the cold water is stable, so fog is common but rainfall is very low, with average rainfall within 40 km of the coast below 20 mm a year.

CL-6137-1.6.08-06-R1
Air rising near the Equator spreads poleward and sinks at about 30° N and S, forming dry subtropical high-pressure belts where many hot deserts lie; the ITCZ shifts south in the southern summer, bringing most of Namibia’s rain between about September and April, with the north-east averaging more than 650 mm a year.

CL-6137-1.7.03-01-R1
Equatorial rainforest climates are hot all year with an annual range of about 2°C because the midday sun is always high; strong heating makes moist air rise in the ITCZ low-pressure belt, where it cools and condenses into cumulonimbus clouds that give heavy convectional rain, often in the early afternoon, with high humidity and light surface winds.
Ohio University, The Physical Environment, Tropical Rainforest climate: https://people.ohio.edu/dyer/TPE/tpe_3e/climate_systems/tropical_rainforest_1.html, accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-1.7.03-02-R1
Savannah (tropical wet-dry) climates are rainy in the high-sun season when the ITCZ brings converging, rising air and convectional rain, and dry in the low-sun season when sinking air of the subtropical high suppresses cloud and rain; monthly means range from about 18°C to above 25°C.
Ohio University, The Physical Environment, Tropical Wet/Dry climate: https://people.ohio.edu/dyer/TPE/tpe_3e/climate_systems/tropical_wetdry_1.html, accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-1.7.03-03-R1
Tropical deserts lie under subtropical high pressure, where descending air warms, its relative humidity falls and condensation is prevented, giving clear skies, very low humidity and a large daily temperature range; cold upwelling coastal water and rain shadows add to the dryness, and many such deserts lie on western coasts beside cold currents.
Ohio University, The Physical Environment, Tropical Desert climate: https://people.ohio.edu/dyer/TPE/tpe_3e/climate_systems/tropical_desert.html, accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-1.7.03-03-R2
On the Namib coast the cold Benguela Current cools the sea air; this cool, moist air is trapped in an inversion within a few hundred metres of the ground beneath sinking anticyclonic air, so it forms fog in the evenings and mornings rather than rain.
Atlas of Namibia online, climate pages: https://atlasofnamibia.online/node/44 and https://atlasofnamibia.online/node/51, accessed 2026-10-30
