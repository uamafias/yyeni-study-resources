BL-6137-1.7.01-06
To locate the regions on a world map, identify the Equator, tropics, continents and oceans first. Then compare the shading with those references. A tropical forest band across central Africa differs from desert shading in northern Africa, while savannah can occupy seasonal tropical environments between or beyond wetter zones. If a resource uses different colours, interpret them from the key rather than from an assumed convention that green always means forest. A map may also show human modification, protected areas or a selected vegetation date, so the legend and caption constrain what can be concluded.

BL-6137-1.6.07-06
An original combined resource can place a cloud band near a mapped front and a low-pressure system. The spatial alignment is consistent with frontal cloud, particularly if movement through successive images supports the relationship. It does not prove that every pixel in the band is producing the same rain rate. A station outside the band may still have local cloud or precipitation. Combine the scales and times carefully: a morning chart and an afternoon image may represent a moving system, so a positional difference is not automatically an error.

BL-6137-1.6.04-21
A combined original station record can test several operations without conflating them. On three comparable days, maximum temperatures are 24, 28 and 26°C, rainfall totals 0, 8 and 4 mm, and sunshine totals 8, 3 and 6 hours. Mean maximum temperature is 26°C; its range is 4°C. Rainfall total is 12 mm and mean daily rainfall is 4 mm. Sunshine total is 17 hours. Each result belongs to its variable and interval. The coincidence that the temperature range and mean daily rainfall are both numerically four does not give them the same unit or meaning.

BL-6137-1.6.08-16
A combined supplied resource can make the interaction visible. Site A is low-latitude but coastal beside cold water and under frequent subsiding-air influence. B is at similar latitude but higher altitude and receives moist air forced over relief. A could have cool near-surface conditions and little rain with fog possible; B could be cooler because of height but receive more rain through uplift. The comparison cannot be explained by latitude alone. Its purpose is to connect each supplied control to a distinct pathway, while keeping the conclusions conditional on the resource’s stated evidence.

BL-6137-1.6.03-17
An original matching resource can include a funnel-and-vessel instrument, paired wet/dry thermometers, a rotating-cup instrument, a direction vane and a recording pressure trace. Match each to rainfall depth, humidity inference, wind speed, direction and pressure change respectively. The key should state any unfamiliar design. A fair interpretation does not require a learner to infer an invisible wick or an unreadable scale. Providing the relevant working part makes recognition an application of the physical principle, rather than a guess from where the picture is placed on the page.

BL-6137-2.1.07-03
Processes can include receiving and checking catch, cleaning, heading or gutting, filleting, chilling or freezing, grading, packing and storage before dispatch. The order and details depend on product and quality requirements. A flow diagram can branch where a by-product leaves one stage or where different products take different routes. Processing is not the same as transport from the vessel to the factory, although that delivery is connected to it. The distinction helps identify secondary activity without claiming that every part of a long fishing chain occurs inside one building.

BL-6137-2.3.93-01
A tourism questionnaire can investigate visitors' activities or residents' experiences, but its target population must be stated. A visitor survey cannot represent residents merely because it asks about the destination. Use clear, neutral questions, suitable answer options and an opportunity to decline. Avoid collecting names or unnecessary personal information. Pilot the questions to identify ambiguity before starting the main sample.

BL-6137-2.3.92-01
A footpath investigation can test whether path width or exposed depth varies with visitor use. Define the measured feature: width between stated path edges differs from the depth of an incision below a reference line. Use a tape across the path at a consistent orientation, and a level reference and ruler where depth is measured. Obtain permission and avoid unsafe slopes or obstructing users.

BL-6137-2.3.91-01
An environmental-quality assessment records selected conditions using a defined scoring scale. For a tourism investigation, conditions might include litter, noise, vegetation damage and waste facilities. State whether higher scores mean better or worse quality. Use the same indicators, category descriptions and observation period at each site so that totals can be compared. A score is an organised judgement, not a direct measurement of every environmental process.

BL-6137-2.3.05-05
A hypothetical spending account shows the distinction. An enterprise receives N$20,000 and makes N$8,000 of payments to local workers and suppliers, N$7,000 outside the area and retains N$5,000 before any other specified obligations. The record establishes those flows, not that every retained dollar reaches households equally. If the account does not state complete costs, ownership or distribution, do not turn the retained amount into a precise profit or poverty-reduction claim.

BL-6137-2.2.02-09
A farm diagram is read by following arrows and their labels. An arrow from a seed store to a field represents an input flow. An arrow through sowing and crop growth represents a production sequence. An arrow from harvest to a market represents an output destination. Water availability is a physical input, while pumping and applying it are processes. A label should identify the quantity or action being represented; arrow direction alone does not explain an unfamiliar symbol.
