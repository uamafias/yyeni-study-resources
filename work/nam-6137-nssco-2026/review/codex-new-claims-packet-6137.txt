CL-6137-1.7.03-01-R1
Equatorial rainforest climates are hot all year with an annual range of about 2°C because the midday sun is always high; strong heating makes moist air rise in the ITCZ low-pressure belt, where it cools and condenses into cumulonimbus clouds that give heavy convectional rain, often in the early afternoon, with high humidity and light surface winds.

Savannah (tropical wet-dry) climates are rainy in the high-sun season when the ITCZ brings converging, rising air and convectional rain, and dry in the low-sun season when sinking air of the subtropical high suppresses cloud and rain; monthly means range from about 18°C to above 25°C.

Tropical deserts lie under subtropical high pressure, where descending air warms, its relative humidity falls and condensation is prevented, giving clear skies, very low humidity and a large daily temperature range; cold upwelling coastal water and rain shadows add to the dryness, and many such deserts lie on western coasts beside cold currents.

The Namibian government, through the Ministry of Health and Social Services, launched the national antiretroviral programme in government health facilities in 2003 and provides ART free of charge in the public sector; it also runs prevention of mother-to-child transmission (from 2002), voluntary counselling and testing, free condom distribution and awareness campaigns, and the National Code on HIV/AIDS and Employment prohibits discrimination based on HIV status.

CL-6137-3.2.04-02-R1
Regional and local authorities take part in Namibia's HIV decision-making structures; NGOs provide counselling and testing, home-based care, psychosocial and nutritional support and help for orphans and vulnerable children, often with donor funding.

CL-6137-3.2.04-03-R1
About 90% of Namibians are Christian, so churches and faith-based organisations reach many people with counselling, care, support for orphans and some treatment; private firms run workplace programmes (a national business coalition on HIV/AIDS was set up in 2004); individuals can test, use condoms, reduce partners, take treatment daily and support people living with HIV.

CL-6137-3.2.05-01-R1
Correct and consistent condom use lowers the birth rate (narrower pyramid base, slower growth) and reduces HIV infections, so fewer adults aged about 15-49 die, working-age bars stay wider, fewer children are orphaned and more people reach old age.
https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/condoms; https://census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2008/demo/namibia08.pdf (with/without AIDS death and growth rates); accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-3.2.05-02-R1
Wider use of ARVs lowers the death rate and raises life expectancy, keeping the working-age bars wide, widening the top of the pyramid and allowing slightly faster population growth; economically it keeps skilled workers productive and households earning, but is a large lifelong cost to government for medicines, staff and clinics.
https://census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2008/demo/namibia08.pdf; https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hiv-aids; accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-3.3.03-R1
Site is the land a settlement is built on and situation its position relative to surrounding places and routes. Namibian functions: Windhoek is the capital and administrative centre; Walvis Bay has the country's only natural deep-water harbour and a large fishing industry; Oranjemund (formal settlement 1936, opened to the public in 2017) is a diamond-mining town; Swakopmund is a beach resort. Kolmanskop grew after diamonds were found in 1908 and was abandoned in 1956 after richer deposits were found near the Orange River.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walvis_Bay; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oranjemund; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swakopmund; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmanskop; accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-3.3.03-R2
Settlements grow through natural increase and in-migration attracted by their functions; rural-urban migration combines rural push factors (drought, few jobs, poor services) with urban pull factors (jobs, schools, hospitals, services); settlements decline when their main function ends.
Standard school model for syllabus objective 3.3.03 (factors influencing size, growth and function); Kolmanskop decline from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmanskop, accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-3.3.04-R1
MEDC residential structure is often taught with the concentric zone model (Ernest Burgess, Chicago, 1925): CBD, zone of transition, working-class inner suburbs, middle-class outer suburbs, commuter zone, with wealthier people farther out. A generalised LEDC model reverses this: wealthy housing near the CBD or along a main road, poorer housing and informal settlements on the urban fringe. Both are generalisations with exceptions.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric_zone_model (accessed 2026-10-30); LEDC pattern is the standard generalised school model for syllabus objective 3.3.04

CL-6137-3.3.04-R2
In Windhoek, black residents were forcibly moved from the Old Location to Katutura, about 8 km north of the centre, which was created in 1961; Havana, one of the poorest informal settlements, lies on the north-western edge, with few shared toilets and limited water and legal electricity.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katutura; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana_(Windhoek_suburb); accessed 2026-10-30

CL-6137-3.3.05-R1
CBD traffic congestion results from converging roads, many people travelling in at the same times, narrow old streets, delivery vehicles, roadside parking and limited public transport; responses include ring roads or bypasses (divert through-traffic only), park-and-ride, bus lanes, one-way streets, pedestrianisation and vehicle restrictions.
Standard school model for syllabus objective 3.3.05 (congestion in the CBD and traffic congestion; suggest solutions)

CL-6137-3.3.05-R2
Informal settlements grow where migrants arrive faster than affordable serviced housing is provided; problems include missing or shared toilets and taps, illegal electricity connections, fire and flood risk, disease and insecure tenure; responses include site-and-service schemes, self-help housing and in-place upgrading.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana_(Windhoek_suburb) (shared toilets, water scarcity, illegal electricity), accessed 2026-10-30; standard school model for syllabus objective 3.3.05

Scale is shown as a representative fraction (e.g. 1:50 000), a linear scale (scale bar) or a statement (word) scale (e.g. 1 cm represents 0.5 km); at 1:50 000, 1 cm = 50 000 cm = 500 m = 0.5 km, and only the scale bar stays correct when a map is resized.

CL-6137-4.2.04-R1
Contour patterns identify landforms: valley contours form Vs pointing towards higher values (upstream), spur contours form Vs pointing towards lower values; a plateau has widely spaced or no contours on top and close contours at its edges; an escarpment is a band of closely spaced contours forming a long steep slope; a ridge is long narrow high ground; a saddle is a dip between two hills; a gorge has very close contours on both sides of a deep valley; an inselberg is an isolated steep-sided hill on a plain. A point on a contour line has that line's height.
Standard map-skills content for syllabus objective 4.2.04 (identify landforms using contours and symbols)

Standard map-skills content for syllabus objective 4.2.05 (human activities in relation to map features)
