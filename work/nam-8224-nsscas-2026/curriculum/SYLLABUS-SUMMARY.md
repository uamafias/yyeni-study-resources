# NSSCAS Chemistry 8224 — syllabus summary (for verification)

Source: NIED syllabus, updated 20 November 2020. Extracted text read for: front matter, the content outline and sub-topic tables, section 6–10 (assessment), Annexe A Paper 3 introduction, Annexes C and D, Annexe E opening. The specific-objective wording inside each content table was NOT read line by line yet; it will be read per unit when a pilot unit is chosen. Drafted 2026-10-18, **not yet reviewed**.

## Qualification
- One-year course, about 180 guided learning hours, grades a–e. NSSCO Chemistry is recommended prior learning, not a stated entry requirement.
- **Paper 1:** 40 multiple-choice items, 1 h, 40 marks (AO A and B).
- **Paper 2:** compulsory short-answer, structured and free-response, 1 h 15, 60 marks (AO A and B).
- **Paper 3:** advanced practical, 2 h, 40 marks (AO C only): two or three experiments, learners collect, record and analyse data on the question paper. Cards cannot test hands-on manipulation; they can test planning, readings, data handling, conclusions and evaluation.
- **Data booklet (Annexe E)** is made available for Papers 1 and 2. **Qualitative analysis notes (Annexe D)** are printed with Paper 3. (Both differ from NSSCO, where the notes were not supplied.) Flashcard design policy (ours, not a syllabus rule): do not make recall of supplied data a card's only demand, though recalling supplied facts can still be useful for fluency; confirm what the data booklet contains before writing numeric-data cards.
- AO A knowledge with understanding (not more than 20% recall); AO B handling information, application and solving problems; AO C practical. The syllabus says "60 per cent for skills" against 40 per cent for knowledge.
- The command-word glossary (section 10) is a guide, "not exhaustive".

## Structure: 4 themes → 20 topics → 52 teachable units (syllabus sub-topics; 1.4 and 4.5 have no sub-topics)
**Theme 1 The particulate nature of matter and stoichiometry (15 units):** 1.1 Atoms, molecules and stoichiometry (1.1.1 relative masses; 1.1.2 mole and Avogadro; 1.1.3 mass spectra; 1.1.4 empirical and molecular formulae; 1.1.5 reacting masses and equations) · 1.2 Atomic structure (1.2.1 particles; 1.2.2 nucleus; 1.2.3 electrons, energy levels and orbitals) · 1.3 Chemical bonding (1.3.1 ionic; 1.3.2 covalent and dative, shapes; 1.3.3 electronegativity; 1.3.4 intermolecular forces, electronegativity and bond properties; 1.3.5 metallic; 1.3.6 bonding, structure and physical properties) · 1.4 The ideal gas and real gases, pV = nRT.
**Theme 2 Physical chemistry (9):** 2.1 Chemical energetics (2.1.1 enthalpy change; 2.1.2 Hess's law) · 2.2 Electrochemistry (2.2.1 redox, oxidation numbers; 2.2.2 electrolysis) · 2.3 Equilibria (2.3.1 chemical; 2.3.2 ionic) · 2.4 Reaction kinetics (2.4.1 rate; 2.4.2 temperature and activation energy; 2.4.3 catalysts).
**Theme 3 Inorganic chemistry (15):** 3.1 Periodic table and periodicity (3.1.1 physical periodicity; 3.1.2 trends down a group; 3.1.3 chemical periodicity Period 3; 3.1.4 other elements) · 3.2 Group 2 (3.2.1 trends; 3.2.2 uses) · 3.3 Transition elements (3.3.1 characteristics; 3.3.2 extraction: copper, manganese and uranium produced in Namibia) · 3.4 Group 17 (3.4.1–3.4.5) · 3.5 Nitrogen and Sulfur (3.5.1, 3.5.2).
**Theme 4 Organic chemistry and analysis (13):** 4.1 Foundations (4.1.1 terminology; 4.1.2 shapes; 4.1.3 infra-red spectroscopy) · 4.2 Hydrocarbons (4.2.1 alkanes; 4.2.2 alkenes; 4.2.3 fuels) · 4.3 Halogen derivatives (4.3.1; 4.3.2) · 4.4 Hydroxyl compounds (4.4.1) · 4.5 Isomerism · 4.6 Carbonyl compounds (4.6.1) · 4.7 Carboxylic acids and derivatives (4.7.1; 4.7.2 esters and amines).
Plus a "Mathematical requirements" section (incl. logarithms for pH, rate-equation models, gradients, significant figures).

## Boundaries stated in the syllabus (cards must respect these)
- 1.1.3: knowledge of how the mass spectrometer works is not required. 1.3.4: knowledge of hybridisation is not required.
- 3.1.1: electrons in orbitals of sub-shells (for ionisation-energy detail) not expected there (see 1.2.3).
- 4.1.3: functional groups needed for infra-red are those in the Data Booklet. 4.5: E/Z nomenclature acceptable but not required; diastereoisomers not required.
- Paper 3: systematic qualitative analysis and traditional separation methods not required. Learners are assumed familiar with reactions of cations NH4+, Mg2+, Al3+, Ca2+, Cr3+, Mn2+, Fe2+, Fe3+, Cu2+, Zn2+, Ba2+; anions CO3 2-, NO3-, NO2-, SO4 2-, SO3 2-, Cl-, Br-, I-; gas tests for NH3, CO2, Cl2, H2, O2. Unknown ions: general conclusions only. Burette readings to the nearest 0.05 cm3.
- Annexe C: organic structural formula conventions (condensed, displayed, skeletal) that cards must follow.

## Problems in the syllabus text itself
1. **Paper weighting conflicts.** Prose and table: Paper 1 31%, Paper 2 46%, Paper 3 23% (77/23). Marks: 40/60/40 of 140 = 28.6% / 42.9% / 28.6%.
2. **AO weighting conflicts with the grid's marks.** Printed 40% A, 40% B, 20% C; the grid's marks give A 50, B 50, C 40 of 140 = 35.7% / 35.7% / 28.6%. (Same pattern as 8223 Biology.)
