## ITEM-8224-3.2-P01

6 marks · Bloom: Apply

Identify one correct option per question. [6]
1. Group 2 melting points are: A strictly increasing; B irregular; C identical; D zero at room temperature.
2. Calcium with dilute HCl produces: A CO2; B O2; C H2; D NO2.
3. Calcium oxide with dilute HCl produces: A salt and water; B calcium metal; C hydrogen only; D carbonate only.
4. Heating CaCO3 sufficiently gives: A Ca and O2; B Ca(OH)2 and H2; C Ca and CO; D CaO and CO2.
5. In 2M(NO3)2 → 2MO + 4NO2 + O2, NO2:O2 amount ratio is: A 1:4; B 4:1; C 2:1; D 1:1.
6. The main reason a lower Group 2 metal generally loses an outer electron more readily is: A lower nuclear charge; B fewer occupied shells; C greater distance and shielding; D fewer neutrons.



### Worked answer

Key: 1 B, 2 C, 3 A, 4 D, 5 B, 6 C; one mark per correct option.
1. B retains the actual pattern. A imposes a one-way rise; C ignores different structures; D confuses melting temperature with ambient temperature.
2. C follows metal–acid reduction of H+. A is a carbonate product; B is not the metal–HCl gas; D relates to nitrate chemistry.
3. A is basic-oxide neutralisation. B would require reduction; C confuses oxide with metal; D introduces carbon absent from the reactants.
4. D is carbonate decomposition. A breaks the oxide into elements without support; B introduces hydrogen; C predicts the wrong carbon oxide and metal state.
5. B follows the coefficients. A reverses them; C borrows the oxide coefficient; D ignores the four.
6. C explains weaker effective attraction. A is false down the group; B reverses shell growth; D does not explain electron attraction.

Required answer parts: key, correct option, distractor, error. Use the specific chemically correct points in the canonical answer; award no additional credit for repetition.



## ITEM-8224-3.2-P02

8 marks · Bloom: Apply

Explain Group 2 behaviour using the linked parts. [8]
(a) State whether a measured rise from magnesium to calcium contradicts a claim of strictly decreasing melting points. [1]
(b) Explain why calcium generally reacts with cold water more vigorously than magnesium. [2]
(c) Write the equation for calcium oxide neutralising dilute hydrochloric acid. Include states. [2]
(d) State the products of heating a Group 2 nitrate sufficiently. [1]
(e) Explain why a carbonate can become more thermally stable down the group while the metals react more readily with water. [2]



### Worked answer

Part (a): yes; the observed rise disproves a strictly decreasing pattern [1].
Part (b): increased distance and shielding [1] reduce effective attraction and make outer-electron loss easier [1].
Part (c): CaO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + H2O(l). Correct products/states [1]; balance [1].
Part (d): metal oxide, NO2 and O2 [1].
Part (e): metal–water reactivity concerns electron loss from atoms [1]; carbonate stability concerns the cation–anion compound and reduced anion distortion by larger cations [1]. Each bracket is a separate creditworthy point.

## ITEM-8224-3.2-P03

5 marks · Bloom: Apply

Calculate and interpret the following original Group 2 data. Use molar masses CaCO3 = 100 and CaO = 56.0 g mol⁻¹. [5]
(a) A 10.0 g sample of pure CaCO3 is heated completely. Calculate the mass of CaO formed, giving three significant figures. [2]
(b) Calculate H+ needed to neutralise all that CaO. [1]
(c) A separate 0.100 mol calcium-metal sample reacts completely with excess dilute HCl. Calculate moles of hydrogen formed. [1]
(d) Supplied melting temperatures are magnesium 650 °C and calcium 842 °C. State what this comparison shows about a strictly decreasing down-group melting pattern. [1]



### Worked answer

(a) Equations: CaCO3 → CaO + CO2 and n = m/M. Working n(CaCO3) = 10.0/100 = 0.100 mol, so n(CaO) = 0.100 mol [1]; mass = 0.100 × 56.0 = 5.60 g to three significant figures [1]. Molar masses are supplied at their intended school precision and no unit conversion is needed.
(b) CaO + 2H+ → Ca2+ + H2O; n(H+) = 2 × 0.100 = 0.200 mol [1].
(c) Ca + 2H+ → Ca2+ + H2; n(H2) = 0.100 mol [1].
(d) Calcium’s higher value shows a local rise, so the pattern is not strictly decreasing [1].
