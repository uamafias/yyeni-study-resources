# 1.1 Atoms, molecules and stoichiometry — practice tasks and worked answers



## ITEM-8224-1.1-P01

6 marks · Bloom: Apply

Six questions. Choose one option for each.

1. Which term is correct for MgO?
A relative isotopic mass
B relative atomic mass
C relative molecular mass
D relative formula mass

2. One mole contains:
A 6.02 × 10²³ g
B 6.02 × 10²³ specified particles
C one particle
D Mr particles

3. Peaks at mass 35 and 37 have intensities 3:1. Which isotope is more abundant?
A mass 35
B mass 37
C equal abundance
D cannot be determined

4. What is the empirical formula of C2H6?
A CH3
B C2H6
C CH2
D C3H

5. Which change balances H2 + O2 → H2O?
A change H2O to H2O2
B 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
C H2 + O → H2O
D H2 + 2O2 → H2O

6. What volume does 0.50 mol gas occupy at r.t.p.?
A 11.2 dm³
B 12.0 dm³
C 22.4 dm³
D 24.0 dm³



### Worked answer

Key: 1 D, 2 B, 3 A, 4 A, 5 B, 6 B.
1 D is correct because MgO is a formula unit; C wrongly treats it as a molecule, B concerns one element, and A one isotope.
2 B is the definition; A attaches a mass unit to a count, C ignores the Avogadro constant, and D confuses relative mass with particle number.
3 A follows the 3:1 intensity; B reverses it, C ignores the different heights, and D overlooks the supplied data.
4 A simplifies 2:6 to 1:3; B is molecular rather than empirical, and C and D use wrong ratios.
5 B conserves H and O using coefficients; A changes a formula, C changes elemental oxygen, and D is unbalanced.
6 B uses 0.50 × 24.0; A uses the s.t.p. value, while C and D are one-mole volumes.



### Marking guidance

Required task parts: key, correct option, distractor, error. Award marks part by part for chemically correct, creditworthy work; the total is 6.



## ITEM-8224-1.1-P02

8 marks · Bloom: Apply

A compound contains 24.0 g carbon and 4.00 g hydrogen and has Mr = 56.
(a) Define empirical formula. [1]
(b) Calculate the amount of carbon and hydrogen atoms represented by these masses. Use Ar(C) = 12.0 and Ar(H) = 1.00. [2]
(c) Determine the empirical formula. [2]
(d) Determine the molecular formula. [2]
(e) State the distinction between relative molecular mass and relative formula mass. [1]



### Worked answer

(a) The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
(b) n(C) = 24.0 ÷ 12.0 = 2.00 mol; n(H) = 4.00 ÷ 1.00 = 4.00 mol.
(c) Divide by 2.00 to give C:H = 1:2, so CH2.
(d) Empirical-formula mass = 14; 56 ÷ 14 = 4, so C4H8.
(e) Relative molecular mass sums Ar values in a molecule; relative formula mass makes the corresponding sum for a formula unit.

Required task parts: part, mark, creditworthy point. Award marks part by part for chemically correct, creditworthy work; the total is 8.



## ITEM-8224-1.1-P03

5 marks · Bloom: Apply

Hydrogen reacts with oxygen: 2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l). A mixture contains 3.00 dm³ H2 and 2.00 dm³ O2 at r.t.p. Calculate the mass of water formed. Use Vm = 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ and M(H2O) = 18.0 g mol⁻¹.



### Worked answer

Equation: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. Working: n(H2) = 3.00 ÷ 24.0 = 0.125 mol; n(O2) = 2.00 ÷ 24.0 = 0.083333 mol. The equation needs 0.0625 mol O2 for 0.125 mol H2, so H2 is limiting. Therefore n(H2O) = 0.125 mol. Answer: mass = 0.125 × 18.0 = 2.25 g, with unit g and three significant figures.



### Marking guidance

Required task parts: equation, working, answer, unit, significant figures. Award marks part by part for chemically correct, creditworthy work; the total is 5.
