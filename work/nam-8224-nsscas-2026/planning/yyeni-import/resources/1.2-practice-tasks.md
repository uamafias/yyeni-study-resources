# 1.2 Atomic structure — practice tasks and worked answers



## ITEM-8224-1.2-P01

8 marks · Bloom: Apply

Identify the correct option in each of these eight questions. Each question has one correct option. [8]

1. What is the maximum electron count in one orbital?
A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 6

2. How many orbitals make up a d subshell?
A. 2
B. 3
C. 5
D. 10

3. Which pair gives the orbital count and electron capacity of a p subshell?
A. 3 and 6
B. 6 and 3
C. 1 and 2
D. 5 and 10

4. Which box shows an allowed two-electron occupancy of one orbital?
A. [↑↑]
B. [↑↑↓]
C. [↑]
D. [↑↓]

5. A p⁴ set is [↑↓] [↑] [↑]. How many orbitals are occupied?
A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4

6. A filled s subshell and a filled p subshell in one shell contain how many occupied orbitals in total?
A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8

7. Five d orbitals each contain one electron. How many more electrons can this subshell accommodate?
A. 0
B. 2
C. 5
D. 10

8. Which statement about a subshell containing three p electrons follows from the capacity rule?
A. Each orbital must hold three electrons
B. The subshell has six orbitals
C. The subshell cannot accept another electron
D. The subshell can accommodate up to three further electrons



### Worked answer

Key: 1 B, 2 C, 3 A, 4 D, 5 C, 6 B, 7 C, 8 D.

1. B: one orbital accommodates two electrons. A confuses single occupancy with capacity; C counts p orbitals; D gives p-subshell capacity.
2. C: a d subshell has five orbitals. A is one-orbital electron capacity; B counts p orbitals; D is d electron capacity.
3. A is correct. B reverses the quantities; C describes s; D describes d.
4. D has two opposite spins. A shows equal spins in one box; B exceeds capacity; C is allowed but contains only one electron.
5. C: all three boxes contain electrons. A counts paired boxes; B counts singly occupied boxes; D counts electrons.
6. B: 1 + 3 = 4 orbitals. A counts subshells; C is the p electron capacity; D is the combined electron count.
7. C: five further electrons can pair, bringing the total to ten. A treats single occupation as full; B is one-orbital capacity; D forgets the five electrons present.
8. D: capacity six minus occupancy three leaves three spaces. A exceeds orbital capacity; B doubles the orbital count; C confuses present occupancy with full capacity.



### Marking guidance

Key: one mark for each correct option. Correct option and every distractor error are explained in the canonical answer; a distractor has no partial credit.



## ITEM-8224-1.2-P02

8 marks · Bloom: Apply

A species has A = 34, Z = 16 and charge 2−. Explain your reasoning in the following linked parts.
(a) State its proton, neutron and electron counts. [3]
(b) Explain why most of its mass is in the nucleus. [2]
(c) State its electron configuration and explain the occupancy of its outer p subshell. [3]



### Worked answer

(a) 16 protons, 18 neutrons and 18 electrons. n = 34 − 16; electrons = 16 + 2. (b) Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus and each is much heavier than an electron, so the nuclear particles supply almost all the mass. (c) 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶. The outer p subshell has three orbitals; each contains a pair of opposite-spin electrons.



### Marking guidance

Part (a): one mark for each correct particle count. Part (b): one mark for nuclear protons/neutrons and one for their much greater mass than electrons. Part (c): one mark for configuration, one for three p orbitals and one for paired opposite spins. Each mark corresponds to a distinct creditworthy point.



## ITEM-8224-1.2-P03

5 marks · Bloom: Apply

Calculate the following quantities for a neutral atom with A = 24 and Z = 12. Use approximate relative masses: proton 1, neutron 1, electron 1/1836.
(a) Calculate its neutron count and electron count. [2]
(b) Calculate the approximate percentage of the particle-sum mass located in its nucleus. [2]
(c) For an electron and proton entering the same uniform electric field at equal speeds, a simple force model gives equal force magnitudes. Calculate their electron-to-proton acceleration ratio, using acceleration = force/mass. [1]



### Worked answer

(a) Neutrons = A − Z = 24 − 12 = 12; electrons = Z = 12. These counts are exact integers. (b) Nucleus relative mass = 12 + 12 = 24. Electron contribution = 12/1836 = 0.00653595. Fraction in nucleus = 24/(24 + 0.00653595) = 0.9997277. Percentage = 99.97277%, or approximately 99.97%. This is an approximate particle-sum model, not a measured isotope mass. (c) a(electron)/a(proton) = m(proton)/m(electron) = 1/(1/1836) = 1836, approximately 1.84 × 10³ to three significant figures. The ratio is dimensionless. The lighter electron bends more strongly; its negative charge determines the opposite direction.



### Marking guidance

Equation and working: (a) n=A−Z and electron count, one mark each; (b) particle-sum expression with electron mass included, one mark, and approximately 99.97%, one mark; (c) acceleration ratio 1836, one mark. Answer and unit: counts and ratios have no physical unit, percentage uses %. Significant figures: counts are exact; mass-sum percentage is approximate and the acceleration ratio may be rounded to three significant figures. No unit conversion is needed because all masses are already relative values.
