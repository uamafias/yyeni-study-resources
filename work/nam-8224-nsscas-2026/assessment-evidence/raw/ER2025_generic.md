# NSSCAS 8224 Chemistry, 2025 Examiners Report: general comments and advice (paraphrased)

## Paper 1 overall (p. 71)
- Easier items: Q2, 6, 7, 11, 19, 25, 26, 31. Particularly difficult: Q10, 12, 15, 17, 22, 24, 28, 29, 36, 37. The report says these will be looked at in more detail. (p. 71)

## Paper 2 general (p. 74)
### Overall performance
- Candidates are sound on fundamentals and straightforward recall but inconsistent when applying knowledge to unfamiliar contexts; higher-order tasks such as justifying the Contact process temperature and applying the mole concept were weak. (p. 74)
- Strong: atomic structure, bonding, core organic chemistry (a marked improvement, especially optical isomerism and infra-red). Weak: physical chemistry, especially mass-spectrum analysis, and introductory inorganic chemistry such as Period 3 trends. (p. 74)
### Command words and reading the question
- Read command words and the stem closely; underline key terms. Example: Question 1 asked for ions yet some gave neutral atoms or molecules. Answer only what is asked, with no extra detail. (p. 74)
### Language, terminology and definitions
- Technical terms were often misused, notably in defining ionisation energy, describing metallic bonding variation across Period 3 and maximum oxidation numbers. Definitions and trends must be learnt precisely; dropping or swapping one key word can cost the mark. (p. 74)
- Keep answers to simple questions short and precise; longer answers with surplus detail sometimes contradicted themselves. (p. 74)
- Organic nomenclature needs attention (hyphens, systematic names across homologous series). (p. 74, p. 79)
### Significant figures and calculations
- Show clear step-by-step working and give final answers to the specified accuracy; most candidates lost marks in 1(b)(ii) (four s.f.) and 3(b) (three s.f.). (p. 74)
### Practical skills
- Teachers should give learners experience of common organic reactions, including the iodoform reaction, and the practical aspects of the syllabus, which helps make stoichiometry and organic chemistry concrete. (p. 74)
### Other
- Organic: teachers should stress classification of alcohols and the reagents and conditions for organic reactions. (p. 74)
- Write only in the answer spaces; label any overflow clearly. Where boxes are given for structures, only the final answer should be in the box, since several structures leave examiners unsure which counts. (p. 74)

## Paper 3 general (p. 81)
### Significant figures
- Quote numerical answers to a number of significant figures that fits the data and the errors inherent in the method. (p. 81; reinforced in comments on 1(b) and 2(c), pp. 81-83)
### Practical skills
- Learners should discuss errors and possible improvements in practical methods during the course. (p. 81)
- Errors must be specific to the experiment with improvements based on the apparatus; parallax and generic human error are not acceptable. (p. 82, p. 83)
- Read and follow the general instructions at the start of the Quantitative and Qualitative Analysis sections; for 'all observations' give several, test for gases, and state 'soluble' or 'not soluble in excess' using the qualitative analysis notes. (p. 81, p. 84)
- Use Ar values with decimals from the AS periodic table (e.g. 24.3 for Mg), not the rounded NSSCO values. (p. 81)
- Burette readings go to 0.00 or 0.05 cm3; mean titre is rounded to 0.01 cm3. (p. 83, p. 84)
### Other (centres)
- Script packets should include, for each session and laboratory, a list of learners present with a seating plan and a copy of the paper with the Supervisor's experimental result. (p. 81)
- Learners had some trouble setting up the Question 1 experiment, which took time. (p. 81)
