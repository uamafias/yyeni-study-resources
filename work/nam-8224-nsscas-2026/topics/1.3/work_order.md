## Content units

**CU-8224-1.3.1** — sub-topic 1.3.1, 4 objectives, 1067–1529 words
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.1-01` tier 2 — describe ionic bonding, as in sodium chloride, magnesium oxide and calcium fluoride, including the use of „dot-and-cross‟ diagrams
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.1-02` tier 2 — describe the electrostatic forces in ionic compounds as being attraction between oppositely charged ions
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.1-03` tier 3 — describe, interpret and predict the effect of ionic bonding on physical properties (including physical state, melting point, boiling point, solubility)
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.1-04` tier 2 — explain the electrical conductivity of ionic compounds when solid, molten or in solution

**CU-8224-1.3.2** — sub-topic 1.3.2, 4 objectives, 1067–1529 words
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.2-01` tier 2 — describe, including the use of „dot-and-cross‟ diagrams: - covalent bonding, including molecules such as hydrogen, chlorine, oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen chloride, carbon dioxide, methane, ethene - dative covalent (co-ordinate) bonding, including in the formation of the ammonium ion and in the Al2Cl6 molecule, CO, NH4+, H3O+
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.2-02` tier 2 — explain the shapes of, and bond angles in, molecules by using the - qualitative model of electron-pair repulsion (including lone pairs), using as simple examples: BF3 (trigonal planar), CO2 (linear), CH4 (tetrahedral), NH3 (pyramidal), H2O (non-linear/v-shaped), SF6 (octahedral), PF5 (trigonal bipyramidal)
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.2-03` tier 2 — use the electron-pair repulsion theory to predict the shapes of, and bond angles in other molecules analogous to those specified in bullet 2 above (see section 4.1.2)
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.2-04` tier 3 — describe, interpret and predict the effect of covalent bonding on physical properties (including physical state, melting point, boiling point, solubility)

**CU-8224-1.3.3** — sub-topic 1.3.3, 2 objectives, 533–764 words
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.3-01` tier 2 — explain the concept of electronegativity
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.3-02` tier 2 — explain, in terms of electronegativity, the properties of molecules such as bond polarity the dipole moments of molecules and the behaviour of oxides with water

**CU-8224-1.3.4** — sub-topic 1.3.4, 9 objectives, 2401–3441 words
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-01` tier 2 — describe the differences between bonding (intramolecular) and intermolecular forces in covalent molecules
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-02` tier 2 — describe that the electrostatic forces in ionic compounds are much stronger than intermolecular forces in covalent molecules
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-03` tier 2 — describe that the bonding (intramolecular ) forces are much stronger than intermolecular forces in covalent bonding
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-04` tier 2 — describe the difference in volatility, solubility, melting/boiling points and electrical conductivity between ionic and covalent compounds in terms of forces of attraction (knowledge of hybridisation is not required)
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-05` tier 2 — describe intermolecular forces in covalent compounds and noble gases: induced dipole (van der Waals) forces, permanent dipole forces and hydrogen bonding
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-06` tier 2 — explain differences in physical properties in terms of intermolecular forces, including: - noble gases, Br2(l), - HCl(g), CHCl3(l), - ammonia and water as simple examples of molecules containing N–H and O–H groups
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-07` tier 1 — state that when giant covalent substances melt, covalent bonds break and when simple molecular substances melt or boil, forces between molecules weaken
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-08` tier 2 — describe hydrogen bonds as the strongest intermolecular force yet still weak compared with covalent bonding
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.4-09` tier 2 — explain the effect of hydrogen bonding on the physical properties of substances, including ice, water, methane, ammonia, hydrogen fluoride and methanol (for example, boiling and melting points, viscosity and surface tension)

**CU-8224-1.3.5** — sub-topic 1.3.5, 2 objectives, 533–764 words
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.5-01` tier 2 — describe metallic bonding in terms of a lattice of positive ions surrounded by delocalised electrons
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.5-02` tier 2 — describe and explain the electrical conductivity, melting point and malleability/ ductility of metals in terms of their structure and bonding

**CU-8224-1.3.6** — sub-topic 1.3.6, 4 objectives, 1067–1529 words
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.6-01` tier 2 — describe, in simple terms, the lattice structure of a crystalline solid which is: - ionic, including sodium chloride, magnesium oxide - simple molecular, including iodine and the fullerene allotropes of carbon (C60 and nanotubes only) - giant molecular, including silicon(IV) oxide and the graphite, diamond and graphene allotropes of carbon g - hydrogen-bonded, including ice of - metallic, including copper
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.6-02` tier 2 — explain the relative energy and temperature required for changes of state in terms of the relative energy needed to break bonds (with reference to ionic, giant covalent and metallic substances) and to weaken intermolecular forces (with reference to simple covalent structures)
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.6-03` tier 2 — describe, interpret and predict the effect of different types of structure and bonding ionic bonding, covalent bonding (giant and simple structures), hydrogen bonding, other intermolecular interactions, metallic bonding on the physical properties of substances, including electrical conductivity
- `OBJ-8224-1.3.6-04` tier 3 — deduce the type of bonding and structure present from given information
