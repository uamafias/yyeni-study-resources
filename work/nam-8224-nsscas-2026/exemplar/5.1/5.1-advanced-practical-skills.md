**What this topic asks of you**

- A titration record must preserve the measurements from which the result is calculated. Record initial and final burette readings, then calculate the titre by subtraction. Use a single table with quantity-and-unit headings. Burette readings are written consistently to two decimal places because the scale is read to the nearest 0.05 cm³. A rough titre helps locate the endpoint but is not used in the mean.

---

## Titration recording and calculation

### Preparing the apparatus

Rinse the pipette with the solution it will deliver and the burette with the solution it will contain. Residual water would otherwise dilute a measured solution. Fill the burette, remove any air bubble from the jet and read the meniscus at eye level. Transfer the pipetted aliquot to a conical flask, add only a few drops of a suitable indicator, then add titrant while swirling. Near the endpoint, add titrant dropwise.

### Concordance and the mean

Suppose accurate titres are 24.35 cm³, 24.40 cm³ and 24.70 cm³. The first two differ by 0.05 cm³ and are concordant; the third is not. Mean titre = (24.35 + 24.40) ÷ 2 = 24.375 cm³, reported as 24.38 cm³ to match burette precision. The rough titre, if present, is excluded even when it lies close to the accurate values.

### From titre to concentration

For a 1:1 reaction, 25.00 cm³ of solution X reacts with an unrounded mean titre of 24.375 cm³ of 0.1000 mol dm⁻³ solution Y. Carry the unrounded mean through the calculation. Convert the titre: 24.375 cm³ = 0.024375 dm³. Moles Y = cV = 0.1000 × 0.024375 = 0.0024375 mol. The 1:1 ratio gives 0.0024375 mol X in 0.02500 dm³. Concentration X = 0.0024375 ÷ 0.02500 = 0.09750 mol dm⁻³ (four significant figures, matching the supplied concentration and pipetted volume).

### Specific evaluation

An improvement must connect cause and effect. Repeating until concordant titres are obtained reduces the influence of random endpoint variation on the mean. Reading the meniscus at eye level reduces parallax in both burette readings. Replacing a measuring cylinder with a volumetric pipette improves the delivered aliquot because the pipette has a smaller uncertainty for that fixed volume. Saying only 'be more careful' does not identify a measurement or a corrective action.

### Quality check before calculation

Before using the mean, inspect the raw table. Confirm that final readings exceed initial readings, titres are calculated by subtraction, all readings use consistent decimal places and the selected accurate titres satisfy the concordance rule. Then carry the unrounded mean into the stoichiometric calculation. This separates recording errors from chemical reasoning and makes an anomalous run visible rather than hiding it inside an average.
