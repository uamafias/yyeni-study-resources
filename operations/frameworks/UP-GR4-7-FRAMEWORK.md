# Upper primary (grades 4–7): resource-generation framework

Draft, 31 October 2026 (Claude, for Vitalis's approval). This is how to build notes and flashcards for the Senior Primary Phase, where there is no external examination and so no past papers or examiner reports. The aim is for evidence to come from the syllabus, teachers and learners instead. Evidence: `GR4-9-SOURCE-MATRIX.md`.

## The idea in one paragraph

For grades 10–12 we let the exams tell us what matters: tariffs, command words and where candidates lose marks. Grades 4–7 have no external exam: learners are assessed through continuous assessment and a school examination, which the syllabus describes in detail. So:
- the **syllabus is both the content and the assessment evidence**: its grade-by-grade competencies, specification grid and continuous-assessment tasks;
- the **quality evidence that exams gave us comes from people instead**: a Namibian senior primary teacher's review, and a small learner trial on YYeni;
- the **cards are designed for children**: short, concrete, one idea each, language matched to the grade, and often visual.

## What stays the same

- **Pipeline:** claim, sources, syllabus first, analysis, plan, author, cross-vendor review, publish. The YOU checkpoints are the same.
- **Rules:**
  - `syllabus-facts.json` with a page and a verbatim line for every value;
  - an objective registry and contracts generated by the tools;
  - Bloom and AO on every card;
  - the visual-resource pipeline with independent acceptance;
  - "mine, do not reproduce", which now applies to revision notes and textbooks;
  - a guarded publication build.
- **Naming** (playbook §6): workspace `nam-<subject>_up-gr4-7-2026`, for example `nam-social_studies_up-gr4-7-2026`; stem `<subject>_up-gr4-7`.

## What changes

### Step 1: sources

1. The SP syllabus. This is required; nothing starts without it.
2. Revision notes for each grade. They are unofficial: used for a coverage cross-check, never copied, never cited as authority.
3. Any official teacher's guide, or NIED sample assessment tasks, if they exist (Harvester).
4. Gaps known now: there is no senior primary English syllabus in the repo, and it is unclear whether Environmental Studies is still taught.

### Step 2: syllabus facts

- **Grade tag.** Every objective and competency gets a grade tag (4, 5, 6 or 7), taken from the syllabus's grade-by-grade learning content.
- **Assessment.** Transcribe the assessment section:
  - the continuous-assessment components per grade or semester, for example investigations, topic tasks and topic tests;
  - the continuous-assessment/examination weighting by grade. In SP Mathematics, continuous assessment is 80 % in Grade 4 and 65 % in Grade 5, and no end-of-term examinations are allowed;
  - the school examination papers;
  - both specification grids (written examination and continuous assessment).
- **Language instruction.** Transcribe the syllabus's language instruction verbatim. For example, SP Mathematics: "For Grade 4 & 5 all vocabulary and terminology should be read, pronounced and written in both language of instruction and English."

### Step 3: assessment analysis (no corpus)

This uses the playbook's syllabus-only step 3, written to `operations/analysis/<stem>-corpus-analysis.md` so that the tools find it. Its sections:

1. **The evidence:** says plainly that there are no past papers; every objective is weighted by the syllabus alone.
2. **What assessment looks like** at each grade: the continuous-assessment task types and the examination papers the syllabus describes.
3. **Task verbs instead of command words.** The verbs in the competencies ("identify", "draw", "count", "explain") become the allowed card verbs, by grade.
4. **What a good answer looks like**, from the syllabus's grade descriptors (A–E). Tariffs are kept small: 1–2 marks, at most 3 in Grade 7.
5. **Emphasis:** from the specification grid and the number of competencies per topic. This never removes coverage.
6. **Learner difficulties:** from the syllabus's "learning support" notes, the teacher reviewer and, after the trial, real YYeni results. Each is labelled with its source. None is invented or labelled as evidenced.
7. **Scope limits** stated by the syllabus.
8. **What this means for the plan.**

### Step 4: plan

**Card design rules.** These are set in `subject-profile.yaml` and checked. The numbers are pilot defaults, to be calibrated with the teacher reviewer.

| Rule | Grades 4–5 | Grades 6–7 |
|---|---|---|
| Ideas per card | one | one |
| Prompt length | ≤ 15 words | ≤ 20 words |
| Answer length | ≤ 20 words | ≤ 35 words |
| Mean sentence length (cards and notes) | ≤ 10 words | ≤ 14 words |
| Bloom levels | Remember, Understand, Apply | add some Analyse in Grade 7 |
| Pictures | expected on most concept cards | where they help |
| Contexts | everyday Namibian settings (home, school, village, town, farm); invented names and businesses | the same |
| Vocabulary | the syllabus's subject terms, each defined the first time it appears; otherwise high-frequency words | the same |

**Card types.** Front/back forms suited to children:
- term → meaning;
- picture → name;
- "what comes next" (sequences);
- "which group does it belong to" (classify);
- a one-step problem with its worked answer;
- "true or false? why".

Long structured or 30-mark practice packages are not used.

**Bank size.** Smaller than for grades 8–12, sized from competencies per grade. Expected: 80–200 cards per grade per subject. This is not a target: the pilot calibrates it.

**Notes.** A short learner page per topic per grade (100–300 words), in plain language with pictures. Each page is written for the learner, not the teacher.

**AO.** Items are balanced against the syllabus's written-examination grid, within the same tolerance as for grades 10–12. Continuous-assessment-only objectives (for example practical investigations) are taught in the notes and are not forced into flashcards.

**Language (decision needed, see below).** Grades 6–7: English only. Grades 4–5: English, with an optional home-language field for key terms, as the syllabus's bilingual-terminology instruction implies.

### Step 5: author

Authoring is the same as for grades 10–12, with the readability rules above as hard checks. Authors write at the grade, not about the grade.

### Step 6: review, with three reviewers

1. **Cross-vendor model review**, as now, plus age-appropriateness and readability.
2. **Teacher review (new YOU checkpoint).** A Namibian senior primary teacher of the subject reviews a sample per grade: 10 % of cards, at least 20, and all notes pages for one topic. They check:
   - level;
   - language;
   - accuracy against the syllabus;
   - whether a learner of that grade could answer.

   Their verdict and comments are saved in `review/teacher-review-<grade>.md`.
3. **Learner trial (pilot only, recommended).** One class, or a small group, per grade uses the deck on YYeni for a week. We record which cards most learners miss and why, and that becomes the evidenced learner-difficulty register that grades 4–7 otherwise lack.

### Step 7: publish

Publication is the same, with decks split by grade: `publish/<workspace>/flashcards/grade-4/` … `grade-7/`.

## Standard and check implications (for the standard owner)

**New checks.**
- Readability per grade: sentence length and words per card.
- One-idea-per-card heuristics.
- The picture expectation for grades 4–5.
- A grade tag on every item.

**Checks to relax by profile.**
- Exam-exposure, command-word-tariff and answer-shape checks that need past papers. These should be declared not applicable, not reported as `not_run`.

**Process.** This becomes a change request (`operations/standard-change-requests/CR-007-...`) drafted during the pilot, once we know exactly which checks misfire. Agents never edit `standard/`.

## Pilot

The playbook's pilot rule applies: Vitalis runs the first upper-primary subject.

**Recommendation: Social Studies.**
- It is text-heavy, as most senior primary subjects are (NSHE, Home Ecology, Agriculture), so it tests the hardest part of this framework: language level.
- It has a clear AO grid and notes for all four grades.
- It sits next to our Geography and History experience.

**Alternative: Mathematics.** It has the largest syllabus and is procedural. That makes the cards easier to judge, but it proves less for the other subjects.
