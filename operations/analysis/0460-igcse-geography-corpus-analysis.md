# Corpus analysis — Cambridge IGCSE Geography 0460

What the syllabus and the assessment corpus say about this subject: what the skills are, and what a learner has to do to succeed on each paper. Step one of the pipeline, written after the syllabus (step zero) and before any plan. 29 September 2026. First exam for this cohort: 14 October 2026.

## 0. The syllabus, first

Transcribed from the 0460 syllabus for 2025 and 2026 into `curriculum/syllabus-facts.json`, every value carrying its page. This learner's route is Papers 1, 2 and 4 (Alternative to Coursework).

| AO | name (page 10) | weight | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Paper 4 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AO1 | Knowledge with understanding | 30% | 48% | 11% | 20% |
| AO2 | Skills and analysis | 52% | 30% | 80% | 60% |
| AO3 | Judgement and decision-making | 18% | 22% | 9% | 20% |

- **Paper 1, Geographical Themes.** 1 hour 45 minutes, 75 marks weighted to 100, 45%. Six 25-mark questions in three sections, one section per theme; the candidate answers one question from each section (page 9).
- **Paper 2, Geographical Skills.** 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 27.5%. All questions answered. One question is mapwork on a 1:25 000 or 1:50 000 map with a full key, worth a third of the marks. No place-specific knowledge is needed; knowledge questions draw on the themes (page 23).
- **Paper 4, Alternative to Coursework.** 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 27.5%. Two compulsory questions set on fieldwork enquiries. The criteria (pages 26 and 31) give 12 marks each to knowledge, to collecting data, to presenting it, to analysis, and to conclusion and evaluation.
- **Content is exhaustive except where the syllabus writes "including"** (page 12). For example, topic 2.3 lists every coastal landform to be taught.
- **Case studies are required** in 18 of the 19 topics: 25 in all, listed under each topic. They can come from anywhere in the world, and one place can serve more than one topic (page 12).
- **Command words, page 38:** 16 words.

The syllabus reports no change that affects teaching, and textbooks written for examination from 2016 still fit it (page 43). So the whole window is used.

- **4,182 question parts carrying 11,333 marks.** Paper 1 prints 150 marks per paper, of which the candidate answers 75. 125 of the 126 papers' part tariffs sum exactly. Where a mark scheme's parse fell short and its question paper summed exactly, the question paper's parts and tariffs are used.
- **The command word** is read from the question-paper part, after "Using Fig. 1.1, …" and similar lead-ins are resolved to the verb that follows. A command word or question form was read for 3,993 parts. 492 of them open with a question, not a command word: What (316), Which (86), How (42), Do (32), Why (13).
- **14,698 examiner-report sentences,** of which 11,145 are located to the question part they discuss. 4,559 of them state an error.

## 2. Three papers, three kinds of skill

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Paper 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| what it tests | the themes, with resources and case studies | reading maps, graphs, tables and photographs | the fieldwork enquiry |
| part tariffs | every question 1, 2, 3, 4, 3, 5, 7 | mostly 1-3 (88% of parts) | mostly 1-4 |
| largest share of marks | Describe 31%, Explain 30%, Suggest 12% | Describe 26%, Identify 15%, Suggest 13% | Describe 21%, Suggest 13%, What 11%, Complete 9% |
| what earns marks | ideas from a longer list, developed at 4-5 marks; a levels-marked case study | a correct reading, a feature named from the key, a pattern described | a precise method, a completed graph, a decision backed by data |

**Every Paper 1 question has the same shape.** All 252 questions in the window run 1, 2, 3, 4, 3 and 5 marks, then end with a 7-mark part. The early parts work from a resource: a map, a graph, a photograph, a table. The 7-mark part is the case study. Of the 251 read, 216 ask about "a named example" or "an area you have studied", and 250 are levels-marked.

**Paper 2 is a third mapwork and two thirds data.** The mapwork question carries 33% of Paper 2 marks, almost all in 1-mark parts:

- naming features from the key;
- six-figure grid references;
- compass directions and bearings;
- distances along a route;
- heights from contours;
- a cross-section;
- relief and drainage.

The rest of Paper 2 sets graphs, tables, pyramids and photographs in theme contexts (migration, settlement, coasts, volcanoes, farming). About half those marks also draw on theme knowledge.

**Paper 4 is one enquiry after another.** Each question follows a group of students through a fieldwork enquiry: the hypotheses, the method, a table of their results, a graph to complete, and a conclusion to judge. Urban land use, pedestrian counts and shopping are the most common settings. Rivers, coasts, weather, tourism and population follow. The marks go mainly to:

- conclusions and evaluation (38% of Paper 4 marks);
- methods of collecting data (29%);
- presenting data (15%).

## 4. What a creditable answer looks like

From the mark schemes' own award structure. Structure only; no scheme wording is reproduced.

| command, marks | parts | how it is marked |
|---|---:|---|
| Identify 1, Give 1, State 1, Name 1 | 399 | one correct element |
| Describe 3, Suggest 3 | 400 | one mark per idea, from a list longer than the tariff (53-60%) |
| Explain 4 | 122 | one mark per idea from a longer list (74-77%) |
| Explain 5, Suggest 5 | 179 | ideas from a longer list, and a developed idea earns a further mark (91-98%) |
| Compare 3 | 57 | comparative points; a third keep a mark for paired figures |
| Describe 7, Explain 7 (Paper 1 case study) | 233 | levels (94-100%); the top level needs a named example and place-specific detail (84-89%); some ideas capped, so a range is needed |
| Paper 4 conclusions | 17% of Paper 4 parts | one mark reserved for the decision (true, false, partly), then statements, and a mark for paired data |

**Three things follow for every card.**

- **At 5 marks an idea must be developed to earn its second mark.** "Deforestation causes flooding" is an idea. "… because there is less interception, so rain reaches the river faster" is its development.
- **The case study decides the top of every Paper 1 question.** The levels are:
  - Level 1 is simple statements.
  - Level 2 is developed or linked statements about a named example. Without one the answer is capped at 5.
  - Level 3 adds place-specific detail: named districts, schemes and figures.
- **A Paper 4 conclusion is a decision plus evidence.** State whether the hypothesis is true, false or partly true, then back it with figures from two sites.

Every topic is examined by a 7-mark part at least twice in the window.

**Nine outcomes are never the best match for any part.** Four are theme outcomes: the idea of over- and under-population, population policies, the impacts of migration, and the volcano case study. Exposure calibrates emphasis. It never removes coverage (RS-05).

## 6. How candidates lose marks on every question (the systemic failures)

Counted over the 117 report sections for Papers 1, 2 and 4: statements that state an error or give general advice and match each failure. **Spread** is the number of report sections that make the point (39 per paper); **series** is out of 17.

| paper | failure | statements | spread | series | what the resource does about it |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | ideas listed but not developed | 346 | 39 | 17 | answers at 4-7 marks model a point and its development |
| 1 | vague or generic terms (infrastructure, pollution, facilities) | 214 | 39 | 17 | every model answer names the specific thing |
| 1 | a case study that answers a different question (background, effects for causes) | 210 | 39 | 17 | case-study tasks ask one precise question; guidance names what earns nothing |
| 1 | a case study with no named example, no place detail, or at the wrong scale | 196 | 39 | 17 | every case study names a real place at the right scale, with place-specific detail |
| 1 | no comparison where one is asked | 118 | 38 | 17 | Compare cards use comparative language in every point |
| 1 | figures without words, or words without figures | 106 | 33 | 16 | data answers state the pattern and support it with figures |
| 1 | the command word not followed (explaining when asked to describe) | 89 | 39 | 17 | Describe and Explain cards are kept apart; MISCON cards drill the switch |
| 1 | the resource not used | 41 | 21 | 15 | resource cards mark only what the resource shows |
| 2 | map skills: grid references, bearings, distance, direction | 91 | 35 | 16 | mapwork items drill each from described map extracts |
| 2 | map terms not understood (relief, drainage, land use) | 76 | 29 | 14 | the glossary settles them; INTERP cards use them |
| 2 | graph and scale reading errors | 70 | 29 | 13 | graph items give the scale and check the reading |
| 2 | each value described instead of the overall trend | 60 | 28 | 14 | trend cards open with the overall pattern |
| 4 | a conclusion without supporting data | 403 | 39 | 17 | conclusions give the decision, then paired figures |
| 4 | graph completion errors: plotting, shading, the key, the order | 363 | 39 | 17 | presentation tasks follow the conventions of the graphs given |
| 4 | a method described too vaguely to repeat | 306 | 39 | 17 | method cards give equipment, siting, measurement and timing |
| 4 | improvements that only say "repeat it" | 165 | 38 | 17 | evaluations name what changes and why it is more reliable |
| 4 | sampling and survey terms confused | 83 | 30 | 15 | sampling cards separate random, systematic and stratified |

The counts come from pattern matches over the report sentences, so treat them as indicative. The spread is the robust figure. Most of these failures are reported in every one of the 17 series.

## 7. Examiner-evidenced misconceptions

**82 confusions, curated by hand** from the 4,559 error statements, restated in our words and attached to the objective they concern (`curriculum/misconceptions.json`). Each entry carries:

- the wrong idea;
- the correct idea;
- a test that tells them apart;
- the count of supporting statements (992 in all).

Every entry is supported by statements located to its own topic. Three examples:

- **Stating that a hypothesis is true without evidence from the data.** 31 statements in 13 series, on Paper 4.
- **Vague reasons for moving to a city** ("a better standard of living", "more facilities"). 25 statements in 12 series, on topic 1.7.
- **Tributaries confused with distributaries, or the river read as flowing uphill.** 16 statements in 10 series, on topic 2.2.

The most entries are on:

- the enquiry skills (9): sampling, pilot surveys, river measurements, recording sheets, graph conventions, conclusions without data, "repeat it";
- the map and graph skills (9): eastings before northings, bearing against direction, route distance, relief against drainage, trend against detail;
- earthquakes and volcanoes, coasts and population dynamics (5 each).

## 8. Scope

Content is exhaustive except where the syllabus writes "including" (page 12). `curriculum/syllabus-exclusions.json` records the closed lists that matter most:

- plate boundaries (2.1);
- river valley forms and landforms (2.2);
- coastal landforms (2.3);
- the two climates and two ecosystems (2.5).

`curriculum/scope-scan.json` carries 6 patterns, each tested against text it must catch and in-scope text it must leave alone. They cover:

- glaciation, which is not in the syllabus;
- coastal and river landforms off the lists (tombolos, salt marshes, braided channels, river terraces);
- hot spots;
- climates and ecosystems other than equatorial, hot desert and tropical rainforest;
- mid-latitude fronts and depressions.

For example, the pattern catches "a tundra biome" and allows "the tundra is sparsely populated because it is very cold". The second is a reason a case study can give for low population density.

## 9. What this means for the plan

- **Three kinds of topic.**
  - Topics 1.1-3.7 are the themes, examined on Paper 1, balanced against 48/30/22.
  - Topic 4 is the Paper 2 skills, balanced against 11/80/9.
  - Topic 5 is the Paper 4 enquiry, balanced against 20/60/20.
  - The subject total answers to 30/52/18.
- **Every theme topic has its case studies written out,** with a named place at the right scale and place-specific detail. Each has a 7-mark case-study task marked by levels. This is the part of the resource that states real-world facts, so each figure carries its year and is rounded.
- **Resource work without a renderer.** Maps, graphs and photographs are given in words or as tables:
  - a map extract as a grid of squares with their features and heights;
  - a graph as its data table;
  - a photograph as a described scene.
  The learner draws and plots on their own paper.
- **Answers are developed at 4 marks and above;** Describe and Explain are kept apart; every data answer gives words and figures together.
- **Paper 4 tasks follow one enquiry end to end:** hypothesis, method, results table, graph, conclusion with paired data, and evaluation.
- **Command words from section 4 only,** at the tariffs they carry. Response formats stay inside tasks.

## Derivation policy

Official assessment material is `mine_do_not_reproduce`. The analysis extracted structure, frequency and examiner-stated failure. No question, mark-scheme or examiner-report wording reaches learner-facing output. Every resource, enquiry and answer in the resource is original. Case studies use real places, described in our own words.
