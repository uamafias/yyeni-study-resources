# Corpus analysis — Cambridge International AS Level Physics 9702

What the syllabus and the AS assessment corpus say about this subject: what the skills are, and what a learner has to do to succeed on each paper. Step one of the pipeline, written after the syllabus (step zero) and before any plan. 29 September 2026. First exam for this cohort: 14 October 2026.

## 0. The syllabus, first

Transcribed from the 9702 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027 into `curriculum/syllabus-facts.json`, every value carrying its page. The AS route is Papers 1, 2 and 3, the AS content (topics 1 to 11) and the AS practical skills.

| AO | name (page 13) | AS weight | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Paper 3 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AO1 | Knowledge and understanding | 40% | 50% | 50% | 0% |
| AO2 | Handling, applying and evaluating information | 40% | 50% | 50% | 0% |
| AO3 | Experimental skills and investigations | 20% | 0% | 0% | 100% |

- **Paper 1, Multiple Choice.** 40 marks, 1 hour 15 minutes, 31%. Forty four-option items on the AS content.
- **Paper 2, AS Level Structured Questions.** 60 marks, 1 hour 15 minutes, 46%. Structured questions on the AS content.
- **Paper 3, Advanced Practical Skills.** 40 marks, 2 hours, 23%. Two laboratory questions of 20 marks each. The physics may lie outside the syllabus content, and no prior knowledge of its theory is needed (pages 40 and 42).
- **Paper 3 mark allocation (page 43).** Question 1 is an experiment with a graph: at least 7 marks for manipulation, measurement and observation, 6 for presentation, 4 for analysis, conclusions and evaluation, and 3 that vary. Question 2 is an experiment whose method is inaccurate on purpose: at least 5, 2 and 10 marks, and 3 that vary. The candidate evaluates the method and suggests improvements.
- **Command words, page 41:** 15 words, for AS and A Level together.
- **One arithmetic note.** Weighting each paper's AO split by its share of the AS gives 38.5 / 38.5 / 23, not the 40 / 40 / 20 the syllabus states. The stated figure is the target for the subject total; each paper's own split is the target for its topics.

## 1. The corpus

| source | held, 2020-2025 | used, 2022-2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paper 1 question papers and keys (11-13) | 44 | 30 |
| Paper 2 question papers and mark schemes (21-23) | 44 | 30 |
| Paper 3 question papers and mark schemes (31-35) | 70 | 48 |
| Principal Examiner Reports | 17 | 12, AS sections only (108 paper sections) |

**Only the 12 series examined on the current content are used.** The syllabus says its content has not changed since the syllabus for examination from 2022 (page 67). The 2020 and 2021 papers were set on the syllabus before it, and the corpus shows the break: electric field strength is examined in six Paper 2s from 2020-2021 and in none after, because electric fields are now A Level (topic 18). Those series were mined and set aside.

- **Paper 1: 1,099 multiple-choice questions** read from 30 papers (1,200 printed). 27 papers are complete; three lost questions to layout.
- **Paper 2: 978 question parts carrying 1,800 marks.** Tariffs are read from the mark schemes. All 30 papers sum to exactly 60.
- **Paper 3: 867 parts carrying 1,938 marks.** 43 of 48 papers sum to exactly 40; the rest lose the marks of one part the parser could not read.
- **The command word** is read from the matching question-paper part. It was found for 976 of 978 Paper 2 parts and 862 of 867 Paper 3 parts.
- **9,071 examiner-report sentences,** of which 7,611 are located to the question part they discuss. 2,731 of them state an error.

## 2. Three papers, two kinds of skill

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Paper 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| format | 40 one-mark items | structured parts | two laboratory questions |
| marks per part | 1 | 1-3 (99% of parts) | 1-4 mostly; 6-10 for a table of readings |
| largest share of marks | — | Calculate 32%, Determine 19%, State 19%, Show 7% | Describe 20%, Determine 12%, bench instructions (Measure, Repeat, Change, Set, Adjust) 34% |
| what earns marks | the right option | a stated equation, a substituted value, a final answer with its unit, or a precise definition | readings, a table, a graph and a conclusion that meet stated criteria |

**Papers 1 and 2 examine the same content, in syllabus order.** The average question number of a Paper 1 item rises steadily from topic 1 (question 7) to topic 11 (question 34). A learner meets every topic on both papers.

**Paper 2 is a calculation paper.** Calculate, Determine and Show carry 58% of its marks. Definitions and statements of laws make up most of the rest. They are asked as "Define" (43 parts), "State what is meant by" (18), and "State the principle of …" or "State … law" (29).

**Paper 3 is the same two experiments every series, in new clothes.** The equipment and the physics change. The skills credited do not: every one of the 48 mark schemes names the number of readings and their range, the column headings, consistent raw data, significant figures, the graph (scales, plotting, line), the gradient and intercept, and a percentage uncertainty. So these skills can be practised away from the apparatus. The manipulation marks (at least 12 of the 40) cannot: they are earned at the bench, and the plan says so.

- **Bench instructions** open most Paper 3 parts: Measure (96), Set (47), Repeat (42), Adjust (25), Change (20), Record (17), Place (15), Take (14), Vary (8), Remove (7). The candidate carries them out; no card is phrased with them. They appear only as steps inside practical tasks.
- **Listed but never used on an AS paper, 2022-2025:** Comment, Predict. **Rare:** Identify (3 parts). The table serves A Level too.
- **Justify is a Paper 3 word only,** and always one mark: justify the number of significant figures of a calculated value, or the choice of a reading.
- **Estimate is a Paper 3 word only:** estimate the percentage uncertainty in a reading.
- **Describe on Paper 3 is almost always four marks:** four limitations of the method, or four improvements.

## 4. What a creditable answer looks like

From the mark schemes' own award structure. Structure only; no scheme wording is reproduced.

| command, marks | parts | how it is marked |
|---|---:|---|
| Calculate 2 | 153 | in steps: the equation or the substitution earns a mark before the answer (97%); the answer carries its unit (63%) |
| Calculate 3, Determine 3 | 112 | in steps (95-100%); more than one route is credited in a third |
| Determine 2 | 194 | in steps (71%); alternative routes credited (34%) |
| Show 2 | 39 | in steps (97%); the working must arrive at the given value |
| State 1, Define 1 | 178 | one precise element |
| State 2, Explain 2 | 90 | one mark per separate point |
| Describe 4 (Paper 3) | 96 | one mark per limitation or improvement, from a longer list |

**Three things follow for every card.**

- **A calculation is marked in steps, so the steps are the answer.** The equation in symbols, then the substitution, then the answer with its unit. A learner who writes only a number gets the last mark or nothing. A learner who writes the equation and the substitution keeps those marks even if the arithmetic slips.
- **A definition is marked on its key words.** Per unit charge, the sum of, about the same point, original length, taken to act. The examiner reports show candidates losing the mark for one missing word in every series (section 6).
- **A "show that" answer must end beyond the given value.** It states the result to one more significant figure than the question, so the working visibly produces it.

**Equations need help to be matched.** An outcome such as "recall and use V = IR" has almost no words for a word-matcher to find, and a calculation part shares its vocabulary with the definition beside it. So calculation parts that went to a definition or a derivation were moved to its "recall and use" neighbour (84 parts). Each equation outcome also carries the names of the quantities it relates, for matching only. After that, three content outcomes are never the best match for any part: Newton's laws stated (3.1.5), the definition of resistance (9.3.1) and the principle of the potential divider (10.3.1). Most of their questions go to the outcome beside them: Newton's second law as F = ma or as rate of change of momentum (3.1.2 and 3.1.4, each in 8-9 series), resistance calculations to V = IR (9.3.2). Exposure calibrates emphasis; these three are still taught in full.

**Paper 3 is marked by criteria that each span several outcomes.** A table-of-readings part credits headings, consistency, significant figures and a calculated column at once. So a part-by-part match under-counts Paper 3 outcomes; 15 of its 40 are never a part's best match. The syllabus allocation (section 0) is the authority for Paper 3. Exposure calibrates emphasis. It never removes coverage (RS-05).

## 6. How candidates lose marks on every question (the systemic failures)

Counted over the 108 AS report sections, 2022-2025: statements that state an error or give general advice and match each failure. **Spread** is the number of report sections that make the point (30 each for Papers 1 and 2, 48 for Paper 3); **series** is out of 12.

| paper | failure | statements | spread | series | what the resource does about it |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | a vague explanation, or none where Explain is asked | 74 | 28 | 12 | MECH and WHY cards give the cause and its effect, in marked steps; State cards never carry an explanation |
| 2 | a sign or direction ignored for a vector | 46 | 29 | 12 | momentum, velocity and force cards fix a positive direction first; MISCON cards drill the rebound and the vertical throw |
| 2 | rounding too early, or too few significant figures | 69 | 26 | 12 | working is carried to three significant figures or more; only the final answer is rounded |
| 2 | the wrong equation recalled, or none stated | 65 | 25 | 12 | every CALC card starts from the equation in symbols |
| 2 | a definition missing its key word | 71 | 25 | 11 | DEF cards give the definition in full; their guidance names the words that carry the mark |
| 2 | an equation rearranged wrongly | 53 | 25 | 11 | worked calculations show the rearrangement as its own line |
| 2 | a power of ten lost converting a prefix or unit | 53 | 26 | 11 | every CALC card converts to base units in its first line |
| 2 | working not shown, or a Show answer that stops at the given value | 76 | 22 | 10 | equation, substitution and answer on separate lines; Show cards end beyond the given value |
| 2 | the question not read closely | 36 | 21 | 11 | scenario cards carry a stated condition the answer must use |
| 3 | limitations and improvements vague, generic or unmatched to each other | 147 | 48 | 12 | Question 2 tasks ask for four limitations and four improvements, each tied to the measurement it affects |
| 3 | raw readings at inconsistent precision; calculated values at the wrong significant figures | 259 | 47 | 11 | practical tasks give raw data at the instrument's resolution and ask for the significant figures to be justified |
| 3 | gradient and intercept from points too close, off the line, or across a false origin | 154 | 47 | 11 | every Question 1 task asks for the gradient triangle and the intercept, read from the line |
| 3 | graph scales, plotting and the line of best fit | 128 | 46 | 12 | Question 1 tasks have the learner choose scales and draw the line on their own grid |
| 3 | a missing or wrong unit in a table heading or an answer | 176 | 45 | 12 | every table heading is quantity / unit, including calculated columns |
| 3 | uncertainty estimated wrongly | 131 | 43 | 12 | Question 2 tasks estimate an absolute uncertainty from repeats and convert it to a percentage |
| 3 | a conclusion with no stated criterion | 84 | 43 | 10 | Question 2 tasks test a relationship against a criterion stated before the comparison |
| 1 | a prefix or unit slip that lands on a distractor | 57 | 28 | 12 | multiple-choice distractors are built from the misconception register first |

The counts come from pattern matches over the report sentences, so treat them as indicative. The spread is the robust figure. Every failure above is reported in at least ten of the twelve series. The Paper 3 failures are reported in nearly every Paper 3 section.

## 7. Examiner-evidenced misconceptions

**100 confusions, curated by hand** from the 2,731 error statements, restated in our words and attached to the objective they concern (`curriculum/misconceptions.json`). Each entry carries:

- the wrong idea;
- the correct idea;
- a test that tells them apart;
- the count of supporting statements (1,456 matches in all).

Every entry has at least one. 98 of the 100 are supported by statements located to their own topic; the other two by statements on the same paper. Three examples:

- **Kirchhoff's first law stated for a single current, or the two laws swapped.** 14 statements across 4 series, on topic 10. The law is about the total current into and out of a junction.
- **Nucleon number confused with neutron number.** 17 statements in 9 of the 12 series, on topic 11.
- **Limitations named without the measurement they affect** ("human error", "parallax"). 60 statements across all 12 series, on Paper 3.

By topic, the most entries are in:

- 12 practical skills (14): timing whole oscillations, table headings, consistent precision, scales, the gradient triangle, the false-origin intercept, half the range, limitations and improvements, the criterion for a conclusion;
- 3 dynamics (11): the resultant force, Newton's second and third laws, momentum as a vector, terminal velocity, elastic collisions;
- 1 quantities and units (9), 4 forces and pressure (9) and 11 particle physics (9).

## 8. Scope

The AS route is topics 1 to 11 and the AS practical skills. The syllabus states eight limits inside that content (`curriculum/syllabus-exclusions.json`):

- no coefficients of friction or viscosity, and a simple model of drag rising with speed is enough (3.2.1);
- no coefficient of restitution (3.3.2);
- forces and deformations in one dimension only (6.1.1);
- the Doppler effect for a moving source and a stationary observer only (7.3.1 and 7.3.2);
- no calculation for an unpolarised wave passing through a polarising filter (7.5.2);
- end corrections negligible and not taught (8.1.2);
- no spectrometer (8.4.2);
- thermistors are taken to have a negative temperature coefficient (9.3.8).

Each limit sits inside the outcome's wording, so the registry moves it to `syllabus_guidance` for authors and keeps the learner-facing wording free of it.

A Level content (topics 12-25) is out of scope. `curriculum/scope-scan.json` carries 21 patterns, one per limit and one per A Level topic (temperature and thermodynamics share one), each tested against text it must catch and in-scope text it must leave alone. For example:

- it catches "gravitational potential" and allows "gravitational potential energy";
- it catches "electric field strength" and allows "potential difference";
- it catches "X-ray imaging" and allows X-rays as a region of the spectrum.

## 9. What this means for the plan

- **Two kinds of topic.**
  - Topics 1-11 are content topics, examined on Papers 1 and 2. Their cards are credited as AO1 and AO2 only.
  - Topic 12 is the practical skills, examined on Paper 3. Its cards and tasks are credited as AO3 only.
- **Every content topic has a multiple-choice set.** Paper 1 is 31% of the AS. Its distractors come from the misconception register.
- **Calculation practice is the core of Paper 2 practice.**
  - Every "recall and use" outcome gets three calculation cards: equation, substitution, answer with unit, to a justified number of significant figures.
  - Working is carried to three significant figures, which also lets C-32 check every stated product and quotient.
- **Definitions are exact.** A DEF card's guidance names the words that carry the mark.
- **Paper 3 is practised as two task types.**
  - Question 1 is a table, a graph and constants from gradient and intercept.
  - Question 2 is uncertainty, a criterion and four limitations with four improvements.
  - The data are ours, and the plan says plainly that the manipulation marks need a laboratory.
- **Command words from section 3 only,** at the tariffs they carry. Bench instructions stay inside tasks. Describe is modelled at 2 marks on cards and at 4 in the Question 2 task.
