# Corpus analysis — Cambridge International AS Level Business 9609

What the syllabus and 95 AS assessment documents say about this subject, and what changed in the already-published resource because of it. 28 September 2026.

9609 was planned and published before the syllabus-first rule existed. This is the enrichment pass that brings it up to that standard: the syllabus read first, the corpus reconciled against it, and the published material audited against both.

*Assess*, *Advise* and *Justify* are in the table because the table serves Papers 3 and 4; none opens an AS question in the current regime.

**The corpus has a regime break at 2023.** The mark schemes changed format that year and the papers changed with them:

- **Discuss**: 46 questions 2020-2022, none from 2023. Not in the current syllabus table.
- **Recommend**: 19 questions 2020-2022, none from 2023. Not in the current syllabus table.
- **Evaluate at 11 marks**: 16 questions 2020-2022; from 2023 the final part is 12 marks.

So everything about the current answer architecture is taken from 2023 onward only. The older series are used to record what the papers have stopped asking, never to shape a card.

## 2. The answer architecture, read off the mark schemes’ own AO grids

From 2023 every 9609 mark scheme attributes each mark to an assessment objective, in a grid. So the shape of a credit-worthy answer is not inferred — it is printed. Every split below is unanimous across the questions that carry a grid.

| command | marks | AO1 | AO2 | AO3 | AO4 | what earns it |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Identify | 1 | 1 | — | — | — | one correct item, 1 mark |
| Define | 2 | 2 | — | — | — | a definition showing clear understanding = 2; partial understanding = 1 |
| Explain | 3 | 1 | 2 | — | — | one point, identified: AO1 1 mark; that point applied to a business context: AO2 2 marks for developed application, 1 for limited |
| Calculate | 3 | 1 | 2 | — | — | formula: 1 mark; formula with the figures substituted: 1 mark; correct answer: 1 mark |
| Analyse | 5 | 1 | 2 | 2 | — | one point: AO1 1; applied to the context: AO2 2; analysed to its consequence: AO3 2 |
| Analyse | 8 | 2 | 2 | 4 | — | two points: AO1 2; each applied: AO2 2; each developed into connected causes, impacts or consequences: AO3 4 |
| Evaluate | 12 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 6 | knowledge: AO1 2; application: AO2 2; analysis: AO3 2; evaluation: AO4 6 - a developed judgement in context |

Two things fall out of this that matter for every card. **An "Explain" is one point taken into context** — AO1 gives one mark for the point, AO2 two for its application; a second point earns nothing. **Half of every 12-mark question is evaluation** — six of the twelve marks are AO4, for a developed judgement in context.

Paper structure, recovered from the arithmetic and matching the syllabus: Section A: Q1-Q3 each Define 2 + Explain 3; Q4 Analyse 5. Section B: two essays of Analyse 8 + Evaluate 12, one answered. Two compulsory case questions, each: Identify 1, Explain 3, Calculate 3, Explain 3, Analyse 8, Evaluate 12 = 30.

## 3. Examiner-evidenced misconceptions, checked against the current syllabus

48 AS sections were read from 11 Principal Examiner Reports (the Paper 3 and 4 sections removed first), and every one of 460 error statements was read by hand — the automatic pair matcher was too noisy on 11 reports.

Then each confusion was **located in the current syllabus** before it was kept. That mattered: 8 confusions the older reports complained about concern content the syllabus now teaches only at A Level, and would have introduced out-of-scope content if turned into cards:

- acid test ratio ↔ current ratio — liquidity ratios are A Level, topic 10.2
- economies of scale as falling unit cost from larger scale ↔ a list of causes such as bulk buying — economies of scale are A Level, topic 9.1
- technical economies of scale ↔ technological change — economies of scale are A Level, topic 9.1
- profit ↔ profit margin — profit margin is A Level, topics 10.1-10.2
- gross profit ↔ gross profit margin — gross profit and its margin are A Level, topics 10.1-10.2
- the 4Cs ↔ the 4Ps — the 4Cs are not in the syllabus; the marketing mix is taught as the 4Ps in 3.3
- CAD ↔ CAM — CAD and CAM are not in the syllabus
- emotional intelligence ↔ leadership qualities — emotional intelligence is A Level, topic 7.3

**44 kept**, each attached to the AS topic the syllabus places it in; 11 recur across two or more series. They include: private limited company ↔ public limited company; shareholders ↔ stakeholders; dismissal ↔ redundancy; person specification ↔ job description; democratic leadership ↔ autocratic leadership; laissez-faire leadership ↔ democratic leadership; price skimming ↔ penetration pricing; product portfolio analysis ↔ product life cycle; product differentiation ↔ product portfolio analysis; working capital ↔ start-up capital or machinery; break-even as a number of units of output ↔ break-even as a period of time.

Five failures are **systemic** — not one topic’s confusion but a way of misreading any question:

- **answered for the wrong party** (5 series). The question named one party - the business, or its employees, or the franchisee - and the answer was written for another. *Fix:* Every prompt that names a party carries that party into its canonical answer by name, and its marking guidance states that an answer for a different party scores nothing.
- **several points where one was asked for** (5 series). An "Explain one..." or "Analyse one..." question answered with two or three shallow points, none developed. *Fix:* Cards that model a one-point question give ONE point taken all the way into context, which is the shape the AO grid rewards: AO1 1 for the point, AO2 2 for its application.
- **the opposite side of the question** (2 series). Advantages given where disadvantages or limitations were asked for, or the reverse. *Fix:* Cards on benefits and limitations of the same concept are paired, each prompt says which side it wants, and each answer opens on that side.
- **the wrong business context** (3 series). A retail business answered as a manufacturer, a car-hire firm as a car dealer. *Fix:* Application items state the kind of business in the stem and the answer turns on what that kind of business does.
- **summary offered as evaluation** (3 series). A closing paragraph that repeats the analysis, where a judgement was required. *Fix:* EVAL cards end on a judgement that could have gone the other way, with the criterion that decided it. Repeating the argument is not a conclusion.

## 4. Scope

9609’s AS content states no explicit "not required" limits. Its boundary is structural: everything in topics 6 onward is A Level. `curriculum/syllabus-exclusions.json` lists all 15 A Level topics and 52 sub-topics, transcribed, plus the specific constructs confirmed A Level only by search.

The published material was searched for each. It is **essentially clean**: passing mentions of "return on capital" and "profit margin" in explanatory sentences, and one boundary case — card `ITEM-9609-3.3-074-DIST` asks learners to distinguish a mark-up from a profit margin. Mark-up belongs to pricing in 3.3; profit margin as a measure is A Level (10.1–10.2).

## 5. Command words in the published cards

The published cards were checked against the AS command-word verdict:

- **1,343 (87%)** instruct with a live AS command word
- **98 (6%)** are direct questions with no command word — study cards, which is fine
- **107 (7%)** instruct with a word the current AS papers never use: Distinguish 67, State 35, Recommend 2, Advise 1, Describe 1, Suggest 1

Those 107 model question forms a learner will not be set. They predate the verdict. `curriculum/command-word-repair-work-order.json` gives each one its target command word, tariff, answer shape and AO split, read off the grids above. It is ready for an agent.

The published cards do not record a mark tariff, so whether each card’s declared AOs match the grid for its tariff cannot be measured on the published set. The 0450 and 0455 handovers require `mark_tariff` on every item for exactly this reason.

## 7. Decisions taken (29 September 2026)

1. **Command-word repair: done.** All 104 real cases on the list were rewritten to one of the six AS command words; the other 3 were false positives and are recorded as unchanged. A second sweep found 118 more prompts instructing with words the papers do not use (Set out, Interpret, Apply, Name, List, Work out, Classify, appended "and state / give / say", and case tasks ending "and recommend"), and those were reworded too. 222 cards in all. Misconception cards ("Correct this statement") and essay plans ("Plan an answer to") are a flashcard form, not a model of a paper question, and were left as written. Every rewrite is logged in `curriculum/command-word-repair-log.json` and the topic's `authoring_notes.json`, with the old text's hash kept as `prior_hash`.
2. **The mark-up / profit-margin card: reframed** to stay on mark-up (`ITEM-9609-3.3-074-DIST`, now resting on `CLM-9609-3.3-069`).
3. **`mark_tariff`: recorded where a card matches one paper part** (84 cards: Define 2, Explain 3, Evaluate 12 and similar). A card that asks for more than one paper part carries no tariff rather than an invented one. `command_word` is now on 1,493 of 1,548 published items; the rest are misconception cards and multi-part data-response tasks.
