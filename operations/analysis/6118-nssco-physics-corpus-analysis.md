## 7. Misconceptions

The curated register contains 23 distinct, report-evidenced contrasts. Each has source-bullet anchors, a located report block and an original diagnostic task outline. These outlines are not official stems and are not finished cards. One supporting block is selected per entry; this does not count all recurrence.

- **MIS-01**: A horizontal speed-time segment means constant speed; it does not mean the object is at rest as a horizontal distance-time segment does. Diagnostic: Show one horizontal speed graph and one horizontal distance graph and ask which object changes position.
- **MIS-02**: Distance comes from speed-time area; acceleration comes from gradient. Diagnostic: Give one speed-time plot and ask for a distance and an acceleration with units that distinguish the operations.
- **MIS-03**: Average speed is total distance divided by total elapsed time, not an unweighted average of speeds. Diagnostic: Compare unequal-duration journey legs where the arithmetic mean of the speeds gives a different answer.
- **MIS-04**: Constant velocity requires zero resultant force; a nonzero driving force can be balanced by resistance. Diagnostic: Specify a steady pulled object and ask separately for the pulling force and the net force.
- **MIS-05**: Moment uses perpendicular distance from pivot to force line; work uses displacement along the force. Diagnostic: Use a pivoted lever and a translating block and require the relevant distance in each calculation.
- **MIS-06**: Mass is retained when gravitational field strength changes; weight is not. Diagnostic: Move an object between two specified gravitational fields and compare both quantities.
- **MIS-07**: Momentum conservation is conditional on no external resultant force, not an unconditional property of every moving system. Diagnostic: Contrast an isolated collision with a system receiving an external impulse and require the governing condition.
- **MIS-08**: Opposing velocities carry opposite signs when adding momenta. Diagnostic: Compare head-on and same-direction collisions with the same speed magnitudes and ask for signed initial momentum.
- **MIS-09**: Specific latent heat concerns phase change per unit mass; specific heat capacity concerns temperature rise per unit mass. Diagnostic: Contrast heating within one phase with melting at constant temperature and select the physical quantity for each.
- **MIS-10**: Energy supplied during a constant-temperature phase change changes intermolecular potential energy rather than increasing average kinetic energy. Diagnostic: Compare one sloping and one flat heating-curve section and ask which energy store changes.
- **MIS-11**: Conduction at a heater boundary and convection through a fluid are different transfer processes. Diagnostic: Ask separately how energy crosses a metal-water boundary and then spreads through the bulk water.
- **MIS-12**: Reflection returns light at a boundary; refraction changes its propagation through media. Diagnostic: Use two original ray paths and require the process at each boundary rather than the name of the apparatus.
- **MIS-13**: A real image is formed where rays actually converge; a virtual image is located by their backward extensions. Diagnostic: Ask which of two ray constructions can form an image on a screen and explain why.
- **MIS-14**: Incidence and refraction angles are measured to the normal, not to the surface. Diagnostic: Provide an angle to the surface and require the angle used in the sine ratio.
- **MIS-15**: Frequency and period are reciprocals, not interchangeable numerical inputs to the wave-speed equation. Diagnostic: Give period and wavelength, require speed, and ask why multiplying the supplied values directly is wrong.
- **MIS-16**: Sound loudness follows amplitude rather than wavelength. Diagnostic: Compare two sound traces with equal period and different amplitudes and ask which is quieter.
- **MIS-17**: Positive charging is explained by electron loss; protons are not transferred in the rubbing model. Diagnostic: Track electron transfer between initially neutral insulating objects and infer both final signs.
- **MIS-18**: Resistances in parallel cannot simply be added as series resistances. Diagnostic: Present an original mixed circuit and require the student to identify the parallel group before calculating.
- **MIS-19**: Work per charge is potential difference; work per time is power. Diagnostic: Give one energy transfer, one charge and one time and ask for the two physically different ratios and units.
- **MIS-22**: Nucleon number counts protons plus neutrons; it is not the neutron count alone. Diagnostic: Give an original nuclide and ask separately for nucleons, protons and neutrons.
- **MIS-23**: Isotopes share proton number and differ in neutron/nucleon number; those roles cannot be exchanged. Diagnostic: Present pairs with different proton and neutron counts and ask which pair can be isotopes of one element.
- **MIS-26**: Attraction alone does not prove that a metal object is a permanent magnet; repulsion of one pole does. Diagnostic: Compare attraction-only and attraction-plus-repulsion observations and ask which identifies a permanent magnet.
- **MIS-28**: Absolute zero is zero kelvin, not zero degrees Celsius. Diagnostic: Ask the student to compare freezing-point and absolute-zero temperatures on both scales.

Do not add a misconception merely because an author predicts it might happen. Some diagnostic contrasts elaborate the correct mechanism beyond the exact report phrase; the source citation establishes the reported difficulty, not a prevalence claim about every possible wrong belief.

## 8. Scope

Three explicit limits have bounded, tested patterns in `curriculum/scope-scan.json`, with must-catch and must-allow examples. All 35 other depth/condition statements are retained for contextual review.

1. Collision **definitions** are not required; one-dimensional two-body momentum applications remain in scope (syllabus PDF p.14).
2. The **no nuclide formulae** condition belongs to **energy sources**, not all Nuclear Physics (PDF p.17). Alpha/beta decay equations remain required and are observed in all six years.
3. Diverging lenses are limited to **eye-defect correction** (PDF p.28); this does not remove converging-lens ray constructions.

Regex screens are deliberately narrow and cannot certify contextual scope by themselves. The planner must review neighbouring content and the depth conditions, particularly gas laws, collision conditions and lens applications. Annex D’s diode symbol and observed rectification context do not authorise semiconductor-junction theory.
